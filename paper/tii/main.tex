\title{AgentWorkShop: Node-Native Integration of Multi-Agent Teams
for Industrial Supervisory Control}

% Working manuscript: replace the anonymous author block only when the

\author{Anonymous Authors}
\markboth{Working Manuscript --- IEEE Transactions on Industrial Informatics Format}{AgentWorkShop: Multi-Agent Industrial Supervisory Control}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Bringing language-model agents into industrial supervisory control requires
associating the semantics, authority, and history of each command across
heterogeneous devices, multiple agents, and changing operating recipes. We
present \system{}, a node-native framework in which explicit member-to-node
bindings determine each agent's tools, source-dependent checks govern
agent-issued writes, and attributable records with an independent backstop
keep authorization, device execution, and process judgment distinguishable.
The implementation covers five protocol drivers and registers fourteen agent
engines as an inventory rather than evaluated controllers. Evidence comes from
three simulated production scenarios over real software protocol transports,
including a 49-signal biaxial-film line. The latest integrated run passes 75 of
75 checks with no warnings or failures. Its four writable protocol fixtures
reject 24 of 24 predefined out-of-constraint probes and block none of four
designated legal writes; a second scenario rejects 9 of 9 hard-range probes
without false blocks. A fixed non-LLM controller over three seeds reaches
0.968--0.972 of an offline grid-search reference. A deployed AgentTeam mission
then reduces biaxial-film thickness from 28.10 to 25.68\,$\mu$m, inside a
$25.0\pm0.7\,\mu$m target, using three governed writes on three actuator
families. These are suite-coverage and single-run capability results on
simulated plants; they do not establish physical safety or LLM superiority.
\end{abstract}
