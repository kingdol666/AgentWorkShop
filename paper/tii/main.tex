\title{AgentWorkShop: Governed Agent-Team Execution for Industrial Recipe Optimization}

% Working manuscript: replace the anonymous author block only when the

\author{Anonymous Authors}
\markboth{IEEE Transactions on Industrial Informatics, Submitted Manuscript}{AgentWorkShop: Governed Agent-Team Execution}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Industrial recipe optimization couples process objectives with equipment limits,
heterogeneous protocols, and the authority to change operating conditions.
Connecting an agent to a device does not, by itself, preserve these constraints
or distinguish a successful command from a successful intervention. This paper
presents AgentWorkShop, a supervisory execution framework that connects deployed
agent-team members to engineering-level acquisition and control nodes through
explicit capabilities and production context. A governed write path combines
applicable operating limits and approval requirements, while intervention
records link accepted writes to observations, submitted judgments, and separate
compensation actions. Evaluation uses real protocol transports connected to
simulated plants, with deterministic controllers and scripted teams to isolate
execution behavior from language-model reasoning. The integrated archive passes
75 implementation checks, rejects all 24 configured out-of-constraint probes
across four writable stacks, and accepts all four legal-write probes. A
four-arm ablation attributes rejection of the tested recipe-window violations
to the window check rather than readback verification. A recorded multi-node team
mission reduces film thickness from 26.90 to 25.48\,$\mu$m with four governed
writes on three parameters, reaching a $25.0\pm0.7\,\mu$m objective. These results
show how a common execution layer can preserve the distinction between action
authority, device acceptance, and process outcome. The evidence supports
simulation-tier supervisory integration; physical reliability and comparative
agent optimization remain to be evaluated.
\end{abstract}
