\begin{abstract}
Bringing language-model agents into industrial supervisory control requires
associating the semantics, authority, and history of each command across
heterogeneous devices and multiple agents. We
present \system{}, a node-native framework in which explicit member-to-node
bindings determine each agent's tools, source-dependent checks govern
agent-issued writes, and attributable records with an independent backstop
keep authorization, device execution, and process judgment distinguishable.
A scripted channel layer and a fixed controller drive the governed write path;
the LLM decision path stays disabled. Results come
from one archive over three simulated scenarios with real
protocol transports, including a 49-signal biaxial-film line. The run passes
75 of 75 checks; the paper also accounts what the gate does not see:
task-level failures surface as warnings, three checks carry skipped write
sub-checks, and the suite is not deterministic at the mission layer. The
writable stacks reject 24/24 predefined gross out-of-constraint probes and none
of four legal writes; a second scenario rejects 9/9 without false
blocks. A fixed non-LLM controller reaches 0.963--0.972 of an offline
grid-search reference. A scripted
team mission brings biaxial-film thickness from 28.10 to 25.68\,$\mu$m
(objective $25.0\pm0.7\,\mu$m) in three governed writes on two protocols;
seven of the ten reportable runs (of eleven same-seed archives) end in the
band (small-$n$); none of the collapses trips the hard gate. These
results characterize the governance path on simulated plants;
physical validation and controlled agent comparisons remain open.
\end{abstract}
