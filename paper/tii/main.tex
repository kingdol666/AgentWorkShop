\begin{abstract}
Bringing language-model agents into industrial supervisory control requires
associating the semantics, authority, and history of each command across
heterogeneous devices, multiple agents, and changing operating recipes. We
present \system{}, a node-native framework in which explicit member-to-node
bindings determine each agent's tools, source-dependent checks govern
agent-issued writes, and attributable records with an independent backstop
keep authorization, device execution, and process judgment distinguishable;
this paper validates the governed write infrastructure end to end with
deterministic scripted proxies and a fixed controller, keeping the LLM
decision path disabled.
The implementation covers five protocol drivers and registers fourteen agent
engines as an inventory rather than evaluated controllers. Evidence comes from
one archived run over three simulated production scenarios and real software
protocol transports, including a 49-signal biaxial-film line. The run passes 75 of 75 checks with no warnings or failures. Its four writable stacks
reject 24 of 24 predefined out-of-constraint probes and block none of four
designated legal writes; a second scenario rejects 9 of 9 out-of-constraint probes without false blocks. A fixed non-LLM controller over three seeds reaches
0.963--0.972 of an offline grid-search reference under the archive's two
scoring conventions. A scripted team mission reduces biaxial-film thickness from 28.10 to 25.68\,$\mu$m, inside a
$25.0\pm0.7\,\mu$m objective, using three governed writes on two protocols;
seven of ten archived same-command runs reach the band (small-$n$; the
collapsed reruns are not flagged by the hard gate), and the remainder end near
zero thickness with only the mission checks downgraded to warnings. These results characterize the implemented governance path end to end on
simulated plants; physical validation and controlled agent comparisons remain
future work.
\end{abstract}
