\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The four-scene matrix shows that the same write controller can enforce configured limits and reach the intended setpoints through different protocol drivers. The approval fixture shows where a pending or declined request stops, and the injection twin records the sequence from approval of a test plan to a subsequent governed write, matching readback, and process measurement. The production-mode fixtures exercise step, timing, and hold rules that the benchmark runs bypassed. This coverage reflects the different entry points and settings used in each experiment.

In the three scripted scenes, the caller read observations, submitted bounded recipe trials, and checked the process again. The nine valid pairs in the reported batch show what those policies achieved relative to no-write. Scene-specific policy knowledge, the absence of an active controller baseline, and the unresolved batch-selection rule limit the comparison. The archived script hashes identify candidate runner sources. Reproducing the deployed service and simulator would require an immutable runtime snapshot. The language-model episodes exercised the recipe tool path and ended below the mass target. A correlated record from the agent's observations through its decisions, driver outcomes, and process response is still needed to evaluate autonomous completion.

In deployment, the application checks operate alongside PLC and equipment interlocks. A future study can compare pre-specified agent and conventional-controller runs on common horizons with verified resets and correlated records. Evaluating operator approval and physical process response requires separate trials.

Two extensions follow from the task design. A lead agent could assign governed exploration steps to workers and use returned measurements to decide whether to continue, revise the plan, or stop. A scene-specific record of operating context, applied changes, and subsequent observations could help agents select later proposals. Both extensions use the same authorization, approval, and write checks.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented a configuration-driven runtime that connects agent-facing acquisition and setpoint tools to scene-specific mappings, conditional approval, and write verification. Across four simulated scenes, the node-write service rejected the tested hard-range and recipe-window violations, and all 216 follow-up reads matched their requested setpoints. The injection twin demonstrated a governed pressure change with a matching readback and later acquisition. The scripted policy reduced accumulated error relative to no-write in nine valid pairs in the reported batch. The three language-model runs exercised two-parameter recipe trials and stopped below target. These experiments establish governed setpoint execution in simulation and provide the starting point for evaluating autonomous operation.
