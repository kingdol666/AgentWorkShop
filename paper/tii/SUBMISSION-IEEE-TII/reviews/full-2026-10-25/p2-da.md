## Review Body

AgentWorkShop’s clearest strength is its explicit separation of command confirmation from process observation and its careful scope limits. The strongest opposing case is that the experiments demonstrate a route composition, not a broadly reusable framework. In the current-version study, three OMP tasks ran sequentially per scene on one simulator instance; the Pi path was a deterministic fixture with no external model decision; direct REST was two calls; and each OMP task yielded one sample. Synthetic approval, prevalidated recipe candidates, test-runner-owned probes, and SQLite fallback lookup could produce the reported joins without establishing that adapters or scene configuration transfer across independent deployments, or that the shared abstraction reduces integration effort compared with direct route-specific handlers. The mock-state ablation shows the tuple check prevents a defined class of stale-context writes in a controlled test; it does not demonstrate atomicity across network I/O, physical safety, or operator effectiveness. The four seed-42 missions do not broaden current-version evidence: they use a different version and procedure and are explicitly not a before/after comparison. Prior work covers skill servers, OPC UA operations, context checks, approvals, and industrial evidence paths, while this paper does not compare its composition against an alternative implementation. TII is in scope: its official remit includes industrial AI and interoperability ([IEEE IES TII scope](https://www.ieee-ies.org/pubs?highlight=WyJpIiwiaSdkIiwiaW4iXQ%3D%3D)). The strongest challenge is therefore contribution significance, not an inferential contradiction: the paper limits its claim to implementing and testing selected integration paths, and does not claim broad portability or process efficacy. I found no CRITICAL or MAJOR defect in D3.
