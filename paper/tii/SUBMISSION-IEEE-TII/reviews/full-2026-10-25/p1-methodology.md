## Contract Paraphrase

D1 — Methodology rigor: The research approach should suit the questions, handle evidence transparently, report analyses clearly, and give other researchers enough information to assess or reproduce the work.

D2 — Domain accuracy: Domain-specific statements should be factually sound, consistent with relevant evidence, and accurate in how they describe prior work and technical terms.

D3 — Argumentative coherence: The central position should hold together logically, with evidence supporting its claims and no reasoning errors that undermine the argument.

D4 — Cross-disciplinary relevance: Readers from neighboring fields should be able to follow the framing and key terms, and any claims about broader relevance should have support.

D5 — Writing and structure: The manuscript should present its ideas clearly and in a coherent order, with effective figures and tables and appropriate adherence to venue conventions.

D6 — Venue fit and contribution: The work should suit the configured venue and offer an original contribution with significance for its intended readers.

## Scoring Plan

### D1: methodology_rigor
dimension_id: D1
what_to_look_for: Assess whether the design, data scope and handling, sampling, metrics, provenance, version comparability, analysis, inference limits, and reproducibility detail support the research questions.
what_triggers_block: A fundamental design or analysis flaw prevents the methods from supporting the central empirical conclusions.
what_triggers_warn: Material gaps in sampling, provenance, reporting, or reproducibility reduce confidence, while the central method remains assessable.
what_triggers_fatal: Evidence establishes a serious methodological integrity breach, such as fabricated or knowingly misrepresented data.

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Trace whether the central claims follow from the presented evidence and whether the reasoning remains internally consistent and respects the evidence’s inferential limits.
what_triggers_block: The central argument relies on an inferential leap or contradiction that makes its principal conclusion untenable.
what_triggers_warn: A localized reasoning gap or insufficiently qualified inference weakens part of the argument without overturning its core.
what_triggers_fatal: The paper’s own evidence directly refutes a necessary central premise, with no possible reconciliation offered.
