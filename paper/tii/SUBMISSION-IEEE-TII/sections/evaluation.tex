\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}
The experiments examine four aspects of AgentWorkShop: route-specific record
continuity across two configured simulator scenes, completion of one documented
blueprint-onboarding workflow, rejection of stale approval context before
software dispatch, and the process samples associated with the tested routes.
These outcomes are kept separate. All plant-facing checks used software
simulators; none involved a physical plant or independent process replication.

\subsection{Test Design and Protocol}
\label{sec:eval-protocol}
For a registered task, the integration criterion is a task linked to its
approval, readback-verified command receipt, and a process sample tagged to the
active run. A direct operation call has no task edge; its criterion is a
verified receipt/readback and an active-run-tagged sample. The fixed-call
harness replay checks record links, not task completion. The context guard is
assessed by whether a changed line, product, recipe, or run is refused before a
driver write.

The route-continuity study used the v0.8.0 application with cast-film and
continuous-annealing simulator scenes. Cast film wrote screw speed through
OPC UA and observed thickness through MQTT (target: 48--52\,$\mu$m).
Annealing wrote the zone-3 setpoint through Modbus TCP and observed strip
temperature (target: 700--760\,$^{\circ}$C). The registered-harness path ran
three sequential tasks per scene on one persistent simulator; each task used
its own production and task context, while process state carried forward.
After each verified receipt, the runner waited 20\,s, probed and ingested one
process sample, queried it by active-run ID, and then checked task state. Thus,
task-completion-to-sample latency is unavailable. The fixed-call replay,
direct REST call, and fixture-backed command-line task ran once per scene in
separate isolated environments.

All route-study records report unavailable MQTT and Timescale endpoints; their
samples used the local SQLite fallback, with online DAQ collection disabled.
The route results therefore assess invocation and application-record handling,
not the normal online DAQ path. Fixture-backed routes used deterministic input;
direct REST created no task and did not invoke an external model.

A separate v0.8.3 onboarding replay used a fresh isolated application home and
the documented C01 blueprint. The blueprint configured one Modbus/TCP device
with four setpoints and seven DAQ nodes, plus MES REST scalar, 48-point vector,
table, and image forms. Dedicated local MQTT and Timescale services were online
for DAQ checks; object storage was unavailable, so image artifacts used the
local-disk fallback. This was one configuration replay, not a route-study
trial.

The approval-context suite changed each field of the approved
line--product--recipe--run tuple for recipe apply, trial, and rollback with
dispatch enabled (12 cases). A separate fault-injection case
changed context after asynchronous grant revalidation but before the caller's
final synchronous check. A test-only ablation removed only the tuple comparison
while retaining the other checks. These outcomes were recorded in mock-driver
state and are not process-level reliability estimates.

\subsection{Registered and Direct-Call Route Results}
\label{sec:testbed}
All six model-mediated registered tasks completed and passed the task,
approval, receipt, and sample-link checks (76 runner assertions). Cast-film
settings of 125/130/125\,rpm produced single 20-s samples of
45.69/46.69/45.69\,$\mu$m. Annealing settings of 745\,$^{\circ}$C produced
679.60/677.70/678.10\,$^{\circ}$C. All six samples were below their target
bands; they do not describe a response trajectory, steady state, or the
quality of the model-selected settings.

The fixed-call harness replay linked both tasks to approval, receipt, and
active-run samples (31 assertions); task completion was not asserted. Its
cast-film and annealing samples were 46.37\,$\mu$m and 674.80\,$^{\circ}$C,
both below target. Direct REST returned verified receipts/readbacks and
run-tagged samples in both scenes (19 checks); its samples were 46.37\,$\mu$m
and 675.20\,$^{\circ}$C, also below target. The fixture-backed command-line adapter completed both
tasks and linked their samples (37 application and 28 isolation checks); its
samples were 46.38\,$\mu$m and 674.40\,$^{\circ}$C, also below target. The
tested routes reached the shared operation services under two preconfigured
signal and protocol mappings. They do not establish process attainment,
configuration reuse, general adapter interchangeability, or portability to an
unseen scene.

\subsection{Blueprint-Driven Scenario Onboarding}
\label{sec:coating-onboarding}
On the v0.8.3 build, the C01 connectivity preflight passed 3/3 checks; line
provisioning passed 20/20; channel construction passed 13/13; and all four MES
forms returned records with the expected format and field dictionary. The
V1--V6 verifier passed 23/23 checks, including sample availability for all
seven DAQ nodes, range rejection, legal write/readback, recipe mapping,
channel/plugin checks, and zero writes to a protected demonstration line.
One smoke proposal used the test-only automatic-approval policy to change line
speed from 37 to 39\,m/min within the 28--48\,m/min recipe window; the PLC twin
confirmed the write. The approval was synthetic. These checks establish one
documented blueprint-to-simulator onboarding instance, not human-review
performance, film quality, process attainment, arbitrary protocol coverage,
configuration reuse, repeatability, or physical transfer. Supplementary
File~S1 includes the inputs, health and DAQ status, runner output, and complete
V1--V6 log.

\subsection{Approval-Context Guard and Ablation}
\label{sec:guard-results}
The production handler blocked all 12 one-field mutations before a mock write
or execution record. The queued context change was also refused before
\texttt{recipe\_apply} dispatch. In the ablation, removing the tuple comparison
allowed six product/run changes through; each produced two mock writes and an
execution record, while the other checks still blocked line/recipe changes.
The local unit suite passed 566/566 checks across seven suites.

The ablation isolates the tuple comparison within the tested software state.
It does not demonstrate atomicity during network or device I/O, safe physical
operation, operator effectiveness, or end-to-end DAQ integrity.

In injection molding, hold pressure and time increased from 45 to 58.2\,bar

\subsection{Evidence Synthesis}
The route study supports record continuity for the tested task and direct-call
contracts across two preconfigured mappings; all twelve route-linked samples
were below target. The separate onboarding replay passed the documented C01
configuration checks with isolated online MQTT/Timescale and one simulator-
confirmed write. The mock-state comparison shows that the tested context check
blocked stale requests before software dispatch. Fig.~\ref{fig:missions} is
historical evidence and is not pooled with these results.

Together, these results distinguish command completion from process outcome:
a verified readback confirms the setpoint path, whereas the later run-tagged
sample indicates whether the process variable entered its target band. The
study establishes selected integration behavior, one simulated onboarding
instance, and a bounded software guard; it does not establish process
improvement, general portability, or physical-plant performance. Supplementary
File~S1 includes the route inputs and logs, the separate onboarding replay,
historical scenario records, and the evidence/hash manifests. The model-mediated
tasks are not deterministic replays, and the route samples are not independent
replicates.
