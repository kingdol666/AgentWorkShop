\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

The evaluation asks whether one governed supervisory path can be exercised,
inspected, and repeated across heterogeneous industrial jobs rather than whether
one optimizer dominates another. Four questions organize the evidence: protocol
closure and governance attribution (Q1); the AgentTeam optimization workflow on
one node and on a multi-node line (Q2); the deterministic cast-film loop (Q3);
and objective attainment for four process missions (Q4). The path they
instantiate is the one shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:walkthrough}.

\subsection{Testbed, Evidence Snapshot, and Scope}
\label{sec:testbed}
The snapshot is integrated run \resRun{} (seed \resSeed, commit \resCommit, harness \resHarness, completed \resEvidenceDate); raw reports and mission logs are archived under that run identifier, and the ablation companion is \resAblationRun. The simulators behind the five stacks are deterministic per seed but not validated digital twins. Deterministic scripted policies, not language models, call the governed tools under a deployed member identity; two live agent-engine turns serve as a slot-occupancy check only, and the four-scenario campaign runs one trajectory per scenario at the fixed seed-42 initial states. Physical-plant transfer, safety certification, and optimizer ranking remain out of scope.

\subsection{Test Procedure and System-Level Profile}
\label{sec:procedure}
The procedure is one \resPhases-phase integrated suite scoring
\resChecksPass{}/\resChecksTotal{} checks with no warnings or failures, an
author-defined regression summary. Each phase probes one claim: preset loading against an offline optimum $J^{*}=\resJstar$; provisioning, DAQ, governed writes, and out-of-window interception on five stacks; the observe--write--judge cycle; the AgentTeam missions; records, rollback, approval, audit, and alarms; recipe lifecycle and the parameter layer; the controller benchmark; the second preset; the backstop drill; and the four-scenario campaign.

Fig.~\ref{fig:systemexec} reports two system-level metrics. Panel~(a) is each phase's wall time on a log scale---the end-to-end clock of all twenty phases, from preset provisioning through the four-scenario campaign---and it answers where the run's \resTotalWall{} actually goes. Three tiers appear. Deterministic governance steps---admission, binding checks, record writes, recipe lifecycle---complete in milliseconds to seconds: the platform's own cost per intervention is validation logic, not optimization work. The long tail belongs to the replaceable strategy slot and to stored observation cadences: the two live agent-engine turns take \resLlmWall{} and \resGoalWall, and the backstop drill spans \resBackstop{} at the stored evaluation cadence. This is the intended cost shape: the framework stays thin, and the expensive tiers are the ones a deployment would swap or re-time. Panel~(b) is the governed closed-loop objective $J$ after two writes, normalized by the simulator's offline optimum $J^{*}$; it measures how closely a permission-checked, journaled loop follows an oracle policy that sees the model. Three seeds land between \resControllerMin{} and \resControllerMax{} with no write rejected, previewing Q3.

\subsection{Q1: Protocol Closure and Governance Attribution}
\label{sec:protocol}
On the five protocol lines the suite stores \resDaqSamples{} DAQ samples through
real drivers, including a Modbus RTU-over-TCP satellite.
Governed writes are accepted and read back on Modbus TCP, OPC~UA, MQTT, and HTTP
with per-line medians of \resTcpP, \resOpcP, \resMqttP, and \resHttpP; the median over all governed writes pooled across the four lines is \resWriteP. The identical admission, record, and verdict code path serves every request regardless of stack, so the residual spread between the fastest and slowest line is attributable to the transports rather than to the governance logic. Each of the four writable lines rejects 6/6 configured out-of-window probes while accepting its legal write, so all 24 probes are intercepted and the interlock discriminates between violating and admissible requests rather than merely blocking.

Two drills then separate the truth paths that a single success flag would
collapse. A frozen-measurement drill accepts a write and a consistent readback
while the measurement stays out of window, and the backstop records a system-judged rollback \resBackstop{} later: readback closes the command path, the backstop closes the record, and neither is a settling time. A second preset with different signals and dynamics
is commissioned by configuration alone: three writable lines and two satellite
acquisition nodes are created with \resPortCode{} framework code changes, five
nodes sample, and the writable lines reject \resPortFfive{} probes. Counting both presets, 33 configured probes are rejected across seven writable lines (four in the first preset, three in the second), so the reuse claim is exercised rather than asserted: the same admission code produced the same discrimination on signals it had never seen.

The parameter layer passes \resParamChecks{} and makes the interval geometry of
Section~\ref{sec:objective} observable: a semantic surface without register
leakage, and a four-layer chain (node $[50,200]$, parameter $[143.7,156.3]$,
product $[146.22,153.78]$, recipe $[141,159]$) narrowing to one admitted
interval, with out-of-layer requests intercepted. Each layer contributes a bound, and a rejection is
attributable to the layer that excludes the request. A manual binding instead suspends a request visibly and unblocks it on approval, after which the write takes effect at the device (automated-roundtrip latency \resHitlLatency): workflow mediation, not physics.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} attributes the rejection rather than assuming it, on a pipeline-tier companion fixture kept separate from the five protocol lines; counts are identical across the three deterministic repetitions. Full governance and the no-readback arm reject 18/18 probes, whereas removing the interlock, alone or with the readback check, lets the six window-class probes execute and be journaled. Two readings follow. First, prevention is the
interlock's job: the readback check contributes nothing to interception because
it closes the command path after execution, yet it is retained because it is
what makes a written-but-unconfirmed command visible. Second, attribution
survives removal: the six executed breaches still appear in the journal, so a
relaxed configuration degrades from prevention to detection instead of failing
silently. The hard range is structurally unbypassable and the soft window is
its removable part; no arm records a false block.

\subsection{Q2: The AgentTeam Optimization Workflow and Its Results}
\label{sec:agentteam}
The workflow ran end to end on the cast-film line and then on a larger BOPET line
(Section~\ref{sec:tasklifecycle}). On the single-node mission one of three
permitted writes sufficed: the setpoint moved from \resMissionFrom{} to
\resMissionTo{}, the record was opened and judged, the observation settled at
\resMissionPv{} against \resMissionTarget, the journal attributed the change to
the agent identity, and the task closed \texttt{COMPLETED}. The mission demonstrates targeting discipline as much as closure: the strategy
stopped after one write inside a budget of three because the first response
already satisfied the predicate.

The BOPET mission is the multi-node case: nine devices across five protocol
families, 49 signals, \resBiaxSp{} writable and \resBiaxDaq{} readable, all
created by the \emph{ensure} pass and projected with driver tests passing on all nine endpoints. Thickness started at \resBiaxStart\,$\mu$m against
$\resBiaxTarget\pm\resBiaxTol$\,$\mu$m. Three governed writes on three distinct
nodes---casting speed $32\!\rightarrow\!33.8$, fast-roll speed
$118\!\rightarrow\!120.3$, and rail width $3000\!\rightarrow\!3035$---brought it to
\resBiaxFinal\,$\mu$m, each opening a record, receiving \texttt{keep}, and leaving
an agent-attributed journal entry, while melt temperature stayed near
291\,$^{\circ}$C inside 268--300\,$^{\circ}$C and the parent task reached
\texttt{COMPLETED}. In these two missions, scaling from one node to nine did not scale writes with node count---one write on the single-node line, three on the nine-device line---consistent with per-write rather than per-node admission, record, and verdict overhead.

The strategy slot is also usable by a live engine: a bounded agent turn reached
\texttt{COMPLETED} in \resLlmWall, and a goal-driven turn took \resGoalWall{} to
drive thickness to \resGoalFinal\,$\mu$m against a self-selected target of
$52.0\pm0.8$\,$\mu$m across three writes and three verdicts, then saved the
recipe. The two turns bracket the autonomy range the slot tolerates: the difference lies in deliberation and in the extra writes the goal-driven turn chooses, not in the evidence chain, which both share. This is slot occupancy through the common chain, not model
quality.

\subsection{Q3: The Deterministic Closed Loop}
\label{sec:controller}
Seeds 42--44 each issue two governed writes with no rejection, raising the cast-film objective $J$ (larger is better) from $J_{0}\approx\resJzero$ to $J/J^{*}$ of \resControllerMin, \resControllerMean, and \resControllerMax against the simulator's offline reference $J^{*}=\resJstar$. The residual gap to unity is expected: $J^{*}$ is an oracle computed with full model knowledge, which an online policy that sees only process observations cannot in general match, and the loop spends its writes where the response is largest. What the benchmark establishes is narrower: the governed path passes a conventional controller's writes through unmodified---admissible, journaled, read back---with no ungoverned baseline run, so the overlay's transparency is shown for admission and evidence handling, not measured as a performance delta.

\subsection{Q4: Four Process Missions}
\label{sec:scenarios}
The campaign runs four lines concurrently, each ensured from a blueprint, projected to nodes, and given one channel (Table~\ref{tab:scenarios}); all four attain their archived predicates, evaluated from subsequent observations rather than from the task state. Fig.~\ref{fig:multiscenarioopt} archives how: in every panel the curve is the observed controlled variable, the hatched band its objective window, triangles the governed writes, and the filled terminal marker the archived observation that satisfied the predicate. Each mission iterates the same loop---read the window, write inside the admitted interval, observe the response, judge---so the four trajectories are four executions of one governed loop, not four bespoke pipelines.

\subsection{Cross-Scenario Reading}
\label{sec:synthesis}
Read together, the four missions bound what the framework contributes. The
platform-side behavior is invariant---admission, driver I/O, records, verdicts,
closure---while the write economy varies by a factor of more than seven
(\resInjWrites{} for injection, \resWwtpWrites{} for wastewater, \resAnnealWrites{} for annealing, \resBiaxWrites{} for BOPET) and the mission clock by a factor of more than six (\resInjWall{} to \resWwtpWall{} over the three retained clocks; the BOPET clock was not retained). That variation is produced by construction by the scenario-specific slot: blueprint, guards, and policy.
The three reversions (two blower steps in WWTP, one speed step in annealing)
show the verdict layer doing real work; that all four terminal markers in Fig.~\ref{fig:multiscenarioopt} lie inside their bands is an observed outcome of this run, not a structural property---\texttt{COMPLETED} and predicate attainment remain separate records. Margins are narrow but on record: three terminals sit within 1\% of a band edge (0.02\,g, 0.04\,$\mu$m, 0.92\,HV to the nearest bound), and the compliant cost figure improves 1.3\%. The evidence is simulation-tier, supporting reusable
supervisory execution and repeatability rather than field optimality or physical
transfer.
