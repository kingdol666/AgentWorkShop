\section{Governed Execution Mechanisms}
\label{sec:mech}
A supervisory intervention must preserve distinctions that are often collapsed
in an agent tool call---permission to propose, admission of a value, driver
acceptance, readback, process response, a caller's verdict, and
compensation---and \system{} exposes these as separate, distinguishable
evidence states. Fig.~\ref{fig:runtime} shows the runtime that carries these
steps. One governed intervention proceeds: a task with goal, line, product,
recipe, run, and task identifiers enters a channel and a lead dispatches it to
a bound worker; authority resolves from the member--node binding, never from
channel membership or prompt content; the proposal must lie in the
Eq.~\eqref{eq:admission} interval; on a manual binding a timed approval wait is
inserted (fail-closed on expiry); the driver dispatches, the device accepts,
and readback, where supported, closes the command path; the write opens an
optimization record; the observation is drawn from the timestamped history and
judged; compensation is a separately admitted write referencing the record it
reverses; and the backstop evaluates records no verdict has closed. Every
agent-originated path to the plant passes through the same governed write; the
subsections below follow that path.

\subsection{Task, Observation, and Admission}
\label{sec:objective}
The strategy receives a task and goal in production context and decides what
to propose and when to stop; the platform governs execution over semantic node
parameters---pressure, dosing rate, line speed, rail
width---in engineering units.

For an ordinary agent setpoint request on node $n$, the
admission interval is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{I}_n(t)=\mathcal{H}_n\cap\mathcal{B}_n
                 \cap\mathcal{P}_n(t)\cap\mathcal{R}_n(t),
\label{eq:admission}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{H}_n$ is the hard engineering range and the remaining terms
the baseline, active-product, and active-recipe limits; missing optional
limits add no restriction. The interval bounds one parameter at a time; joint,
cross-variable windows live in the scenario guards. The interval is not a
learned feasibility region or
a physical safety certificate; availability, binding state, and approval policy
are checked separately, and it bounds magnitude, not intent: in-window but
adverse sequences surface at judgment and compensation
(Section~\ref{sec:scenarios} reports two).

\subsection{Semantic Write and Source-Specific Routes}
\label{sec:nodeexec}
A semantic card exposes quantity, unit, precision, range, value, freshness, and
recipe context. The controller resolves the binding and the limits; the driver
then serializes the command. Readback, where supported, closes the command
path, not the process-response path.

A successful agent or manual write installs a post-write hold window
(\texttt{writeLockSeconds}, default \resWriteLock): the loop rests so the
process can respond before the next proposal; a further agent-origin request
inside the window receives HTTP~429 \texttt{WRITE\_FREQUENT}. The
details are narrow: the lock installs only after driver success,
recipe-dispatch and rollback writes are exempt, zero disables it, and it is
per node and agent-origin only---a pacing for loops whose response window may
be comparable to the sampling cadence, not a rate limit on the plant.
\texttt{AW\_BENCH\_MODE=1} admits
harness-declared ablation arms on the manual route that relax the soft window
(the Stage-1 gate of Fig.~\ref{fig:runtime}), the readback check, or the
hold---the hard engineering range is never
bypassable.

Four governed routes---agent tools, human-origin writes, recipe dispatch, and
rollback compensation---reach the same driver through one admission and
journaling entry point, the hard range included; recipe dispatch adds run
context, its values validated against the recipe window at save time. Hold heartbeats are the one non-governed re-dispatch---periodic
rewrites of an already-admitted value, sharing runtime write exclusion without
re-passing admission or conferring member authority. Line
start installs active context before recipe parameters dispatch individually,
and line stop clears context and closes retained open records. These
multi-step operations are not atomic with device state.



\subsection{Observation, Judgment, and Compensation}
\label{sec:agentloop}
An accepted write opens an optimization record; the verdict tool
\texttt{dcw\_judge} records \texttt{keep}, \texttt{rollback}, or
\texttt{uncertain} plus a reason; the verdict, stored with the record it closes
and the response window actually queried, performs no actuation and does not
independently validate the evidence, so a scripted keep can coexist with a
missed objective. Verdicts are attributable---the actor and timestamp travel
with them; corrections amend the stored reason visibly rather than replacing
the verdict, and compensation opens a new record that references the one it
reverses.

Compensation is a new, separately admitted write that may fail or partially
complete; its dispatch is read-back verified, and a rollback record settles to
rolled back only once the compensating value is confirmed at the device, while
recipe rollback restores a definition, not physical PLC values. These
separations keep command status, record status, and process recovery
independently inspectable (Fig.~\ref{fig:walkthrough}).

The backstop is the platform's safety net for writes that no
verdict has closed: when the recorded value drifts out of its window, the
platform rolls the write back on its own. The stored policy waits at least
120\,s, checks at most every 30\,s, and triggers after three returned points
or averages leave the window; recovery executes only while fewer than two
retained rollback records stand on the node---past that point the backstop
still judges and records but defers actuation to the approval policy that
governed the write under review, so rollback loops cannot form. The cadence is
neither a deadline
nor a formal run-time assurance bound.

\subsection{Human Approval Boundary}
\label{sec:hitl}
Manual bindings insert the timed approval wait of the life cycle above before
an agent setpoint or
compensation request; automatic bindings omit the wait but keep the checks.
The pending request shows member, node, value, interval, and expiry; approval
reserves neither interval nor device state, so binding and admission are
re-checked at execution. The wait fails closed: an expired request resolves as
refusal and does not actuate, with expiry and refusal recorded; duplicates are
suppressed. Selecting the approval-free mode is itself a user-side,
line-permission configuration action---agent identities hold no
binding-administration capability---so an agent cannot reclassify its own
writes as approval-free.

The decision endpoint authenticates a user but does not enforce a line-scoped
approver role, and reads, verdict recording, and some configuration changes do
not use physical-write approval---workflow mediation, not segregation of
duties or certified human supervision; a recipe
switch between approval and execution re-admits the value against the new
window rather than re-presenting it to the approver (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\subsection{Design Rationale and Invariants}
\label{sec:rationale}
The strategy engine is replaceable rather than trusted as a control primitive:
the stable plane keeps bindings, semantic I/O, evidence, approval, compensation,
and backstop handling, while a scenario supplies blueprint, limits, objective,
response window, and policy. Two rules enforce that boundary: scenario code
declares and configures but never executes plant I/O, and the platform never
infers a process objective---so every write still passes admission, dispatch,
and evidence capture, and records can be re-evaluated against a revised
predicate without rewriting history.
