\section{Governed Execution Mechanisms}
\label{sec:mech}
A supervisory intervention must preserve distinctions that are often collapsed
in an agent tool call: permission to propose, admission of a value, driver
acceptance, readback, process response, a caller's verdict, and compensation.
\system{} exposes these as separate, distinguishable evidence states
(Figs.~\ref{fig:chanloop} and~\ref{fig:walkthrough}).

\subsection{Task, Observation, and Admission}
\label{sec:objective}
The strategy receives a task and goal in production context and decides what
to propose and when to stop; the platform governs execution. Decision variables
are semantic node parameters---pressure, dosing rate, line speed, rail
width---in engineering units. Authority belongs to deployed member--node
bindings, not the channel, prompt, or task template.

Before a proposal, \texttt{daq\_query} obtains a baseline or response window
and reports freshness. For an ordinary agent setpoint request on node $n$, the
implemented admission interval is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{I}_n(t)=\mathcal{H}_n\cap\mathcal{B}_n
                 \cap\mathcal{P}_n(t)\cap\mathcal{R}_n(t),
\label{eq:admission}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{H}_n$ is the hard engineering range and the remaining terms
the baseline, active-product, and active-recipe limits; missing optional
limits add no restriction. The intersection constrains submitted values---it
is not a learned feasibility region or a physical safety certificate---and
availability, binding state, and approval policy are checked separately.

\subsection{Semantic Write and Source-Specific Routes}
\label{sec:nodeexec}
\label{sec:writepath}
\label{sec:paramlayer}
A semantic card exposes quantity, unit, precision, range, value, freshness,
and recipe context; the controller resolves the binding and limits, then the
driver serializes the command. Readback, when supported, closes the command
path, not the process-response path.

After a successful agent or manual write, the node installs a post-write hold
window (\texttt{writeLockSeconds}, default \resWriteLock): a further
agent/manual request during the window receives HTTP 429
\texttt{WRITE\_FREQUENT}. The lock is installed only after driver success;
recipe-dispatch and rollback writes are exempt, zero disables it, and
\texttt{AW\_BENCH\_MODE=1} bypasses it for benchmark harnesses. The hold is
per node and agent-origin only: it guards a loop whose response window may be
comparable to the sampling cadence, not a rate limit on the plant.

Guards vary by source:
\begin{itemize}
\item ordinary agent writes---the full binding and admission path;
\item recipe dispatch---run context plus route-specific checks;
\item hold heartbeats---periodic writes that keep an operational line hold
  alive; they share runtime write exclusion but confer no member authority;
\item line start---active context installed before recipe parameters dispatch
  individually;
\item line stop---context cleared and retained open records closed.
\end{itemize}
These multi-step operations are not atomic with device state.



\subsection{Observation, Judgment, and Compensation}
\label{sec:agentloop}
An accepted write opens an optimization record with the prior and requested
values, applicable limits, actor, and timestamps. \texttt{dcw\_judge} records
keep, rollback, or uncertain plus a reason; it performs no actuation and does
not independently validate the evidence, so a scripted keep can coexist with a
missed objective. The verdict is stored with the record it closes and the
response window actually queried, so a later reader sees the evidence in front
of the caller; a verdict is never rewritten---compensation or a backstop
action adds a new record.

Compensation is a new, separately admitted write that may fail or partially complete; recipe rollback restores a definition, not physical PLC values. These separations keep command status, record status, and process recovery independently inspectable, and Fig.~\ref{fig:walkthrough} shows the resulting observe--write--judge path from device export to the recorded verdict.

A stored-policy backstop evaluates open records: it waits at least 120\,s, checks at most every 30\,s, and triggers after three returned points or averages leave the window; recovery runs only while fewer than two retained records are already rolled back. The cadence is neither a deadline
nor a formal run-time assurance bound.

\subsection{Human Approval Boundary}
\label{sec:hitl}
Manual bindings insert a timed approval wait before an agent setpoint or
compensation request; automatic bindings omit the wait but keep the checks.
The pending request shows member, node, value, interval, and expiry; approval
reserves neither interval nor device state, so binding and admission are
re-checked at execution. Expiry and refusal are recorded; duplicates
suppressed.

The decision endpoint authenticates a user but does not enforce a line-scoped
approver role, and reads, verdict recording, and some configuration changes do
not use physical-write approval: this mechanism demonstrates workflow
mediation, not segregation of duties or certified human supervision.

\subsection{Design Rationale and Reuse Boundary}
\label{sec:rationale}
The strategy engine is replaceable rather than trusted as a control primitive;
authorization, readback, task termination, and attainment remain distinct. The
stable plane retains bindings, semantic I/O, evidence, approval, compensation,
and backstop handling; a scenario supplies blueprint, limits, objective,
response window, and policy---confining scenario-specific work without
claiming physical portability.

Two rules keep the boundary stable: scenario code may declare and configure
but never executes plant I/O---blueprints, signal maps, guards, and policies
are data and strategy, and every write still passes admission, driver
dispatch, and evidence capture; and the platform never infers a process
objective---attainment is computed by the scenario predicate, so records can
be re-evaluated against a revised predicate without rewriting history.
