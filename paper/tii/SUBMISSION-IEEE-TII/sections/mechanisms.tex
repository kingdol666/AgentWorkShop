\subsection{Approval, Execution, and Evidence}
\label{sec:mech}
Routes add timing checks and request approval when the operation and its
configuration require it. Outside benchmark mode, online agent and manual
node writes must be at least 60\,s apart. Recipe trials follow a per-line
cadence of 300\,s by default; their intervals start at approval.
Manual approval binds an actor and proposed operation to candidate values,
applicable limits, expiry, and the active line--product--recipe--run tuple.
After approval, the handler rechecks authority and compares the current tuple
with the approved tuple before device-write dispatch. Single-parameter waits
expire after 180\,s by default. Recipe-dispatch approvals have a 30-minute
window. A changed recipe definition requires a new
\texttt{recipe\_apply} proposal.

its horizontal positions do not represent measured or run-derived values.
Panel (b) shows the decision and rechecks when approval is required.

A \texttt{recipe\_trial} illustrates this sequence. The handler checks the
bound recipe, active run, candidate parameters, and route timing. If approval
is required, it requests approval and revalidates the request before submitting
a bounded batch through the configured driver. It returns a receipt for each
parameter. A trial does not save or apply a recipe version; the caller can
retrieve later DAQ observations separately before updating, applying, or
rolling back the recipe. The driver registry maps semantic controls to
configured protocol drivers, so operation arguments do not contain
protocol-specific details.

Receipts record what happened to a command; observations record what followed
in the process. Command results are graded as readback-verified,
transport-acknowledged, or unverified. A readback mismatch can trigger up to
three reads at 2\,s intervals, and recipe batches retain verification outcomes
for each parameter. A readback-verified result means the returned value
matched the request. A transport acknowledgement alone does not establish that
the device applied the value, and an unverified result does not establish a
matching readback. DAQ stores later timestamped process observations, while
recipe history, approvals, and task records remain with their owning services.
Identifiers and timestamps can link these records when available, but a later
observation alone does not prove that the command caused the measured change.
Section~\ref{sec:eval} evaluates selected parts of this evidence chain under
the stated test conditions.
