\section{Operational Workflows and Execution Contracts}
\label{sec:mech}
AgentWorkShop connects a strategy proposal to plant-facing work through
two route-specific lifecycles: bounded node exploration and operational
recipe control. Both use the channel's profile and persisted capabilities,
apply route-specific limits and approval, and return execution evidence to
the task. Their records remain distinct: node exploration couples one
changed-value intervention to a later target summary, whereas recipe work
records a trial or dispatch before its caller observes the process and
decides whether to retain a new version. The shared execution contract is
described below.

\subsection{Authority, Admission, and Pacing}
\label{sec:objective}
\label{sec:nodeexec}
A request identifies the actor, operation, node or recipe, and production
context. The server resolves its persisted capability and channel profile;
authority comes from these records. For an online node intervention, the
admitted interval is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{online}}_n(t)=\mathcal{H}_n\cap\mathcal{B}_n
\cap\mathcal{P}_n(t)\cap\mathcal{R}_n(t),
\label{eq:admission}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{H}_n$ is the hard engineering range and
$\mathcal{B}_n$, $\mathcal{P}_n$, and $\mathcal{R}_n$ are baseline,
active-product, and active-recipe limits. Missing optional limits impose no
additional restriction. The interval in~\eqref{eq:admission} is the value-admission
input to the executor; an empty intersection refuses the request. The
resolver identifies the excluding layer (Fig.~\ref{fig:hitl}a), making the
decision interpretable in production terms.

Admission follows the requested operation. Recipe dispatch checks
$\mathcal{H}_n\cap\mathcal{B}_n\cap\mathcal{P}_n(t)$, omitting the existing
recipe window when applying its replacement. This lets a new recipe carry
its own setpoints while preserving equipment, baseline, and product bounds.
Parameter admission constrains magnitudes. The process objective and
coupled-variable guards are evaluated from subsequent observations.

Timing checks regulate supervisory interventions. Online node writes have
an effective minimum spacing of 60\,s. Recipe dispatch uses a persistent
per-recipe interval anchored to approval; trials also use a configurable
per-line cadence (default 300\,s). Applicable checks run at proposal or
execution according to the route; the recipe-operation interval can
refuse a proposal before creating an approval card. Experimental settings
are stated with each trial. Stored-setpoint hold heartbeats form a
separate, default-off maintenance route that reapplies the stored value
without fresh admission.

\subsection{Human Approval and Recipe Control}
\label{sec:hitl}
Manual execution bindings create an approval request carrying actor, node,
candidate value, applicable interval, expiry, reasoning, and parameter
bases. This structured object gives the approver the proposed operation
alongside the governing context. The default timed wait expires after
180\,s and refuses execution; a hold mode waits for an explicit decision.
One pending request per member--node pair suppresses duplicates. The
decision note returns to the proposing member, completing the interaction
between strategy and approver.

Approval is bound to an action and followed by execution checks. The
executor rechecks authorization and active limits, thereby accounting for
capability or product changes during the wait. For
\texttt{recipe\_apply}, a changed definition requires a new proposal; an
unchanged approval dispatches a copied parameter snapshot. The approved
definition thus determines the values passed to batch dispatch.

A line-level recipe-dispatch master switch defaults to manual. It requires
approval for otherwise approval-free recipe operations; changing it to
automatic requires explicit administrator confirmation. The manual line
mode also applies to internal exploration using an automatic recipe
capability. Direct node bindings retain their own execution mode and
admission checks. Agents use these policies but cannot change the master
switch. Approval records workflow authorization; process protection retains
its independently engineered control-system role.

The proposer owns the candidate and interpretation, the authenticated
approver authorizes the pending action, and the verdict issuer owns the
recorded decision. Receipts and operations logs identify outcomes needing
operator follow-up. Deployment assigns that responsibility and the
line-specific approval roles; closing a record is a lifecycle operation
rather than proof of equipment recovery.

\subsection{Graded Command Confirmation}
\label{sec:ack}
Command evidence has two levels: the driver's acceptance ACK and the batch
verifier's terminal verdict. The driver assigns
\texttt{readback-verified}, \texttt{transport-ack}, or
\texttt{unverified}. A matching post-command read supports the first;
a transport or MES application receipt supports the second. The batch
verifier assigns \texttt{verified}, \texttt{unverified}, or
\texttt{failed}. It accepts an existing matching readback or performs up to
three rereads, 2\,s apart. A matching reread upgrades the final ACK to
\texttt{readback-verified}; exhausted confirmation attempts produce an
unverified verdict, and command failure produces a failed verdict. MES
acceptance without a read mapping follows the unverified outcome. Write
history retains the initial ACK, while the batch result stores the final
ACK and terminal verdict.

Batch dispatch records each parameter's result and summarizes verified,
unverified, and failed counts. This granularity preserves a verified
parameter even when another parameter's command fails. A
readback-verified value describes the endpoint's returned setting; target
attainment is evaluated from process observations. Section~\ref{sec:hardening}
tests the distinction with a publish-only endpoint whose
\texttt{ok=true} broker receipt is transport acceptance and whose terminal
confirmation remains unverified.

\subsection{Observation, Verdict, and Compensation}
\label{sec:agentloop}
Bounded exploration couples an intervention with a target summary.
It captures the starting control before asynchronous execution and averages
1-s buckets over a trailing 45-s target window. A successful changed-value
write opens the node record. The exploration workflow waits
10--300\,s (default 45\,s) and queries another trailing 45-s window.
These are query windows relative to their query times: approval can
separate the baseline from actuation, and a 10-s wait can leave about 35\,s
of pre-write observations in the later window. The receipt and target
statistics are returned separately for interpretation of this single-factor
probe. The exploration summary is saved after this observation; refused or
expired approval instead produces a rejected summary without actuation or
post-write sampling.

The governed node lifecycle accepts attributed \texttt{keep},
\texttt{rollback}, or \texttt{uncertain} verdicts with the caller's
interpretation and queried window. Compensation requests a new command
under its applicable admission policy and retains its own outcome,
including partial completion. In the archived experiments, a harness also
compares recipe value, endpoint readback, and an acquisition-store sample.
This three-way check measures agreement between representations sharing an
endpoint.

A record backstop supplies monitoring while an optimization record remains
open. After 120\,s it polls every 30\,s; three cumulative samples outside
the recipe monitoring window trigger a system-attributed rollback toward
the preceding value. Its window has a monitoring role separate from the
admission interval and mission target. Available samples determine breach
counts; missing samples contribute no breach. A caller's keep verdict
closes the record and its monitoring. When retained rollback records reach
the configured limit, further actuation follows the approval policy.

Attribution joins actor, production context, receipt, verdict, and
recipe/run records. The write audit deduplicates the same value on the same
node within 10\,s, while batch records and optimization journals retain
their route-specific lifecycles. Together these records let an investigator
trace the command, the evidence available to its evaluator, and the
parameterization retained for subsequent work.
