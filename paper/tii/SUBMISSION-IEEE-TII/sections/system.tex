\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}
\looseness=-1 The platform occupies the replaceable strategy slot that
Section~\ref{sec:related}'s comparison leaves open: it links team
coordination to plant interaction through industrial nodes
and production context (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). Responsibilities group into
five planes---logical groupings, not deployable units, each an assembly of
modules whose outputs are evidence records:
\textbf{team} (channel and member identity, task state, episodic memory,
engine adapters, retrieval, analysis) turns acquired data into attributed
proposals;
\textbf{integration} (DAQ and DCW nodes, per-node runtimes, protocol
drivers, gateway, MES mirror/direct modes) owns protocol acceptance,
readback, freshness, and transport errors, presenting devices as semantic
nodes;
\textbf{governance} (binding authority, admission resolver, approval queue,
recipe dispatch, acknowledgment grader and verifier, verdict, compensation,
backstop, journal) owns the governed life cycle;
\textbf{operations} (line, product, recipe, and run registries, versioned
recipes, run ledger, operations log) attaches production context while PLC
protection stays independent; and
\textbf{extensibility} (plugin registry, typed SDK, REST/MCP/A2A/CLI/TUI
entry points) admits drivers, tools, and clients through sanctioned
registration services.
Scenario knowledge sits in blueprints, signal maps, predicates, and driver
configuration---not in scheduler, approval, or persistence code; members
are interchangeable across engine adapters, and team topology,
bindings, and limits are data rather than code (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}).
The subsections below describe the node classes, the binding boundary, and
the task life cycle; Section~\ref{sec:mech} specifies the mechanisms the
governance plane implements.

\looseness=-1 As an open-source project under the Apache-2.0 license (links
withheld for double-blind review), \system{} ships these planes as one
service with 58 industrial agent tools, the typed SDK, and the one-command
benchmark pipeline of Section~\ref{sec:procedure}.

\subsection{Supervisory Core and Nodes}
\looseness=-1 The Node.js core hosts channel routing, industrial services, and protocol
adapters. A channel contains a lead, workers, tasks, messages, and retrievable
memory, deployed through WebSocket, MCP, agent-to-agent (A2A), REST, CLI, or
TUI entry points;
channel membership alone confers no write authority.

\looseness=-1 DAQ nodes convert device readings to engineering quantities across four
modalities---scalars, vector profiles, image frames carrying content
fingerprints, and mirrored manufacturing-execution-system (MES)
records---so plant data-shape heterogeneity does not reach the team loop. MES endpoints are reached in
\emph{mirror} mode, pulling records through the same acquisition pipeline, or
in \emph{direct} mode, declaring read and write mappings against the MES
interface. DCW
nodes translate engineering setpoints into driver commands and, where
supported, readback; per-node runtimes manage timing, and gateway services supply
connection state, recipe context, and write bookkeeping. The acquisition
surface declares six protocol families, five with drivers today---Modbus TCP,
Modbus RTU-over-TCP, OPC UA, MQTT, HTTP/REST, and an S7
stub that fails loudly until its driver lands; the write surface adds one
driver per writable family plus MES REST (mock drivers excluded), and the
evaluation instantiates five families as lines (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).
The deployment reaches author-implemented dynamic simulators through the same
node and driver contracts a plant would use; they are deterministic per seed,
not validated digital twins. Reuse is declarative---signals, units, scaling,
precision, driver configuration---and the team loop is unchanged.

\looseness=-1 Driver contracts are deliberately narrow: a driver declares how a semantic
quantity maps to its transport payload, whether the endpoint is writable, and
whether a readback is available; the controller resolves authority and active
limits. Errors attach to the request, register addresses and byte order appear only here, and a new
fieldbus family leaves the loop untouched; undeclared drivers fail loudly
rather than being silently mocked.
Acquisition is supervisory rather than hard real-time: each DAQ node runs its
own cadence under a rotating sweep; the verdict stage queries the timestamped, quality-stamped
history, not a blocking read.

\subsection{Bindings, Context, and Reuse Boundaries}
\label{sec:reuse}
A member--node binding grants access to a sanctioned tool on a specific node and
selects automatic or approval-mediated execution. Line permissions (none,
read-only, operate) authorize binding creation and mutation; runtime
invocation is authorized by the persisted capability, not channel membership.
Intervals are re-checked every request, so revoking
a binding or switching the recipe takes effect on the next request; the same path
serves a console, a harness, and an agent engine---authority does not
depend on the client, only the evidence record differs.

\looseness=-1 Production objects are versioned rather than overwritten: recipes
dispatch against a known-good version, revert non-destructively, and are
compared with the values actually applied; an intervention is read against the
definition active when it was admitted. Plugins register hooks, routes, agent tools, drivers,
and panels only through sanctioned services, so an added driver inherits
admission, readback, and evidence capture. Scenario reuse is bounded by an onboarding contract: the engineer declares a
blueprint (devices, signals, units, roles, setpoints); a differential
``ensure'' pass creates missing devices, repairs drift, and projects the
blueprint into semantic nodes and a recipe; a channel is
built from the team template, and a mission supplies objective, guards,
response windows, and policy. A
BOPET worker proposes casting-speed, fast-roll, or rail-width changes through
bound DCW nodes; the other three scenarios reuse the same pattern
(Section~\ref{sec:scenarios}).

\subsection{Governed Optimization Task Life Cycle}
\label{sec:tasklifecycle}
A governed optimization task is a life cycle rather than a single tool call, and
\system{} exposes each transition separately. The task enters a channel carrying
goal, line, product, recipe, run, and task identifiers; a lead member claims it
and hands it to a worker holding member--node bindings, whose fixed job repeats:
read the controlled quantity, propose a setpoint inside the resolved interval,
invoke the governed write, and record a verdict
(Section~\ref{sec:procedure} instruments it).

\looseness=-1 Each iteration leaves an optimization record, the acknowledgment
grade, an attributed journal entry, predicate attainment, and a workflow
terminal state. Two write lifecycles coexist: direct node writes (agent
\texttt{dcw\_control} or admin REST) open these records, while recipe-chain
operations (batch dispatch) are gated by the run ledger and open none. This
bounds what ``automatic optimization'' means: a controller, scripted policy,
optimizer, or agent engine may occupy the strategy slot, while authority,
evidence, and closure stay on the platform.
