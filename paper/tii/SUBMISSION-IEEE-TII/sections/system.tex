\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}
\system{} links team coordination to plant interaction through industrial nodes
and production context (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). Channels coordinate deployed
members and tasks; controller services mediate node operations; production
objects identify the line, product, recipe, and run. This organization is a
deliberate separation of concerns: a team plane decides what to investigate and
propose, an integration plane presents devices as semantic nodes, a governance plane decides whether a specific request is admissible, an operations plane supplies production context and evidence, and an extensibility plane adds drivers and services through sanctioned registration points. Table~\ref{tab:capabilities}
summarizes the resulting functional surface and, for each plane, what remains
scenario-specific. The decomposition is what makes the framework reusable:
scenario knowledge is concentrated in blueprints, signal maps, objective
predicates, and driver configuration rather than spread through the team
scheduler, approval service, or persistence code.

\subsection{Supervisory Core and Nodes}
The Node.js core hosts channel routing, industrial services, and protocol
adapters. A channel contains a lead, workers, tasks, messages, and retrievable
memory; WebSocket, MCP, A2A, REST, CLI, and TUI entry points let a team be
deployed by a UI, an external harness, or a programmatic client. Engine adapters
preserve member and node identity across runtimes, and channel membership alone
confers no write authority.

DAQ nodes convert device readings to engineering quantities; DCW nodes translate
engineering setpoints into driver commands and, where supported, readback.
Per-node runtimes manage timing and in-flight work, and gateway services supply
connection state, active recipe context, and write bookkeeping. The built-in
semantic surface covers Modbus TCP, Modbus RTU-over-TCP, OPC UA, MQTT, and
HTTP/REST. The deployment connects to author-implemented dynamic process simulators through the same node and driver contracts a plant deployment would use; the simulators are deterministic per seed but are not validated digital twins. This is the first reuse boundary: a scenario
declares signals, units, scaling, precision, and driver configuration, and the
team loop remains unchanged. Fast regulation and independent protection stay
in plant controllers.

Driver contracts are deliberately narrow. A driver declares how a semantic
quantity maps to its transport payload, whether the endpoint is writable, and
whether a readback is available; the controller, not the driver, resolves member
authority and active limits. Connection, transport, and protocol errors are stored with the request instead of being folded into one success flag, so a timeout, a rejected frame, and a written-but-unconfirmed value remain distinguishable. Register addresses, data types, and byte order appear only here, keeping engineering quantities free of vendor-specific encoding at higher layers, so adding a fieldbus family leaves the team loop untouched.

Acquisition is deliberately supervisory rather than hard real-time. Each DAQ
node runs its own cadence with due-time scheduling and in-flight tracking under
a rotating sweep, so a slow endpoint delays only its own samples. Every reading
carries a timestamp, a quality state, and a freshness age, and the response
window later used by a verdict is a query over that history rather than a
blocking read. This lets a proposal be judged against the process response rather than the command echo---and is why a bucket average can lag the device by one cadence.

\subsection{Bindings, Context, and Reuse Boundaries}
A member--node binding grants access to a sanctioned tool on a specific node and
selects automatic or approval-mediated execution. Operator line permissions (none, read-only, and operate) authorize binding creation and mutation; runtime invocation is then authorized by the persisted member--node capability, not channel membership. Hard, baseline, product, and recipe intervals are resolved and re-checked at every request rather than cached: revoking a binding or switching the active recipe takes effect on the next request, without redeploying the team. The same request path serves an operator console, an external harness, and an agent engine, so authority does not depend on the client; only the evidence record differs, carrying the acting member and the proposal's origin.

Production objects are versioned rather than overwritten. A recipe is a versioned named definition, so a run is dispatched against a known-good version, reverted non-destructively, and later compared with the values actually applied; a governed intervention is read against the definition that was active when it was admitted, not against the configuration current at inspection time. The extension surface follows the same rule: a
plugin may register server hooks, routes, agent tools, DAQ drivers, and browser
panels, but only through sanctioned services rather than plant I/O of its own,
so an added driver inherits admission, readback, and evidence capture.

The second reuse boundary is exercised by a reference onboarding adapter built on the platform APIs: it reads a scenario blueprint, performs an idempotent ``ensure'' over the registered simulator endpoints, exports driver configurations, creates one semantic node per signal, and attaches a recipe; existing lines are reused by scenario label without rebuilding the team. The SDK exposes the same REST and typing surface to ordinary programs, and runtime settings are schema-described and hot-reloadable, so deployment policies and sampling cadences change without editing the mission loop.

The stores share identifiers rather than a transaction domain: platform state uses SQLite and files; measurement and object backends use TimescaleDB and MinIO with local fallbacks. A device may therefore accept a write even if later bookkeeping fails, and records lack a run identifier, so time-range queries can cross run boundaries unless callers constrain them---boundaries disclosed rather than hidden.

For a BOPET thickness task, a worker queries the thickness node and proposes changes to casting speed, fast-roll speed, or rail width through bound DCW nodes; the same mission pattern serves injection molding, WWTP, and annealing with only blueprint, guards, and policy differing. Task completion remains distinct from device acknowledgment and target attainment.

\subsection{Reference Scenario-Onboarding Contract}
The reference integration follows a bounded onboarding contract rather than duplicating the supervisory application: the engineer declares a blueprint of device and signal identifiers, engineering units, writable/read-only roles, initial setpoints, and plant-model parameters. The platform performs a
differential ``ensure'' pass---creating missing devices, repairing drifted
signals, verifying ports and connectivity, and loading the scenario model
without disturbing other registered lines---projects the blueprint into
semantic DAQ/DCW nodes and a recipe, and tags the line so a later run can reuse
it idempotently. A channel is then instantiated from the common team template
with bindings to the required nodes, and a mission supplies the objective, guards, response windows, and policy; every observation, write, and judgment flows through the same governed services. The adapter demonstrates
reuse of platform services, not configuration-only commissioning of arbitrary
physical plants. The ensure pass is differential and idempotent, so a scenario
can be re-applied after a partial failure, and the projection is non-destructive
because each scenario is tagged and its nodes resolved through that tag.

\subsection{Governed Optimization Task Lifecycle}
\label{sec:tasklifecycle}
A governed optimization task is a life cycle rather than a single tool call, and \system{} exposes each transition separately. The task enters a channel carrying goal, line, product, recipe, run, and task identifiers; a lead member claims it and dispatches the work to a worker holding member--node bindings on the relevant nodes. The worker then repeats one fixed
job: read the controlled quantity over a bounded time window, propose a single
setpoint inside the interval resolved for that request, invoke the governed
write, wait for the process response, and record a verdict.
Section~\ref{sec:procedure} shows this path in operation.

Every iteration leaves more than a written value. The time-range read returns samples, freshness, and the active window; the write opens an optimization record holding the prior and requested values, the admitting interval, the acting member, and timestamps; readback, where supported, closes the command path. The parameter journal attributes the change to its source; attainment is evaluated against the scenario predicate, and the task closes with a workflow terminal state (Section~\ref{sec:mech} gives the admission, readback, judgment, and compensation semantics).

This separation bounds what ``automatic optimization'' means here. The framework
automates the execution of the loop---reading process state, admitting and
applying candidate setpoints, waiting for a response, recording evidence, and
accepting or compensating the intervention. It does not prescribe a search
algorithm, choose the stopping rule, or claim that a language model
autonomously selects the best action. A controller, a scripted policy, a
conventional optimizer, or an agent engine may occupy the strategy slot, while
authority, evidence, and closure remain platform responsibilities. A mission can
therefore reach its workflow terminal state while missing the process objective,
and the two are never merged into one status.
