\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}
\looseness=-1 \system{} occupies the replaceable strategy slot that
Section~\ref{sec:related}'s comparison leaves open: it links team
coordination to plant interaction through industrial nodes
and production context (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). Responsibilities group into
five planes---logical groupings, not deployable units: a \emph{team} plane
owns member identity, task state, and
episodic memory; an \emph{integration} plane owns protocol acceptance,
readback, freshness, and transport errors, presenting devices as semantic
nodes feeding both the agent and a live plant-mirror view; a
\emph{governance} plane owns the governed life cycle---admission through
compensation and rollback; an \emph{operations} plane
attaches line, product, recipe, and run context while PLC protection stays
independent; and an \emph{extensibility} plane admits plugins, drivers,
agent tools, and SDK clients through sanctioned registration services.
Scenario knowledge sits in blueprints, signal maps, predicates, and driver
configuration---not in scheduler, approval, or persistence code; members
are interchangeable across engine adapters, and team topology,
bindings, and limits are data rather than code---in every plane, a scenario
supplies data and strategy (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}).

\looseness=-1 As a deployable project, \system{} is open source under the
Apache-2.0 license,\footnote{\url{https://github.com/kingdol666/AgentWorkShop};
deterministic plant simulators:
\url{https://github.com/kingdol666/plc-node-simulator}.} and ships the
planes above as one service---team and channel runtime, multi-protocol
acquisition, governed write control, versioned recipes and run ledgers, an
adaptive-modeling training loop with candidate-to-production promotion, and
plugin bridges to external knowledge-base and diagnosis services---with 58
industrial agent tools and the typed SDK declared over the same node and
driver contracts, and the one-command benchmark pipeline of
Section~\ref{sec:procedure} in the distribution.

\subsection{Supervisory Core and Nodes}
\looseness=-1 The Node.js core hosts channel routing, industrial services, and protocol
adapters. A channel contains a lead, workers, tasks, messages, and retrievable
memory, deployed through WebSocket, MCP, agent-to-agent (A2A), REST, CLI, or
TUI entry points;
channel membership alone confers no write authority.

\looseness=-1 DAQ nodes convert device readings to engineering quantities across four
modalities---scalars, vector profiles, image frames carrying content
fingerprints, and mirrored manufacturing-execution-system (MES)
records---so plant data-shape heterogeneity does not reach the team loop. MES endpoints are reached in two
modes---\emph{mirror}, pulling MES records through the same
acquisition pipeline, and \emph{direct}, declaring read
and write mappings against the MES interface. DCW
nodes translate engineering setpoints into driver commands and, where
supported, readback; per-node runtimes manage timing, and gateway services supply
connection state, recipe context, and write bookkeeping. The acquisition
surface declares six protocol families, five with drivers today---Modbus TCP,
Modbus RTU-over-TCP, OPC UA, MQTT, HTTP/REST, and an S7
stub that fails loudly until its driver lands; the write surface adds one
driver per writable family plus MES REST (the evaluation's mock driver lies
outside these counts), and the evaluation instantiates five families as
lines (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).
The deployment reaches author-implemented dynamic simulators through the same
node and driver contracts a plant would use; they are deterministic per seed,
not validated digital twins. The first reuse boundary is declarative---signals,
units, scaling, precision, and driver configuration---and the team loop is
unchanged.

\looseness=-1 Driver contracts are deliberately narrow: a driver declares how a semantic
quantity maps to its transport payload, whether the endpoint is writable, and
whether a readback is available, while the controller resolves member authority
and active limits. Errors attach to the
request, register addresses and byte order appear only here, and a new
fieldbus family leaves the loop untouched.
Undeclared drivers fail loudly rather than being silently served by a
mock.
Acquisition is supervisory rather than hard real-time: each DAQ node runs its
own cadence under a rotating sweep; the verdict stage queries the timestamped, quality-stamped
history, not a blocking read.

\subsection{Bindings, Context, and Reuse Boundaries}
\label{sec:reuse}
A member--node binding grants access to a sanctioned tool on a specific node and
selects automatic or approval-mediated execution. Line permissions (none,
read-only, operate) authorize binding creation and mutation; runtime
invocation is authorized by the persisted capability, not channel membership.
Intervals are re-checked every request instead of cached, so revoking
a binding or switching the recipe takes effect on the next request; the same path
serves a console, a harness, and an agent engine---authority does not
depend on the client, only the evidence record differs.

\looseness=-1 Production objects are versioned rather than overwritten: a recipe is dispatched
against a known-good version, reverted non-destructively, and compared with the
values actually applied, and an intervention is read against the definition
that was active when it was admitted. Plugins register hooks, routes, agent tools, drivers,
and panels only through sanctioned services, so an added driver inherits
admission, readback, and evidence capture. Scenario reuse is bounded by an onboarding contract: the engineer declares a
blueprint (devices, signals, units, roles, setpoints); a differential
``ensure'' pass creates missing devices, repairs drift, and projects the
blueprint into semantic nodes and a recipe; a channel is
built from the team template, and a mission supplies objective, guards,
response windows, and policy. A
BOPET worker proposes casting-speed, fast-roll, or rail-width changes through
bound DCW nodes; the other three scenarios reuse the same pattern
(Section~\ref{sec:scenarios}). The stores share identifiers, not a
transaction domain: a device may accept a write even if bookkeeping fails.

\subsection{Governed Optimization Task Life Cycle}
\label{sec:tasklifecycle}
A governed optimization task is a life cycle rather than a single tool call, and
\system{} exposes each transition separately. The task enters a channel carrying
goal, line, product, recipe, run, and task identifiers; a lead member claims it
and hands it to a worker holding member--node bindings on the relevant nodes,
whose fixed job then repeats: read the controlled quantity over a
bounded window, propose a setpoint inside the resolved interval, invoke the
governed write, and record a verdict
(Section~\ref{sec:procedure} instruments it).

Each iteration leaves more than a written value: an
optimization record, the acknowledgment grade, an attributed journal
entry, predicate attainment, and a workflow terminal state
(per-intervention detail in Section~\ref{sec:mech}). Two
write lifecycles coexist: direct node writes (agent \texttt{dcw\_control} or
admin REST) open these records, while recipe-chain operations (batch
dispatch) are gated by the run ledger and open none
(Section~\ref{sec:mech}). This bounds what ``automatic optimization'' means
here: a controller, a scripted policy, a conventional optimizer, or an agent
engine may occupy the strategy slot, while authority, evidence, and closure
stay on the platform.
