\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Industrial Agents and Closed-Loop Applications}
Agent-based manufacturing has long connected organizational roles to industrial
capabilities \cite{leitao2009agentbased}. Recent LLM-oriented work spans
industrial knowledge graphs \cite{zhang2026comaikg}, diagnosis
\cite{liu2024kgdiag}, PLC generation and verification
\cite{liu2026agents4plc,fakih2024llm4plc}, digital-twin configuration
\cite{xia2024llmsim}, OPC UA machine control \cite{hofmann2025nlc}, and
simulated fault handling \cite{vyas2026ftc,gill2025llmdt}; InstructMPC combines
language-derived objectives with model-predictive control
\cite{wu2025instructmpc}. These studies motivate agent-facing industrial tools; the shared execution layer that carries authority and evidence across scenarios, our focus here, is complementary rather than a new controller or reasoning method.

\subsection{Orchestration, Industrial Models, and Governance}
AutoGen, LangGraph, and CrewAI coordinate tasks and tools
\cite{wu2023autogen,langgraph2024,crewai2024}; the Model Context Protocol (MCP) and Agent-to-Agent (A2A) specifications standardize tool and inter-agent interfaces \cite{mcp2024,a2a2025}. Such frameworks coordinate reasoning and tool invocation, but the exposed tool is usually a function signature with no line, product, or recipe context and no record that separates admission from execution; industrial deployment additionally needs engineering semantics, driver behavior, and production context. ISA-88, ISA-95, ISO~23247, and IEC~63278 provide complementary models for recipes, operations, and digital twins \cite{isa88,isa95,iso23247,iec63278}: \system{}'s versioned recipes and run dispatch instantiate a reduced subset of this stack---master-recipe-style versions over a line--product--run resource model---without batch procedure models or full equipment hierarchy. IIoT and shop-floor agent architectures similarly emphasize contextualized integration \cite{gautam2025iiot}; the nearest neighbors \cite{biskupovic2025supervision,zhao2026shopfloor} target control decoupling and task decomposition, while \system{} ties each write to production-scoped authority and a stored evidence chain.

Permissions, range checks, approvals, and audit trails have DCS/SCADA/MES and
IEC~62443 counterparts \cite{iec62443-3-3}. Formal shielding, control-barrier
functions, and simplex run-time assurance offer stronger specified properties
\cite{alshiekh2018shielding,ames2017cbf,sha2001simplex}. In contrast,
\system{} implements source-dependent checks and a rule-based backstop but makes no formal protection guarantee; the backstop evaluated in Section~\ref{sec:protocol} is an alarm-style rule in the spirit of ISA-18.2 and EEMUA-191 \cite{isa182,eemua191}, not a run-time assurance bound. Formal methods aside, human oversight itself requires more than an approval dialog \cite{parasuraman2000model,bainbridge1983ironies}.

\subsection{Evaluation Scope}
AgentBench and consequential tool-use benchmarks assess agent behavior and
policy compliance \cite{liu2024agentbench,yao2024taubench,xu2024company}; TEP and SWaT support
process-monitoring research \cite{downs1993tep,goh2017swat}. Indirect prompt
injection and its defenses motivate compromised-tool studies
\cite{greshake2023injection,liu2024pinjection}. Our evaluation instead separates source checks, protocol execution, mechanism ablation, controller trials, and scripted multi-scenario missions; adversarial prompting and comparisons with other agent frameworks are outside scope.
