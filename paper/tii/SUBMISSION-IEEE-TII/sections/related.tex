\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
The closest work connects agent runtimes to industrial processes, assets, or
plant-facing services. These studies differ in the operation boundary they
implement and the evidence they report; Table~\ref{tab:nearest} summarizes
representative examples.

\subsection{Industrial Agents and Plant Integration}
Industrial-agent studies span production supervision, control generation,
PLC programming, human-guided control, and equipment-facing assistance
\cite{xia2025control,huang2026made,liu2026agents4plc,wu2025instructmpc,
lim2025llmenabled,zhao2026shopfloor,gill2025llmdt,hofmann2025nlc,
waseem2026pretrained}. Biskupovic et al. focus on service-oriented industrial
cyber-physical systems: agents select services using performance and
computational-load measures, monitor them online, and reconfigure selection
when performance degrades \cite{biskupovic2025supervision}. This line of work
studies agent decisions and service adaptation within industrial applications.

Xia et al. organize cognitive, digital-twin, and physical-automation layers,
representing twin information through snapshots, event messages, and plans
\cite{xia2025architecture}. Other systems expose factory-module operations
through MCP with state-based tool filtering, constrain manufacturing plans,
or compose semantic recipes from OPC UA skills
\cite{koehle2026llmdriven,silva2025mcpmanufacturing,
koehle2025semanticrecipes}.
Safety and audit mechanisms are also being integrated with agent execution.
Graul et al. ground proposals in the AAS and route approved actions through
OPC UA Methods with signed approvals and audit logging \cite{graul2026aas}.
Schall separates advisory language-model agents from P\&ID-grounded
verification and execution, evaluating structural checks across five
simulated process scenarios \cite{schall2026safe}. Lin and Chen build a
governed digital thread across sensors, MES, and ERP and evaluate closed-loop
monitoring and control on manufacturing records \cite{lin2026agentic}.

The present work focuses on the boundary between an agent runtime and a
configured production scene: scene-specific context and signal mappings
remain explicit, while task-mediated and direct operations enter one shared
dispatch path. Route handlers apply configured limits and approval
requirements. Command receipts and subsequent process observations remain
separate records. The studies in Table~\ref{tab:nearest} report different
mechanisms and evaluation settings, so their outcomes are not compared as
though they came from a common benchmark.

Industrial-informatics architectures provide related foundations for
communication and control boundaries. Gehrmann and Gunnarsson describe trust
boundaries and synchronization requirements for digital-twin-based control
and evaluate a protected-update proof of concept
\cite{gehrmann2020dtiacs}. Mahmood et al. organize industrial-IoT functions
around network requirements; Sengupta et al. distribute security services
across fog and device layers and report simulation and testbed evidence
\cite{mahmood2023functional,sengupta2021securefog}. These architectures
address system decomposition and protection at different layers. The
operation checks in this paper complement safety-aware decision support
\cite{jiang2026agentic}; equipment interlocks and plant protection remain
outside the application-level dispatch path.

\subsection{Production Context, Agent Interfaces, and Automation}
Several standards and methods define parts of this integration problem.
MCP specifies interoperable tool discovery and calls \cite{mcp2024}; its
transport-level authorization is separate from the platform's operation-level
scope, policy, grant, and binding checks. ISA-88 and ISA-95 describe recipes
and production operations \cite{isa88_2010,isa95_2025}, while run-to-run
control connects process observations to successive production runs
\cite{moyne2000r2r}. Human--automation research formalizes the allocation of
information acquisition, analysis, decisions, and actions between people and
automation \cite{parasuraman2000model}. AgentSpec and CaMeL address tool
behavior and capability-based information flow
\cite{wang2025agentspec,debenedetti2025camel}. These standards and methods
inform the production context, tool interface, approval, and information-flow
boundaries considered here. This system places the corresponding checks along
a common task-to-device operation path.
