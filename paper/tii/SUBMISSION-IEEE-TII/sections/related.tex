\subsection{Industrial Agents and Plant Integration}
Industrial-agent studies consider service supervision, control generation, PLC programming, and production tasks. Biskupovic et al. study online service selection and reselection in industrial cyber--physical systems. Xia et al. present a digital-twin-connected automation architecture and control generation; related work generates PLC programs or incorporates human instructions into model-predictive control \cite{biskupovic2025supervision,xia2025architecture,xia2025control,liu2026agents4plc,wu2025instructmpc}. In recent TII work, Li et al. use structured instructions, retrieval, and iterative verification to generate and check finite-element scripts for Abaqus; that study evaluates engineering-artifact generation rather than runtime authority over process writes \cite{li2026modsolagent}. Other studies examine language-model agents, manufacturing skills, and natural-language machine interaction \cite{koehle2026llmdriven,lim2025llmenabled,zhao2026shopfloor,hofmann2025nlc,huang2026made}. Vyas et al. combine multi-agent fault-recovery planning and plant-knowledge retrieval with Digital Process Plant Twin studies of mixing and continuous stirred-tank reactors (CSTRs) \cite{vyas2026detection}.

These papers address agent organization, generated actions, or task execution. The present study does not introduce an agent policy or control law. It examines the runtime boundary that carries production context and direction-specific signal mappings into an operation and applies the applicable checks before dispatch.

\subsection{Industrial Interfaces and Semantic Commissioning}
Agent-facing interface work includes Model Context Protocol (MCP) adapters for industrial protocols, semantic recipes built from OPC UA skills, and tools for binding manufacturing data to a cognitive digital twin \cite{silva2025mcpmanufacturing,koehle2025semanticrecipes,xavier2026industriConnect,liu2026semanticcommissioning}. These systems address capability discovery, protocol access, or commissioning. Here, configuration binds known protocol drivers to scene-specific read and write nodes; a named operation resolves those bindings within the same runtime path that checks authority and write limits. We do not treat MCP or a semantic node name as an authorization mechanism.

\subsection{Governed Operations and Verification}
Prior work already provides substantial plant-facing verification. Graul et al. ground proposals in the Asset Administration Shell and combine documented human authorization and PLC-validated OPC UA Methods \cite{graul2026aas}. Schall verifies proposals against piping and instrumentation diagrams, SCADA data, and standard operating procedures; Lin and Chen describe a policy-governed sensor--MES--ERP digital thread \cite{schall2026safe,lin2026agentic}. PLCBench evaluates autonomous agents against commercial PLCs with hardware-in-the-loop process simulation \cite{zhou2026plcbench}. These studies complement the runtime examined here and demonstrate that authorization and safety controls are not new. Our implementation instead examines how configured signal bindings, route-specific checks, shared dispatch, and separate command and process records operate together at the write boundary. This is a system-integration contribution; the studies use different settings and do not establish comparative safety.

\subsection{Production Context and Outcome Evidence}
MCP standardizes tool discovery and invocation; the application remains responsible for operation-specific authority and process constraints \cite{mcp2024}. ISA-88 and ISA-95 define recipe and production-operation concepts \cite{isa88_2010,isa95_2025}. Run-to-run control relates process observations to subsequent decisions, while human--automation research examines allocation of responsibilities between people and automation \cite{moyne2000r2r,parasuraman2000model}. These works inform the production context and evidence distinctions used in this paper. The implementation does not claim conformance to a complete ISA-88/95 information model, and its scripted approval fixture is not a human-factors evaluation.
