\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The probes and missions support different claims. The probes show that selected configurations resolved to the intended node and driver paths; the missions show that a deterministic policy issued governed writes and returned endpoints in band in three simulator scenes. Neither block isolates the write-path effect: the conditions differed in duration and mission logic, and C1 stopped at its first in-band reading. The planned scene--protocol matrix remains incomplete, and the film-line check reused a running platform.

The ablation supports a narrower causal claim: removing the process-window guard allowed all six targeted excursions to reach the mock driver while structural checks remained active. Because it ran in benchmark mode with a mock driver and no readback-mismatch stimulus, it does not assess the full production guard stack. Production-mode examples exercised the interval, step, node-range, recipe-window, and hold-window branches, but each is a route check rather than a rate estimate. Approval decisions were scripted and not matched, so the pilot cannot establish an operator-decision effect. The runtime exposes a common operation contract to registered harness adapters, but no active harness was used in the deterministic blocks. The Agent pilot issued no governed writes and is reported as a negative result.

The first recorded Agent episode is more consequential than its zero-write outcome: the platform marked the task \texttt{COMPLETED} while the process variable was outside the target band. A runtime that treats task completion as evidence of goal satisfaction would hide a failed operation. Goal completion should therefore be a postcondition checked against the same independent data plane used for evaluation, not a status reported by the orchestration layer. This is a design requirement for the harness path, not a property demonstrated by the present experiments.

The evidence model also constrains deployment evaluation. A command receipt, an actuator readback, and a DAQ sample record different things; the tests do not verify that every configured observation is the commissioned response to the selected control point. Before using measurements to assess a write, each scene needs a verified control--measurement pairing and observation delay.

The manifests identify commit \texttt{da627d21} and show that the platform and simulator were already running; they do not capture a hermetic snapshot of all active service source. Supplementary File~S7 retains the runner inputs and records needed to audit the reported runs. Application-level checks supplement PLC, SIS, and equipment protection; the simulator and mock-driver tests do not establish physical-plant safety. Time-matched controls, a PID or rule baseline, cold-start deployment, and a positive live-harness write remain future work.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This work presents a configuration-driven runtime that binds production context and named signals to operations, registered protocol drivers, and shared write governance. Across the collected four-scene simulator probes, 220/220 admissible requests were accepted, 219/219 collected structural-range requests were rejected, and 216/216 available actuator readbacks matched. In the three-scene scripted comparison, 15/15 C1 runs ended in band, while the 60-s no-write condition did not; 12 pairs began from a matched blueprint state, and one C0 control was warm-started. The comparison is descriptive because exposure times, mission logic, and endpoints differed. In the mock-driver ablation, the process-window guard blocked all six targeted violations, whereas removing it forwarded all six while structural checks remained active. A separate Agent-route pilot issued no governed writes; one recorded episode was marked \texttt{COMPLETED} while the process variable was outside the band, which motivates a completion postcondition. In these simulation and mock-driver settings, the results demonstrate the selected scene mappings, scripted-write path, and process-window guard. They do not establish live-Agent or harness behavior, human-approval effects, or physical-plant protection.
