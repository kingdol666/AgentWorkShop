\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\subsection{What the Loop Delivers}
Primary archive $B$ is the reference for the results in Sec.~\ref{sec:eval};
earlier campaigns provide historical limitations, not the headline outcome.
The evaluated paths connect engineering
quantities, member-level authority, and production context to intervention and
compensation records across implemented drivers. Their output is an executed
setpoint and associated evidence within configured limits, not a validated
safe operating interval.

The distinction is operationally useful because the remedies differ. A binding
or range rejection calls for revising authority or the proposal; a transport
or readback failure calls for inspecting execution; an accepted write followed
by an out-of-band process response calls for a new strategy or compensation.
The interlock ablation and frozen-process drill exercise two of these
distinctions: attribution does not prevent an inadmissible command, and
matching a setpoint does not establish a responding process. The platform
makes these stages inspectable without claiming that the agent's judgment is
an independent quality oracle.

Proposal, admission, readback, and process judgment remain distinct.
\texttt{keep} records a submitted verdict; readback checks device-level
agreement; sustained convergence requires separate process evidence.
Observation-quality gating is not enforced, so a judgment may use stale or
unrepresentative telemetry. The deterministic controllers and scripted members
test these execution paths, not LLM optimization quality. Neither successful
writes nor a passing regression gate establishes reliable process control.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:repro}
Commands, seeds, source fingerprints, and per-check evidence support reruns
and archive comparisons, but deterministic decision policies do not make the
whole experiment deterministic. Simulator initial state, asynchronous
execution, and sampling windows can change outcomes. A harness fingerprint
does not pin the separately versioned plant simulator, and working-tree hashes
can depend on line endings. Exact reconstruction therefore requires the
simulator version and reset state as well as the platform and harness sources.

The checks were developed against this implementation, and the reference
$J^{*}$ derives from the same simulator used by the controller. They form a
regression suite rather than an external performance oracle. Historical
warning-only mission outcomes show why an aggregate gate alone is
insufficient, and the current archive also contains a journal check with an
empty evidence array (Sec.~\ref{sec:repeatability}). Historical real-LLM
campaigns used different scoring inputs and observation windows and remain
exploratory, not comparable optimization evidence.

The study is simulation-tier: no physical-device validation, TEP or SWaT
replay, human-operator study, or external-framework comparison is reported.
Readback-consistent writes can still accompany adverse process behavior, and
bookkeeping can fail after actuation without reversing it. Device agreement,
attribution, and process validity are thus separate properties.

\subsection{Deployment Boundaries}
Cross-agent oscillation within permitted ranges, long-horizon contention,
storage-failure availability, and worst-case recovery time remain unevaluated.
The backstop depends on data and policy; its wait and recheck cadence is not a
recovery deadline. At its retained-history threshold $K=2$, escalation
replaces automatic restoration and may leave a record unresolved without an
operator.

Bindings govern sanctioned tool calls, not arbitrary I/O by a privileged or
compromised engine. Least privilege, endpoint security, and IT/OT network
separation remain deployment responsibilities \cite{iec62443,nist80082}.
Governed writes are not a certified safety instrumented function or an
independent protection layer: controller limits, interlocks, and any required
IEC~61511 protection must remain independent \cite{iec61511}. The platform
neither detects nor compensates for their absence.

Manual approval can introduce fatigue and over-reliance
\cite{parasuraman2000model,bainbridge1983ironies,leesee2004trust,parasuramanmanzey2010complacency}.
Role-, batch-phase-, and magnitude-specific policies and enforced segregation
of duties remain extensions. Deployment also requires handling
approval-channel and alarm-path failures \cite{eemua191}, records left open by
crashes, synchronized clocks, and off-host evidence.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\system{} connects deployed agent teams to industrial supervision through
member--node capabilities, production context, and hypothesis-bearing
intervention records. Source-dependent admission, submitted judgment, and
separately executed compensation make the supervisory loop inspectable without
making it atomic or safety-certified. Simulation-tier protocol checks,
deterministic controllers, and scripted missions characterize implementation
behavior, not LLM optimization or field reliability. Physical validation,
stronger execution consistency, observation-quality gating, and controlled
agent comparisons remain open.
