\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:limits}
\subsection{Interpretation}
AgentWorkShop composes collaborative-task services, production context, named
operations, semantic signal access, configured device drivers, and
service-owned records into a shared task-to-device path. The current
experiments trace this design through task or direct-operation entry,
approval-bound dispatch, readback receipt, and a later run-tagged process
sample. Registered task routes preserve the task, approval, receipt, and
sample associations; direct REST preserves operation, readback, and sample
records without a task edge. The two simulator scenes use different signal
and protocol mappings, so the observed joins support the tested composition
and route-specific record contract. Separately, the v0.8.3 replay configured
the documented C01 coating-line blueprint through the existing onboarding
workflow, verified its MES forms and DAQ nodes, and confirmed one simulated
write. This shows that one documented configuration can pass through that
workflow; it is not a repeatability or general-portability result.

The process results bound the interpretation of that evidence. Cast-film
samples range from 45.69 to 46.69\,$\mu$m, or 1.31--2.31\,$\mu$m below the
48\,$\mu$m lower bound. Annealing samples range from 674.40 to
679.60\,$^{\circ}$C, or 20.40--25.60\,$^{\circ}$C below the 700\,$^{\circ}$C
lower bound. These measurements are single snapshots taken 20\,s after an
operation, so they cannot establish a response curve, convergence, or the
cause of the observed shortfall.

Fig.~\ref{fig:missions} pairs requested setpoint changes with the resulting
process observations in four separate archived seed-42 simulator missions.
The trajectories show scenario-specific responses, including the wastewater
mission's later pH and total-phosphorus actions and the annealing candidate
that was reverted. Their terminal observations lie within their own target
windows, but each panel contains one historical scripted direct-node
trajectory. The figure therefore explains the structure of those archived
action--response records; it does not establish target attainment by current
v0.8.0 routes or repeatability across runs.

This gap between command evidence and process evidence is central to the
framework's execution model. A verified readback confirms that the requested
setpoint reached the configured device; it does not show that the process
variable entered its target band. A task can therefore complete its approved
operation path while its associated process sample remains out of band.
Keeping the receipt and run-tagged sample as separate, linked records lets the
system report both facts without treating command completion as product
attainment. The current results validate this distinction in the tested
software paths, not an improvement in control quality.

The route evidence also depends on each entry contract. Registered
model-mediated and fixture-backed CLI tasks retain task, approval, receipt,
and sample links. The fixed-call replay exercises the registered harness with
deterministic input but does not assert task completion. Direct REST reaches
the same operation boundary and retains a verified receipt and run-tagged
sample without a task edge. The fixture replay, direct REST, and CLI checks
ran in separate isolated environments; the model-mediated triplets shared a
persistent simulator per scene. These path-specific contexts and unequal
sample counts mean their numerical similarity is not evidence of route
equivalence. All route-study runs used the SQLite fallback because MQTT and
Timescale were unavailable; the separate C01 onboarding
replay exercised online MQTT/Timescale on isolated services. The results
support the specified record contracts under two configured signal and
protocol mappings and feasibility of one blueprint-driven simulated
onboarding workflow. They do not establish interchangeable adapters,
configuration reuse, or general portability to an unseen scene.

The context-guard test identifies the software check's contribution under
mock state: comparing the approved line--product--recipe--run tuple blocked
product/run mutations that the retained checks alone did not reject. This
supports a bounded claim about software dispatch. It does not show atomicity
across network or device I/O, safe physical operation, or operator
effectiveness. Synthetic approval also does not measure human review
performance \cite{parasuramanmanzey2010complacency,parasuraman2000model,bainbridge1983ironies}.

\subsection{Scope and Limitations}
Only the reported adapter and scene configurations were exercised. The single
C01 replay does not test repeatable onboarding, profile or binding reuse across
lines, arbitrary unseen-scene configuration, or physical transfer. The
evaluation also does not establish general adapter interchangeability,
normal online DAQ operation in the route trials, or a platform
baseline. The
feature-level related-work comparison positions the implementation question;
it does not show that AgentWorkShop is more efficient or more capable than
another platform. A comparative study would need a shared task set, explicit
integration-effort or record-completeness measures, and matched deployments.

The route study used one simulator instance per scene, three sequential
model-mediated trials per scene, and one post-trial process sample per trial.
These samples are not independent replicates and cannot establish a process
response curve. Follow-up evaluation should vary configuration profiles
under a controlled onboarding protocol, use repeated process trials, and
compare against a defined alternative integration path. Operation
restrictions do not establish security
\cite{greshake2023injection,liu2024pinjection,iec62443-3-3} or replace
independent PLC/SIS safeguards \cite{iec61511}.

Scope and channel-policy refusals are not uniformly persisted; auditability
is limited to the records and denial classes described in Section~IV.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
AgentWorkShop brings task adapters, production context, named operations,
semantic I/O, configured protocol drivers, and service-owned records into a
modular task-to-device framework. Tested task routes and direct calls used a
shared operation boundary across cast-film and annealing simulator mappings
while preserving route-specific records. A separate v0.8.3 replay completed
one documented coating-line onboarding configuration with online DAQ checks
and a simulator-confirmed smoke write. Route-study samples remained below
target, and a mock-state ablation isolated the effect of an approval-bound
context check. The study establishes selected integration, one simulated
onboarding instance, and software-contract behavior; it does not establish
process improvement, configuration reuse, physical transfer, general
portability, or comparative platform benefit.
