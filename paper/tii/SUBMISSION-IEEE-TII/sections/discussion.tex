\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\subsection{Industrial-Informatics Interpretation}
The principal result is that one supervisory path carried four different jobs
while keeping command closure, submitted judgment, task state, and process
attainment as separate records. In run \resRun{} all four scenario predicates
hold and the governed closed loop reaches $J/J^{*}$ between \resControllerMin{} and \resControllerMax{} (mean \resControllerMean). That agreement does not collapse the records: a \texttt{keep} is
still the caller's verdict, a completed task is still a workflow state, and a
process predicate is still computed from subsequent observations.

The separation supports diagnosis: a binding or range rejection calls for a
permission change, a driver or readback failure for execution investigation, and
an accepted write followed by an adverse response for revision or compensation.
The two reverted blower steps in the wastewater mission show that the verdict,
not the write count, identifies a rejected proposal.

\subsection{Validity and Reproducibility}
The snapshot fixes the run identifier, seed, commit, harness fingerprint, and raw
artifacts. Initial state, asynchronous sampling, response windows, and simulator
timing still affect deterministic policies; the controller optimum and the
scenario predicates are internal oracles; and the ablation has three deterministic repetitions and therefore supports mechanism attribution, not statistical inference; and latencies are environment observations, not service-level bounds. Evidence remains simulation-tier, from author-implemented dynamic simulators rather than validated digital twins. No physical-device campaign, operator study, TEP/SWaT replay, or external-framework comparison is reported; the four process models broaden structural variety without showing transfer to actual equipment; and records lack run identifiers, so poorly bounded queries may mix production runs.

\subsection{Deployment Boundaries}
Execution and recovery are non-atomic: bookkeeping may fail after actuation, the
post-write hold is a per-node anti-frequency guard rather than a stability proof,
readback agreement need not imply process recovery, and multi-node compensation
may partially succeed; within-range oscillation, storage failure, clock error,
and worst-case recovery time remain unevaluated.

Bindings govern sanctioned tools, not arbitrary I/O from a privileged engine, so deployment requires least privilege, endpoint security, and IT/OT separation \cite{iec62443-3-3,nist80082}. Independent interlocks and any required IEC~61511 protection must remain: \system{} is neither a safety instrumented
function nor a substitute for plant protection \cite{iec61511}. Approval can
also introduce fatigue, automation bias, and over-reliance
\cite{parasuraman2000model,bainbridge1983ironies,leesee2004trust,parasuramanmanzey2010complacency},
and the present endpoint lacks line-scoped approver authorization; deployment
would require fail-closed decisions, segregation of duties, and alarm-path
failure tests \cite{eemua191}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\system{} contributes a reusable supervisory execution architecture rather than a
new process optimizer: a stable path linking agent-team identities, semantic
industrial nodes, production context, governed writes, readback, observations,
verdicts, and compensation, exercised by a \resPhases-phase suite, two
AgentTeam missions, a three-seed closed loop, and four process missions. In this run five protocol stacks execute through
the common node abstraction, all configured full-governance probes are rejected,
and all four mission predicates hold, while the records keep command acceptance,
readback, submitted judgment, and task completion distinct from those predicates.
Claims of rapid commissioning, optimization superiority, autonomous LLM
control, physical-plant transfer, and safety assurance require additional
evidence.
