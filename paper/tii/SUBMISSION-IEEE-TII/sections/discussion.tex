\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Interpretation}
Protocol connectivity and the onboarding replay test different parts of device
integration: communication with configured simulator endpoints and access to
declared acquisition and control nodes. They do not show that arbitrary
equipment can be integrated without adaptation. The entry-path checks document
which records were retained for each tested call. Because the calls used
separate environments and no handler-level trace was retained, they cannot
establish route equivalence or confirm that both reached the same handler. In
the approval-context test, stale product and run identities reached mock writes
when tuple comparison was removed and were rejected when it was enabled. This
isolates the comparison's contribution within that setup.

The six registered-harness tasks and four direct-node missions answer different
questions. The harness tasks link approvals and receipts to single post-trial
probes; the missions record one process trajectory per scenario. They use
separate setups, including different film target bands and different annealing
measures, so mission endpoints do not benchmark the harness probes. Across the
missions, requested actions precede measured responses, including a reverted
annealing candidate. In wastewater treatment, the structured aeration sequence
and final mission summary also refer to different observations. These records
describe trajectories, not isolated effects of individual commands.

The missions also illustrate the role of scene-specific configuration.
Part weight, film thickness, hardness, and dissolved oxygen have different
units, target bands, and available actuators. The operation--response
structure accommodates these differences, but the trajectories do not
identify which architectural component caused an outcome. Related approaches
likewise connect industrial operations to semantic skills, equipment context,
or process validation \cite{koehle2025semanticrecipes,graul2026aas,schall2026safe}.
The present study complements those designs by describing a shared operation
path and reporting responses in four simulated scenes.

\subsection{Scope and Limitations}
The optimization evidence comprises one deterministic simulated trajectory per scene,
without independent replicates or a matched alternative controller. It cannot
establish statistical repeatability, superiority to another platform, or the
effect of individual controls. The missions use direct-node operations and
do not test the full range of entry routes or language-model decisions
described by the architecture. Final tensile-strength and surface-defect
readings for annealing and the complete final wastewater effluent vector are
missing, limiting conclusions about secondary constraints. The process model
and simulator configuration also limit transfer to physical production.

The mission records show automated judgments, not human pre-approval. The
approval-context test uses simulated decisions and mock drivers; it tests a
software condition, not operator performance or the effectiveness of human
review \cite{parasuramanmanzey2010complacency,bainbridge1983ironies}.
Software operation restrictions are not a complete security or safety
assessment \cite{greshake2023injection,liu2024pinjection,iec62443-3-3};
independent plant protection remains necessary \cite{iec61511}. Future work
should repeat missions across initial conditions, preserve all terminal
quality measurements, compare matched controllers and entry paths, and
evaluate the system with suitable physical equipment and operator oversight.

This modular separation is a property of the implemented software structure.
The current evidence does not measure whether it reduces engineering effort,
operating cost, or reliability; those outcomes require matched deployments.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
The platform links industrial task context to scene-specific device operations
through shared dispatch, configured drivers, and separate command and
observation records. The evaluation covered simulator protocol and node access,
registered-harness tasks, mock-driver approval checks, and four direct-node
missions. The four mission endpoints were within their primary target bands.
Separately, all six single probes after harness tasks remained below their
bands despite verified receipts. These simulator and mock-driver results do not
establish repeatability, comparative benefit, human-review effectiveness, or
performance on a physical plant.
