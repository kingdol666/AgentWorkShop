\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The results show that selected configured operations and process-window checks executed in the tested simulator and mock-driver fixtures. This is a prototype integration demonstration, not evidence of process-control efficacy or deployment readiness. The target-band comparison is descriptive: the no-write control ran for 60~s and used a fixed endpoint, whereas the scripted setpoint policy stopped at its first in-band observation. The scene--protocol matrix is incomplete, the guard ablation used a mock driver without a readback-mismatch stimulus, and approval decisions came from a fixture. The follow-up injection result is one scripted run on a simulated twin. The live-Agent pilot issued no governed writes.

One live-Agent episode was marked \texttt{COMPLETED} even though the process variable remained outside its target band. A completion status should therefore be checked against an independent process observation. The present experiments also do not validate every control--measurement pairing. Each scene needs a commissioned signal relationship and observation delay before a process change can be attributed to a write.

The November 1 manifests identify platform commit \texttt{da627d21}, but the active services were not captured as a hermetic snapshot; available runner inputs and records are retained in Supplementary File~S7. Application checks complement PLC, SIS, and equipment protections. Simulator and mock-driver results do not establish physical-plant safety. A process-efficacy study should reset each scene to the same initial state, randomize paired condition order, apply a common fixed horizon, and predefine trajectory metrics such as integrated absolute error, time in band, overshoot, and write count; it should compare against a named rule-based or PID baseline and report scene-stratified uncertainty. Governance coverage should separately test context changes, permission revocation, approval rejection or timeout, and readback mismatch across configurations. Claims about operator approval or live-Agent control require corresponding operator or verified live-harness trials.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This work presents a configuration-driven runtime that binds production context and named signals to registered drivers and governed operations. The probes and mock-driver ablation support the tested mappings and guard paths; scripted missions provide a descriptive target-band comparison. In a later single-run injection-twin pilot, an acceptance script recorded a governed setpoint update with matching readback and an in-band process observation. The reported gain ratio cannot be independently checked from the archived data. The live-Agent pilot issued no governed writes. The evidence supports selected simulator integration paths, not autonomous-Agent performance, operator-approval effects, or physical-plant safety.
