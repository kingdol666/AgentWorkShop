\title{Configuration-Driven Integration of Industrial Agents Across Production Scenes}

\author{}
\markboth{IEEE Transactions on Industrial Informatics, Submitted Manuscript}{Industrial Agent Integration}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Industrial agents can propose setpoint changes, but executing a proposal requires preserving its production context, signal meaning, write authority, and process limits. We present a configuration-driven runtime that binds production context and signal semantics to named operations and registered protocol drivers. Registered Agent harness adapters and direct clients submit operations to a shared dispatcher. Write handlers resolve live context at execution and apply authorization and configured value limits. Routes that require approval hold the request pending and repeat their route-specific checks after approval. Command receipts, actuator readbacks, and later process observations remain separate records. Probes across four simulator scenes exercised selected node-to-driver mappings: 220 admissible writes were accepted, 219 collected structural-range requests were rejected, and 216 available readbacks matched. In deterministic missions across three scenes, a scripted policy reached the target band in all 15 seed--scene runs; no-write conditions reached it in none. This contrast is descriptive because the conditions differed in exposure time and mission logic. With the process-window interlock enabled, none of six targeted violations reached the mock driver. Removing the interlock sent all six to the driver while structural checks remained active. These results provide evidence for the tested integration and interlock paths in simulation; they do not establish live-Agent performance or physical-plant safety.
\end{abstract}
