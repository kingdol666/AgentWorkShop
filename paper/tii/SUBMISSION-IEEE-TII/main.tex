\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Industrial recipe optimization requires more than selecting a promising setpoint:
an intervention must respect equipment limits, use the correct protocol, and remain
traceable when its outcome is judged or reversed. AgentWorkShop links agent-team
coordination to industrial supervision through member--node capabilities, shared
production context, and a governed write path. Engineering limits, recipe windows,
and approval requirements constrain execution; optimization records distinguish
command acceptance, observed process response, agent judgment, and compensation.
The evaluation uses real protocol transports with simulated plants and scripted
teams, keeping language-model decisions outside the measured execution path.
The integrated evaluation passes 75 checks, rejects 24 of 24 configured
out-of-constraint probes across four writable stacks, and blocks none of four
legal writes. A four-arm ablation attributes window-violation prevention to the
recipe interlock: removing it admits six violations across three repetitions,
whereas bypassing readback verification alone does not affect rejection. A fixed controller
achieves 0.966--0.970 of a simulator-derived grid reference across three seeds.
A recorded team mission reduces film thickness from 26.90 to 25.48\,$\mu$m through
four governed writes on three parameters, meeting a $25.0\pm0.7\,\mu$m objective.
The results support a common execution layer that separates authorization,
device acceptance, and process success. Physical validation and controlled
agent comparisons remain necessary before claiming field reliability or
autonomous optimization superiority.
\end{abstract}
