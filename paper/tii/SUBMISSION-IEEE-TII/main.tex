\title{Governed Setpoint Writing for Industrial Agents: A Configuration-Driven Runtime and Simulated Evaluation}

\author{}
\markboth{IEEE Transactions on Industrial Informatics, Submitted Manuscript}{Governed Setpoint Writing}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Industrial agents can propose process changes, but a device write must be resolved against the active production context, control point, and current limits. This paper presents a configuration-driven runtime that binds line, product, recipe, and run context to direction-specific signal mappings. Task-mediated and direct calls share dispatch; route handlers resolve live state, perform applicable revalidation and approval, and pass writes through common guards. This separation makes a task's initial context descriptive rather than a grant of authority. Command outcomes and optional device readbacks are recorded separately from subsequent process observations. Across four simulated scenes, 220/220 admissible mapping probes were accepted, 219/219 collected structural-range probes were rejected, and 216/216 available simulated readbacks matched. In a mock-driver ablation, all six targeted violations returned HTTP 400 with the process-window guard enabled and HTTP 200 with it removed. In a separate three-scene comparison, 15/15 scripted and 0/15 no-write endpoints were in band, but their different endpoints and horizons preclude an efficacy estimate. The live-Agent pilot issued no governed writes. The evidence supports selected simulator and mock-driver paths, not process-control efficacy or physical-plant safety.
\end{abstract}
