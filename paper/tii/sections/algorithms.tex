\section{Agent-Side Algorithms}
The governance of Sec.~IV constrains what agents may do; the algorithms in this section determine how well they do it. \emph{Hybrid memory assembly.} Long-horizon tasks exceed any context window, so \system{} maintains typed memories (session, task, peer, chronicle, reflection, semantic) with importance, access counts, and timestamps. Retrieval fuses a lexical channel (FTS5, CJK-aware unigram segmentation) with a dense vector channel (sqlite-vec $k$-NN) by reciprocal rank fusion ($K{=}60$ \cite{cormack2009rrf}), scores candidates by $0.5\,\rho + 0.3\,\phi + 0.2\,\iota$ (relevance, kind-aware recency with half-lives of 3--14\,d, access-weighted importance), and injects the primer through maximal-marginal-relevance selection ($\lambda{=}0.7$ \cite{carbonell1998mmr}) in three layers charged to a 500-token budget; compaction triggers at 70\,\% context fraction and harvests summaries back into memory. \emph{Lead supervision with guaranteed fallback.} The lead's LLM call is skipped when the sorted state fingerprint is unchanged (an idle plant costs no tokens), and a rule-guaranteed fallback executes whenever the LLM abstains, errors, or returns nothing actionable---FIFO dispatch, shortest-queue-then-longest-idle worker selection, up to three retries, and a 300\,s stall watchdog that treats tool activity as liveness. Both algorithms are ablated as E2/E3 in the released protocol; their constants are verified anchors in the static audit (Sec.~\ref{sec:static}).
