\section{Integrated Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Evidence Design and the Reference Run}
\label{sec:protocol}
\label{sec:static}
Four questions drive the evaluation: can the platform provision heterogeneous
protocol stacks and acquire from them; does the governed write path reject requests
that break engineering or recipe constraints without blocking legal ones; can a
fixed controller close the acquisition--actuation loop on a process model; and can a
scripted team turn a production objective into governed actions that reach it?
Source
inspection, mock drivers, real protocol transports driving simulated plants, and
historical LLM campaigns stay apart; nothing here comes from physical hardware or
human operators, and the latency figures do not transfer to physical transports. No headline
claim in this paper rests on an LLM decision.

The evidence is not uniform: the out-of-constraint write probes exercise the hard-range and recipe-window checks directly, the lifecycle and mission checks exercise rollback and approval, and the ownership and cooldown checks are traversed by every governed agent write (Sections~\ref{sec:port}, \ref{sec:e1a}, and \ref{sec:agentloop}). The probes are fixed protocol cases, not model-generated adversarial inputs.

Except where an archive is named, the results in this section come from one reference archived run,
$B=\texttt{20260918043504-bdo}$: seed 42, commit \texttt{7d4bfc0}, hash \texttt{00e7dc827f4a2cea} over the modules of the \bench{} evaluation harness, Node v24.19.0 on \resHost{} x64. All 75 checks pass, with no warnings and no
failures; the objective, control law, and probe definitions are fixed properties
of the harness source named by that hash, not archive contents. The
run therefore measures platform, protocol, governance,
controller, and team mechanisms: the agent-tool loops use the deterministic
\texttt{opencode} engine, and the LLM agent option is off. Three plants are exercised: a
cast-film physics model, a strategy-based film line, and a 49-signal
biaxially oriented polyethylene terephthalate (BOPET) line. The reference is
designated rather than sampled; its position in the eleven-archive family of
Sec.~\ref{sec:agentloop} is reported there.

The 24/24 count lives in phase P3 (per-line integration checks
\texttt{line-$n$-io}) and the 9/9 scenario count in phase P8 (checks
\texttt{port-$n$-*}). Each writable stack rejects four engineering-range and two recipe-window

The probes are node-high/low $\pm$15\,\% of span, $10^{6}$, $-50$, and the
recipe window widened by 20\,\%, so the set shows rejection
of gross violations and nothing about where the true limit lies. The readback
column rests on one post-write sample per stack, and MQTT and HTTP return no

transport level.

The same run provisions the film-line scenario with three writable lines and two
satellite nodes: its nine out-of-constraint probes (three node-range probes per
writable line) are all rejected and its three legal writes accepted, with no code
change; that set exercises no recipe-window branch. Both scenarios come from the same
simulator and five drivers, so this is configuration reuse within the
existing vocabulary, not portability to an independent plant.

A recorded rollback verdict leaves the register untouched until a separate rollback
call passes through the write path; and manual mode suspends an agent request until
the scripted approval releases it, measured once at 16\,ms, timing the approval
polling path rather than a human decision. The audit
surface holds 11 parameter-change anchors with 200-entry audit and
operations-log windows, so intent, approval, and execution are separately
inspectable, though the journal is not a signed ledger and protocol I/O is not
atomic with a concurrent recipe change.

A frozen four-arm ablation, archived separately (\texttt{run-e1lite-4arm} in the
frozen 20260914 baseline; seed 42, three repetitions, commit \texttt{d6c824d},
an ancestor of the reference commit), shows what the write path contributes; its arms are full, no interlock, no readback, and ungated. The ablation runs
on the mock driver tier against a single-node recipe window, and its three
repetitions per arm produce identical verdicts (latencies and a few probe
readings vary at sub-check resolution), i.e., the ablation evidence is one
deterministic decision per arm. Per-write latency showed no detectable difference at the observed resolution. With the full path, all six probes are rejected. With the batch-window interlock disabled, or with the path ungated, four are still rejected and the two recipe-window violations execute at the (mock) driver tier; disabling readback alone changes nothing. The engineering range is therefore structurally enforced at the mock tier, and the batch-window check is the one that adds protection, while attribution records what happened without preventing it. The ablation archive carries boundary probes one tenth of a unit either side of the window edge: rejected where the window check is active, accepted where it is disabled, with no false block in any arm. The process-parameter layer passes 4/4 checks: out-of-product and out-of-baseline
values are rejected at their respective layers, the parameter reads back, and an
unbound agent is denied; agents thus receive a process-facing interface that does
not disclose transport addresses (Sec.~\ref{sec:paramlayer}).

\subsection{Fixed-Controller Closed-Loop Benchmark}
\label{sec:clbench}
The controller in this layer contains no LLM. It starts the cast-film twin at screw
speed $N=150$\,rpm, line speed $v=95$\,m/min, and zone setpoint $200\,^\circ$C, and
\emph{maximizes} the objective
\begin{equation}
J = 55\,J_\text{thick} + 25\,J_\text{quality} +
8\,J_\text{energy} + 7\,J_\text{throughput},
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
subject to melt temperature in $[195,225]\,^\circ$C and pressure at most 22\,MPa.
Each term is a normalized score in $[0,1]$, so $J\in[0,95]$: thickness quality
saturates once $|h-50|\leq2\,\mu$m, and the other terms reward low defect rate, low
screw speed, and high line speed. The controller updates $N\leftarrow N(50/h)$ under
a 22-rpm step limit, holds line speed fixed, and corrects heating only when
temperature leaves $[196,224]\,^\circ$C. The reference $J^{*}$ comes from the
separately maintained simulator, not from the run. Two features of that scoring
bound the ratio: the throughput and energy terms are evaluated from the issued
setpoints rather than from measured values, because the twin returns no
independent reading for them, and the control law inverts the model's own
relation $h\propto N/v$, so the plateau is structural. The suite's gate is
$J/J^{*}\geq0.8$, so the reported value is not near a threshold.

The three seeds start at $J_0=70.115$, 68.792, and 70.248;
Fig.~\ref{fig:clbench}(b) plots each seed's objective against the archived
reference. All six governed writes
are accepted and the mean wall time is 36.193\,s per seed. The
archive reports an end-of-run score $J_{\rm end}=87.105$, 87.027, and 87.366
(the mean of the last two evaluations), giving
$J_{\rm end}/J^{*}=0.969$, 0.968, and 0.972; the last measured evaluation is
lower in every seed, at 86.921, 86.609, and 86.975, or 0.967, 0.963, and 0.968. The
stopping rule requires two consecutive converged observations, and the first write
already brought thickness inside the $50\pm2\,\mu$m plateau, so the second write
follows from the rule and not from further improvement: it raises defect rate from 0.38--0.53\,\% to 0.62--0.72\,\% and lowers the composite objective. We therefore
quote $0.963$--$0.972$ across both conventions and do not describe the trajectory as
monotone. Final thicknesses are 49.883, 49.383, and 49.317\,$\mu$m.

Decomposing $J$ at the final point shows the residual gap: both reference and
seeds saturate the thickness term, and the seeds' deficit on defect quality,
screw-speed energy, and line throughput reflects the controller's restricted
action set (it holds line speed at 95\,m/min while the reference reaches
$h=49.18\,\mu$m at 40\,m/min and 55\,rpm), not a failure to reach thickness.

\subsection{Scripted Team Missions}
\label{sec:agentloop}
Both missions are dispatched by \emph{scripted} channel members: the harness creates
the lead and the worker on its mock engine and the benchmark script issues every
tool call itself, so no language model decides a setpoint; the missions exercise the
path an agent would use, not agent performance. The first asks whether an objective
filed on the channel task board can travel through team delegation, a governed
write, and back to a closed task: bring a Modbus-TCP temperature node to
$204.704\pm0.75\,^\circ$C in at most three governed writes after reading recent
data. The lead dispatches the task, a bound worker reads five minutes of
\texttt{daq} history, and one \texttt{dcw\_control} request raises the setpoint from
200 to 204.704; the readback returns 204.700, the record is judged \texttt{keep},
the write is journal-attributed, and the parent task reaches \texttt{COMPLETED}
with all six mission checks passing. The $\pm0.75\,^\circ$C band is loose (a
$204.0\,^\circ$C request would also pass); the mission shows
the delegation--write--completion path closes, not that the band certifies
precise targeting.

The second mission is the more demanding one; Fig.~\ref{fig:agentteam} maps it to
the line, the team, the admission gate, the three recorded writes, and the
response they produced. Its BOPET preset is provisioned as one full line with nine
devices, 30 writable setpoints, 19 configured acquisition nodes, and driver tests
passing on all nine, all from the benchmark's own probe-and-provision
step, so this layer measures provisioning and governance, not commissioning a line
that already existed. The scripted team files a thickness objective of
$25.0\pm0.7\,\mu$m, accepted as $|h-25.0|\leq0.7\,\mu$m; the initial gauge
reading through the real MQTT path is 28.10\,$\mu$m, outside the window. The worker
then applies three governed actions: cast-roll speed
$32\rightarrow33.8$\,m/min on the casting Modbus-TCP node brings
thickness $28.10\rightarrow26.62\,\mu$m (\texttt{opt-86313140}); fast-roll speed
$118\rightarrow120.5$\,m/min on the machine-direction orienter (MDO) Modbus-TCP
node brings it to
$25.90\,\mu$m (\texttt{opt-41929fe2}); and exit rail width $3000\rightarrow3031$\,mm
on the transverse-direction orienter (TDO) OPC UA node brings it to $25.68\,\mu$m (\texttt{opt-8d2faadb}). Each
action is a separate governed write to a different line section, spanning
two protocols and two engineering units. Every write cleared the gate of
Fig.~\ref{fig:agentteam} (checks \texttt{biax-mission-multinode} and \texttt{biax-mission-attained}), was accepted by its driver, opened its own
optimization record, and was followed by a gauge re-read after a settling wait;
the archive stores no post-write readback value for these
three writes, so we claim none. The verdicts are not an independent finding:
the scripted caller issues \texttt{keep} unconditionally, so the records show
that judgment is stored separately from execution and compensation, not that a judgment was earned. The journal query for the cast-speed node returned an agent-source record (the parameter layer of Sec.~\ref{sec:port}); the mission check asserting journal attribution is the one with an empty evidence array in Sec.~\ref{sec:repro}.

Eleven archived runs of the reference command family share its seed; ten match its command line verbatim and one collapsed archive adds an LLM-agent flag. Seven report a final thickness inside the band (a small-$n$ observation, not a reliability estimate), three collapsed to near-zero thickness and reappear in the film-break account of Sec.~\ref{sec:repro}, and one registered zero governed writes with a hard multinode failure and no final thickness, excluding it from the ten with reportable endpoints. The reruns span several harness revisions; the strictly same-version comparison is the $25.68\,\mu$m/$-0.02\,\mu$m pair of Sec.~\ref{sec:repro}. The run reported here is one of the seven.

The final thickness is 25.68\,$\mu$m, satisfying $|h-25.0|=0.68\leq0.7\,\mu$m by
$0.02\,\mu$m on a short bucket mean (the mean of the last five one-second acquisition
buckets), and the mission closes with 3 of its 6 allowed
writes used. This margin is finer than the run-to-run variation of
Sec.~\ref{sec:repro} and characterizes this run, not a robust property.
Melt temperature stays near $291.5\,^\circ$C inside its
268--300\,$^\circ$C process window, an observation rather than an enforcement
result, since no disturbance tested that bound.

The mission is a scripted policy, not a search: each actuator carries a preset
proportional gain on the relative thickness error, chosen
by fixed rotation through the three knobs, with gains $0.5$, $0.35$, and $0.3$ set
deliberately low so convergence takes three to four rounds and more than one
node is involved; the shrinking response ($1.48$, $0.72$, and $0.22\,\mu$m) and
the three-write length follow from that configuration, not from emergence. What the
mission shows is operational: the binding model resolves a different node,
range, unit, and recipe window for each write, and the records keep the
three writes separate while attributing them to one objective; it does not establish
autonomous strategy quality, cross-model generality, or physical-process
performance.

\subsection{Reproducibility, Recovery, and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:e1a}
\label{sec:repro}
The backstop drill freezes a process variable outside its active window; the harness
injects the breach rather than a drifting plant producing it. The system identifies
an eligible open record, records a rollback judgment, and restores the recorded
baseline in 120.161\,s, a latency set by the backstop's own wait-then-recheck cadence
rather than by the plant. The drill shows one bounded recovery execution: it does
not exercise escalation at $K=2$, does not test detection of a slowly drifting
excursion or of a gap between batches, and sets no worst-case recovery deadline.

First, during harness development a burst of writes across several protocols drove the plant
into a collapsed state---pump pressure, draw ratio, and thickness at their
limits---while every platform-side write stayed readback-consistent: a readback
therefore confirms that a driver delivered a value, not that the plant is in a sane
state. Second, a film-break guard that reapplies the recipe baseline is present in the mission harness but is not represented in archive $B$, which never entered that branch. Three archives of the Sec.~\ref{sec:agentloop} family entered it, one of them earlier on the same day at the same recorded commit, and in each case the re-application failed to restore thickness, leaving the plant near or below zero thickness (final values $0.02$, $-0.02$, $-0.02\,\mu$m against a $\sim$25\,$\mu$m band); one $-0.02\,\mu$m archive is the same-hash, same-seed sister of the nondeterminism example below. A fourth archive collapsed without entering the branch. The guard therefore aborts rather than recovers, and end states near or below zero thickness indicate that the plant model, not only the governance layer, bounds this result. In each of the three collapses the mission's own optimization records were superseded within about a minute of the write, so the independent backstop's 120-second eligibility window never opened; the guard's abort, not a backstop rollback, is what ended those runs. Third, the audit path fails open: bookkeeping errors are caught without reversing an executed write, so a physical change can occur without an attribution record, and the platform logs
the error without blocking the write or retrying the record. Attribution is
therefore a strong default, not a guarantee.

The command
(\texttt{node bench/pipeline.mjs --profile integrated --seed 42 --cl-seeds 3}),
seed, commit, and a hash over the nine harness modules let another operator rerun
the suite and compare judge-class outcomes against the same baseline. Five caveats remain. (1) The harness-hash covers working-tree modules and is sensitive to checkout line endings. (2) The plant simulator is a separately versioned dependency outside the fingerprint, so the exact harness and plant behind these numbers cannot be reconstructed from the archive alone. (3) The biaxial journal-attribution check passed with an empty evidence array, so the run bookkeeping is not uniformly clean. (4) The checks were written against this implementation. (5) The reference $J^{*}$ comes from the same simulator the controller optimises, so the gate is a development-time regression suite, not a benchmark with an external oracle. The simulator snapshot submitted with the paper corresponds to the plant state described here. A final caveat concerns the mission layer. Its starting thickness is a simulator
residue rather than a pinned initial condition. Later runs of the same command on
the same host reached different outcomes: two archives sharing one harness hash
and one seed, neither of them $B$, ended at $25.68\,\mu$m and at $-0.02\,\mu$m.
In the second, both mission checks are recorded as warnings, no check failed, and
the hard gate stayed green. The suite is therefore not deterministic at the mission layer, and the 75/75 gate describes archive $B$ rather than a property of the system. Whether this stems from harness-version drift or runtime nondeterminism was not isolated; a rerun campaign against a pinned simulator revision is required to separate the two. Verdicts and record states are deterministic within a run; protocol latencies are not, and the two should not be read as interchangeable. The evidence remains simulation-tier: no
physical device, TEP or SWaT replay, human-operator workload study, or
external-framework comparison was run.

Historical real-LLM campaigns are exploratory only: same governed interface but
different scoring inputs and temporal windows, a legacy script that substituted
missing observations, and a completion regex that could match an instruction
template---so their scores are not aggregated above. Archive $B$ uses the scripted
path; Section~\ref{sec:limits} collects the resulting limitations.
