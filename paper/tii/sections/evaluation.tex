\subsection{Benchmark Protocol and Data Layers}
\label{sec:protocol}
\bench{} is governed by three principles. \emph{Determinism}: a seeded PRNG (mulberry32) and a scenario-snapshot API make structural metrics (interception verdicts, judge-class checks) point-wise reproducible; objective values over \emph{sampled} process variables remain timing-sensitive (Sec.~\ref{sec:repro}); the offline grid-search optimum $W^{*}$ is the reference bound. \emph{Layered realism}: results are reported per data layer (Table~\ref{tab:layers}), never pooled, so claims carry their realism level. \emph{Executable protocol}: one released audit harness (\texttt{bench/run.mjs} and \texttt{bench/pipeline.mjs}; seeded tiers) executes every check of this section---nothing in it is asserted by hand.

The workload is phrased as five task families---\emph{T1} closed-loop setpoint correction in-window, \emph{T2} out-of-window excursion, \emph{T3} multi-node joint adjustment under the step rule, \emph{T4} batch parameter recovery, \emph{T5} mid-run objective change---and six fault families injected at the simulator control API: F1 step drift, F2 sensor freeze, F3 communication interruption, F4 out-of-order injection, F5 out-of-window writes, F6 storage unavailability. F5 stresses containment (I1, Sec.~\ref{sec:writepath}) and F6 degraded-mode integrity; F2/F3 are exercised in Secs.~\ref{sec:dynamic} and \ref{sec:plc}. The measured baseline is \emph{B1}, the pipeline's own ablation via env-gated bench-mode-only bypass switches (Sec.~\ref{sec:e1a}); further baseline groups---general agent frameworks, human operators, classical control, cross-engine replication---are defined in \bench{}'s released protocol and are \emph{not} measured here.

Metrics form three families. \emph{Safety} metrics require the governed pipeline: out-of-window write interception rate (denominator = seeded attacks), readback-failure capture rate, false-block rate (denominator = legal in-window writes attempted), spurious-rollback rate, and per-write attribution rate (target 100\,\%). \emph{Performance} metrics cover the \emph{automatic segment} (proposal acceptance to verified write), kept separate from human-approval waiting time so human response is never conflated with system latency. \emph{Cost/quality} metrics cover task goal rate, tokens and LLM calls per task, approval wait time, and human interventions per task. \emph{Reproducibility protocol}: every run records its seed, harness source hash, and commit, appends to never-overwritten timestamped directories, and is compared against frozen baselines by a gate that checks judge-class equality (Sec.~\ref{sec:repro}); released LLM arms pin model versions, and temperature-0 applies to campaign configurations.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
All results were produced by one executable pipeline (\texttt{bench/pipeline.mjs}) that boots the plant simulator and the platform, provisions the plant over real protocol transports, drives the governance surface, runs the closed-loop campaign, and writes a machine-readable archive; one command reproduces the section:
\begin{center}\footnotesize\texttt{node bench/pipeline.mjs --profile integrated --seed 42 --cl-seeds 3}\end{center}
Every reported number is read from a never-overwritten run directory recording the seed, the harness source hash (SHA-256 over the eight checker sources), the commit, the platform, and the evidence chain. The integrated runs quoted here are the 2026-09-14 pair (\texttt{20260914152838-fwg}, hash \texttt{204fc9ee}) and its reproduction twin \texttt{20260914153400-ce0}, unless a range over all released runs is stated. Host environment: \resHost{}. Environment-class quantities (latency, sampling counts, wall time) vary across releases and are reported as archived ranges; judge-class quantities (interception, false blocks, boundaries, attribution, structural verdicts) were bit-identical in every same-seed re-run.

The fourteen stages (P0--P9 with P4b--P4e and P8b; no P5) run preflight and bootstrap, plant instantiation with the offline optimum, multi-line provisioning over five drivers, acquisition and interlock checks, the deterministic tool-level control loop over three observe--actuate--judge cycles per line, the approval gate, audit reads, the recipe lifecycle, the closed-loop benchmark, multi-modal acquisition, cross-scenario portability (Sec.~\ref{sec:port}), the backstop drill and the subsystem benchmarks (Sec.~\ref{sec:govsurface}). A full run over five devices takes about 5.2\,min---the closed-loop benchmark about 36\,s per plant seed, the backstop drill a cadence-bound 120--130\,s window.

\emph{Headline results.} Across both scenarios the pipeline intercepted 29/29 seeded out-of-window/range writes (Wilson 95\,\% [88\,\%, 100\,\%], zero false blocks)---20 real-protocol attacks (Sec.~\ref{sec:plc}) plus 9 portability attacks (Sec.~\ref{sec:port}); the six API-tier probes of Sec.~\ref{sec:dynamic} re-exercise the same invariants and are not double-counted---recommissioned the deployment onto the second scenario with zero platform code changes, recovered 96.2--97.3\,\% of the offline grid optimum in the governed closed loop under a fixed controller (quoted run: 97.0--97.2\,\%), and exercised the backstop end-to-end ($\approx$120--130\,s). An LLM agent team closed the same loop over real transports through the same path, but reached only a median 0.76 of that optimum (Sec.~\ref{sec:case}).

\emph{Scope.} The reported run exercises T1--T2 and F2/F3/F5 against baseline B1; T3--T5, F1/F4/F6, hardware-in-the-loop, public-dataset replay, cross-engine replication, and B2--B8 are covered by the released campaign designs and are \emph{not} reported here. All measured layers are simulation-tier (Table~\ref{tab:layers}).

\subsection{Cross-Scenario Portability (Framework Claim)}
\label{sec:port}
The central framework claim---adapting the deployment to a new line is a configuration task, not an integration project---is measured directly by the pipeline's final stage. Everything is first provisioned on the \emph{cast-film physics} scenario (ODE plant model, six actuators, seven sensors, five protocol drivers, vector and image frames); the plant then switches to a second, independently defined scenario, \emph{film-line}: a coating-oven line with a disjoint signal domain and per-signal strategies rather than a physics engine. No scenario-specific code exists in the commissioning path---the identical five-step flow (export, connection test, node creation, line/product/recipe provisioning, agent binding) re-instantiates the deployment on both, because driver configurations derive from each device's export (P3; Sec.~\ref{sec:nodes}).

Measured on the second scenario: five devices across all five protocol drivers are commissioned in one pass (the commissioning artifact is five derived driver configurations plus one scenario identifier); three writable-setpoint devices become governed lines (Modbus TCP, OPC UA, MQTT) and the two acquisition-only devices attach as satellites; all five nodes stream live data (8--12 one-second buckets in the quoted pair); governed writes are accepted with readback deviation $\le 0.04\,^\circ$C on Modbus TCP and OPC UA (exactly zero on OPC UA in the quoted run); and all nine seeded out-of-window attacks are rejected with zero false blocks (none of the 3 legal in-window writes blocked). The stage completes in about 16\,s in the quoted run (15.7--23.0\,s across releases) and the rig is restored. The structural verdicts---100\,\% interception, 0\,\% false blocks---are identical across scenarios: per-line engineering moved into authoring the scenario definition, and the platform absorbed the difference unchanged.

Fig.~\ref{fig:walkthrough} shows that path as an operator drives it on the live instance: provisioning a device as a \emph{node} whose driver, cadence and line derive from its export (\textbf{1}); binding it to a device twin and a recipe window, so the process window travels with the deployment (\textbf{2}); deploying one lead plus harness-backed workers into a channel, where one team definition runs on an LLM engine or on the deterministic replay engine (\textbf{3}); and each write then opening an optimization record that \texttt{dcw\_judge} resolves to \emph{keep} or \emph{rollback} beside the setpoint ledger (\textbf{4}). No plant-specific code appears in any of the four surfaces: this is the integration claim of Sec.~III made operational.

\subsection{Static Audit and Dynamic Invariant Verification on a Live Instance (L1 Tier)}
\label{sec:static}
Before any dynamic campaign, the released audit harness (\texttt{bench/run.mjs}) verifies the artifact at source level. Three checks run offline: a \emph{capability inventory} against the claims of Sec.~III; a \emph{paper--code consistency} check that locates 20 representative quantitative constants cited in this paper (sweep quota 64, breach threshold 3, rollback-chain limit 2, RRF $K{=}60$, MMR $\lambda{=}0.7$, compaction 0.7, watchdog 300\,s, hysteresis 2\,\%, among others) at their implementation anchors; and a \emph{pipeline stage-ordering} check asserting that the governed-write stages of Algorithm~1 appear in the declared order at their source anchors. All pass: 20/20 anchors and 2/2 ordering chains---engineering discipline rather than validation evidence, since the harness audits the artifact of which it is part.

\label{sec:dynamic}
The static audit verifies structure; the rest of this subsection verifies behavior: L1-tier evidence (Table~\ref{tab:layers}), driving a pinned platform instance (v0.7.39, mock drivers) exclusively through its REST API with seeded randomness (mulberry32, seed 42). A benchmark line is created with global safety range $[120, 260]\,^\circ$C and a batch started with active recipe window $[175, 205]\,^\circ$C; the safety invariants of Sec.~\ref{sec:writepath} are then attacked and probed, including the decision boundaries and both branches of Eq.~\eqref{eq:constraint}.

\emph{I1 (containment).} Six seeded gross out-of-constraint writes---global-range violations (107, 269), window violations while the batch is active (219, 163), and extreme/negative values ($10^6$, $-25$)---are all rejected with typed validation errors, while all three in-window control writes are accepted: 100\,\% interception at 0\,\% false blocks. Boundary probes at $\pm$one least-count behave as specified (205.0 accepted; 205.1, 174.9 rejected), and the rejection message cites the window as the binding constraint. With the batch stopped the window branch deactivates and the hard range alone governs: an out-of-range write (300) remains rejected while a within-range, out-of-window write (130) is accepted---the soft interlock is a batch-scoped constraint, not a constant.

\emph{I2 (readback closure) and attribution.} All accepted writes return a driver readback within the tolerance of Eq.~\eqref{eq:tolerance} ($\tau = 0.7\,^\circ$C at one decimal digit and a 140$^\circ$C span) and produce journal anchors with source, actor, timestamp, and previous/new values---a 100\,\% attribution rate. Acquisition held the 1\,s cadence, and the samples API answered 20 probes at p50 6--22\,ms / p95 8--63\,ms.

\emph{Reproducibility.} Every execution on a pinned harness---the two identical-harness pipeline runs of Sec.~\ref{sec:repro} and the released ablation runs (three aggregated in Sec.~\ref{sec:e1a})---produced identical judge-class metrics: the same seeded attacks, rejections, boundary verdicts and ledger outcomes; only fixture identifiers and latency varied. \texttt{bench/compare.mjs} asserts judge-class equality between a fresh run's record and the archived baseline (\textsc{reproducible}; the gate rejects a tampered metric, validated by a self-test). The Wilson 95\,\% interval for 6/6 trials ([61\,\%, 100\,\%]) is the appropriate reading of any small-$n$ rate.

\emph{Scope.} These checks exercise the accepted-action language at the API boundary with the LLM out of the loop---deliberately so, since the invariants are defined over the pipeline, not over any engine (P4, Sec.~\ref{sec:writepath}).

\subsection{Real-Protocol Plant Scenarios (Five-Protocol PLC Node Simulator)}
\label{sec:plc}
The API-tier checks of Secs.~\ref{sec:dynamic} and \ref{sec:e1a} use in-process mock drivers; this subsection re-runs the working-condition scenarios over \emph{real protocol stacks} against an independent five-protocol PLC node simulator (Modbus TCP/RTU slaves, OPC UA server, MQTT broker/publisher, HTTP endpoints, ODE plant model). Driver configurations derive at runtime from the simulator's integration-export API---no hand-written connection settings---and every candidate driver passes the connection test before a node is created.

The pipeline provisions one production line per device that exposes a writable setpoint (4 lines: Modbus TCP, OPC UA, MQTT, HTTP) and attaches the acquisition-only device (a Modbus RTU gel counter) as a \emph{satellite} node on the first line, because acquisition samples only nodes whose line has an active batch. All five nodes stream live data through their real transports (2--14 one-second buckets in the settle window, Table~\ref{tab:plc}). A governed setpoint write costs 19--112\,ms (p50) by protocol, the Modbus TCP transaction dominating (its p95 reflects the first write's connection setup); readback closes within $0.04\,^\circ$C on Modbus TCP and exactly on OPC UA, while the MQTT and HTTP control channels report no readback by design (publish-only / echo-only actuators)---a driver property the write path records rather than conceals. Five seeded attacks are rejected on \emph{every} writable protocol (5/5 each; 20/20 pooled, Wilson 95\% interval [84\,\%, 100\,\%]) with zero false blocks on the four legal in-window writes, and the deterministic tool-level control loop (\texttt{daq\_query}$\to$\texttt{dcw\_control}$\to$\texttt{dcw\_judge}) converges on all four lines. A link-interruption drill (simulator service stopped and restarted) is detected and recovered, and a process-freeze injection shows defense in depth: the governed write still passes (readback verifies the register, not the process) while the independent recipe-window monitor catches the failure within 2\,s---the layer separation of Secs.~\ref{sec:dynamic} and \ref{sec:writepath}.

\subsection{Governance Surface: Recipe Lifecycle, Rollback, and Approval}
\label{sec:govsurface}
With the LLM out of the loop, every probe below ran end-to-end in one pipeline run and exercised the \emph{lifecycle objects} the governance stack manages. \emph{Non-destructive history}: a revert \emph{creates} version~3 rather than overwriting. \emph{Judge--execute separation}: judging \texttt{rollback} changed no PLC value ($196.60\,^\circ$C throughout)---only the separately invoked rollback restored the origin value ($203.40\,^\circ$C); an agent's verdict is an intent record, not an actuation. \emph{Bounded autonomy in action (backstop drill)}: freezing the process variable out of window under an open auto-binding record triggers the backstop---the record is judged \texttt{rollback} \emph{by the system} and the PLC restored to the baseline ($186.2\pm0.75\,^\circ$C), cadence-bound at 120--130\,s across releases; chain limit $K{=}2$ remains specified (Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}). \emph{Default non-execution}: in manual mode an agent write blocks until the approval releases it (148.2\,rpm on OPC UA in the quoted run; poll-inclusive decision latency ranged 6--468\,ms across released runs); the journal holds ten anchors over four sources and both log surfaces return full 200-entry windows.


\emph{Platform subsystems (P7/P9)}: vector and image frames from the profile and CCD channels are stored as first-class acquisition objects (P7); a mock lead dispatches a child task that a worker completes (parent \textsc{completed}); duplicate team-memory writes under one deduplication key yield one entry; the registry enumerates 14 engines, with environment-available counts of 12--14 across releases (one run's probe glitched to zero and is excluded).

\subsection{Closed-Loop Optimization Benchmark}
\label{sec:clbench}
The closed-loop benchmark quantifies how much of the offline optimum a \emph{governed} loop recovers on the cast-film twin. The pipeline instantiates all six actuators (zones on Modbus TCP, screw on OPC UA, line speed on MQTT, die gap on HTTP), starts a batch off-optimum ($N{=}150$\,rpm, $v{=}95$\,m/min, zone $200\,^\circ$C), and runs a controller that reads through the acquisition path and writes through the governed path. The objective
\begin{equation}
J = 55\,J_\text{thick} + 25\,J_\text{quality} + 8\,J_\text{energy} + 7\,J_\text{throughput}\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
constrains melt temperature to $[195, 225]\,^\circ$C and pressure to $\le 22\,MPa$.

The controller is model-light and fixed in advance: it inverts $h\propto N/v$ at constant line speed ($N\leftarrow N\cdot(50/h)$, $\pm$22\,rpm per step) and corrects the heater setpoint only when melt temperature leaves $[196,224]\,^\circ$C. It contains no LLM, so the benchmark isolates the \emph{governed loop} rather than a model. Convergence requires two consecutive evaluations with $h\in[48,52]\,\mu$m, defect $<2\,\%$, pressure $\le22$\,MPa.

Fig.~\ref{fig:clbench} reports the trajectories; $J_{\text{end}}$ is the mean of the two consecutive in-band evaluations. From $J_0=64.7$--$70.2$ the loop reaches $J_{\text{end}}=87.2$--$87.4$ against the offline grid optimum $W^{*}=89.894$ (weights unnormalized, 0--95 scale; real-time physics), i.e.\ 97.0--97.2\,\% of the optimum in the quoted run, converging in two iterations with at most two governed writes per seed, no rejected write and no constraint violation; mean wall time is 36\,s per seed. Thickness moves from 55.2--56.1\,$\mu$m (target $50\pm2\,\mu$m), defect settles at or below 0.64\,\%, pressure at 16.6--16.9\,MPa, and melt temperature stays inside $[195,225]\,^\circ$C. Per-seed values in the quoted run ($J_0 \to J_{\text{end}}$, ratio): seed 42: 68.07$\to$87.26 (0.971); seed 43: 70.21$\to$87.16 (0.970); seed 44: 64.69$\to$87.37 (0.972). Across all completed releases of the same command and seed (three early-development and two interrupted runs excluded), per-seed $J/J^{*}$ spans 0.962--0.973 and run-level means 0.965--0.971; the residual spread reflects the sampled process variables from which $J$ is computed. The 3--4\,\% shortfall is a throughput/energy trade, not a control failure: the grid optimum sits at a low-throughput corner where the objective's energy weight slightly exceeds its throughput weight, while the loop holds the higher line speed it started from.

\subsection{E1a: Pipeline-Tier Governance Ablation (Completed)}
\label{sec:e1a}
Using the env-gated bench-mode bypass (production builds refuse the flag), we compare the full pipeline against the three ablation arms of B1---soft interlock bypassed (\texttt{no-interlock}), readback verification bypassed (\texttt{no-readback}), and fully ungated---on the live instance (mock drivers, seed 42, three repetitions per arm per released run, fresh fixture per repetition). Because the hard range is enforced structurally at the driver layer and cannot be bypassed, the ablation isolates exactly the two bypassable mechanisms. Fig.~\ref{fig:e1a} reports the result; values were identical across repetitions. Three findings: (i) bypassing the interlock collapses interception from 6/6 to 4/6 (identically in the no-interlock and ungated arms)---the window-class attacks execute while global-range attacks remain structurally rejected; (ii) the executed breaches are fully journaled (twelve out-of-window anchors per bypass arm per run: six breach writes plus six accepted edge probes): \emph{attribution is not prevention}; (iii) no arm adds latency resolvable at this tier (p50 110--141\,ms, p95 150--169\,ms across all arms). Boundary behavior follows each arm's specification (9/9 probes; Wilson 95\,\% [70,100]\,\%), false blocks are 0/3 in every arm, and the no-readback arm matches Full on in-memory drivers (the mock readback echoes the command); its verification value is exercised by the frozen-process injection above.

\subsection{Case Study: LLM-Agent Closed-Loop Optimization (L4)}
\label{sec:case}
The benchmark of Sec.~\ref{sec:clbench} isolates the governed loop under a fixed controller; this subsection reports the complementary result---the same plant and objective closed by an LLM agent team. Four campaigns are released (first-party \texttt{omp} engine driving glm-5.3-flash in a lead-supervised team; manual-mode bindings; physics accelerated $6\times$; seed 42; plant and objective as in Sec.~\ref{sec:clbench}). Their sampling settings predate the temperature-0/model-pinning discipline of \bench{}'s released protocol (harness defaults), one reason we report them as an existence proof rather than a controlled measurement. In each campaign the team opened an optimization record, probed the plant with \texttt{daq\_query}, and actuated through \texttt{dcw\_control} over real protocol transports (Modbus TCP, OPC UA, MQTT, HTTP); the platform's write ledger records 7, 7, 15 and 10 governed writes respectively with per-write journal anchors held in the platform archive, and approval decisions recorded by the campaign's scripted operator emulation under the campaign operator identity---an autonomous-campaign configuration, not a production posture.

The outcome is unfavorable to the agent. The agent objective reached $J_{\text{agent}} = 67.7$, $31.2$, $68.3$, $68.5$ against $W^{*} = 89.894$---$0.75$, $0.35$, $0.76$, $0.76$ of the offline optimum, median $0.76$---and the thickness window $50\pm2\,\mu$m was \emph{not} reached: three campaigns settled at a $55.33\,\mu$m window mean and the fourth produced no in-window samples (its MQTT thickness channel also refused connection), while the melt-pressure node failed its connection test on OPC UA in all four. Of 39 acceptance checks per campaign, 33--35 passed.

We report this as a negative result with a design implication. It supplies the existence proof the framework needs---an LLM team drove real protocol transports through the governed path with every write reconstructable---while showing that on this objective the team does not match a two-step fixed controller ($0.76$ vs $0.97$ of $W^{*}$). That is the propose/dispose division of roles working as intended: the agent explores and proposes, and the deterministic path converts proposals into converged actuation.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:repro}
Re-running the whole pipeline twice with an identical harness (source hash \texttt{204fc9ee}; runs \texttt{20260914152838-fwg} and \texttt{20260914153400-ce0}), seed, and commit yields \emph{bit-identical} structural results: 55/55 pipeline checks pass in both runs; every seeded attack is rejected with zero false blocks on \emph{both} plant scenarios (9/9 on the second); all four per-line loops converge; the portability and backstop-drill outcomes are identical; and $W^{*}=89.894$. The one structural difference is benign: governed-write totals are 6 vs.\ 5 because seed 44 converged on its first write---``at most two governed writes per seed'' still holds. What varies is what the abstraction predicts: governed-write latency is environment-dependent, and the closed-loop objective---computed from \emph{sampled} process variables---lands in $J/J^{*}\in[0.962,0.973]$ across campaign repetitions. Every run appends to a never-overwritten timestamped directory; the archive keeps the accumulated run records across tiers, each with its seed, harness hash, and commit.

\emph{Threats to validity}. \emph{Construct}: success oracles are database- and readback-defined, mitigating self-reported success bias. \emph{Internal}: temperature-0, pinned-version LLM arms reduce but do not eliminate variance; hence mock-arm matrices with LLM spot-checks and nonparametric tests. \emph{External}: all measured layers are simulation-tier (two seeded plant scenarios, bounded by the ODE twin's fidelity); public-dataset replay (TEP, SWaT) is designed but unmeasured; the four LLM campaigns share one seed and carry no cross-seed variance estimate. Results are layer-labeled (Table~\ref{tab:layers}) and we claim no transfer beyond L1--L3--L4 without per-layer evidence. \emph{Conclusion}: the closed-loop result is reported over a fixed, pre-declared controller and three plant seeds, and the LLM campaign is reported as a measured negative rather than a headline.
