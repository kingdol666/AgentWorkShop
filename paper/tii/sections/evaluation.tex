\section{Benchmark Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Setup and Evidence Scope}
\label{sec:protocol}
The evaluation asks whether the governed write path enforces its declared boundaries, whether integration and lifecycle operations execute through the simulator stacks, whether a fixed controller can close the acquisition--actuation loop, and whether an agent team---including a real-LLM member---can execute that loop as the operator. These are separate questions, not a single benchmark score. We distinguish source inspection, mock-driver probes, real protocol stacks connected to simulated plants, and historical LLM campaigns. None measures physical-device performance. Table~\ref{tab:layers} identifies the evidence units; checks nested inside an integrated run are not additional independent trials.

The reference integrated archive, denoted $R$, is \texttt{20260914152838-fwg}: seed 42, commit \texttt{d6c824d}, harness hash prefix \texttt{204fc9ee}, Node v24.19.0 on Windows x64. It contains 55 passing checks, with no recorded warnings or failures. This is a within-run checklist result, not a replication count. An extended archive, $E$ = \texttt{20260917044223-17ho} (commit \texttt{f45b231}, same host, fresh fixture), adds an agent-team mission and a real-LLM loop to the integrated profile: 62 passing checks, no warnings or failures. Separate static and API records and the frozen PLC baseline support the other suites. The cast-film physics preset and the strategy-based film-line preset are simulated scenarios. TEP/SWaT replay, physical equipment, human-operator comparisons, and cross-engine effectiveness remain unmeasured.

Safety denominators are attempted attacks or designated legal writes, not log entries. Latency describes automatic execution and is separated from scripted approval waiting. Repeated samples within a seed or fixture do not establish population-level independence. We report counts and observed ranges without significance, equivalence, or reliability claims. The protocol's task and fault families are a coverage vocabulary: measured out-of-window writes do not establish coverage of every task, sensor-staleness condition, storage outage, or escalation branch.

Recommissioning the second preset provisions five devices without framework-code changes: three writable lines and two acquisition satellites. Its nine hard-range attacks (three per writable line) are rejected and three legal writes accepted. Unlike the first scenario, this probe set does not test recipe-window-only violations. The independent PLC baseline additionally records five-stack connectivity, a separate 5/5 attack rejection check with a legal write accepted, a process-freeze alarm after 2 s, and detection/recovery after simulator interruption. The freeze and interruption are individual drills, not fault tests repeated on all five protocols; process freeze is not a test of semantic-card sensor freshness.

Lifecycle probes retain distinct meanings. Two recipe sequences create a third version when reverting, preserve a known-good reference, restore a baseline, and apply a recipe. One judge--execute probe leaves the register at 196.60 after recording a rollback verdict; explicit execution subsequently restores approximately 203.40. A separate node rollback exercises the undo path. Manual binding suspends a write until scripted approval; the recorded 456 ms is polling-inclusive script latency, not human response time. Ten journal anchors and 200-entry audit/log query windows demonstrate accessible surfaces, not exhaustive attribution of unrelated history.

One system backstop drill records a rollback verdict after 120.105 s and a readback near the 186.2 baseline. This supports one rollback, not escalation at $K=2$. The implementation counts retained rolled-back records per node, not per agent or over a lifetime. Post-write record creation is conditional on the write outcome, source, and deduplication; bookkeeping exceptions are caught rather than undoing an accepted write. The run also stores five vector and five image frames, completes a mock lead/worker dispatch, and returns one memory entry after two writes with the same deduplication key. The registry lists 14 engines, 12 environment-available; enumeration is not cross-engine execution evidence.

\subsection{Deterministic Closed-Loop Benchmark}
\label{sec:clbench}
The fixed controller starts the cast-film twin at screw speed $N=150$ rpm, line speed $v=95$ m/min, and zone setpoint $200\,^\circ$C. It reads acquired process variables and submits governed writes; no LLM chooses the actions. Its objective is
\begin{equation}
J = 55\,J_\text{thick} + 25\,J_\text{quality} + 8\,J_\text{energy} + 7\,J_\text{throughput}.
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
Melt temperature must remain in $[195,225]\,^\circ$C and pressure at most 22 MPa. The controller uses $N\leftarrow N(50/h)$ with a 22-rpm step limit, holding line speed fixed, and adjusts heating when temperature leaves $[196,224]\,^\circ$C. The stated convergence criterion is two consecutive evaluations with thickness $h\in[48,52]\,\mu$m, defect below 2\%, and pressure at most 22 MPa. All three reference trajectories satisfy this criterion directly; we do not infer it solely from the stored convergence flag.

Figure~\ref{fig:clbench} shows seeds 42--44. Their starting objectives are 68.071, 70.206, and 64.694. Defining $J_{\rm end}$ as the mean of the final two evaluations gives 87.262, 87.157, and 87.366, respectively. Relative to the archived finite-grid reference $W^*=89.894$, the rounded ratios are 0.971, 0.970, and 0.972. The reference is a grid-search result, not a proven global optimum or universal performance bound. These ratios describe this objective and simulator, not industrial optimality.

Each seed uses two governed writes in $R$, with no rejected writes; mean elapsed time is 36.188 s per seed. Final thickness is 50.03, 49.73, and 50.58 $\mu$m. Measured temperatures and pressures satisfy the objective constraints. The two-write result is run-specific: later archive \texttt{18no} records four writes for seed 44. Timing-sensitive acquisition can change trajectories despite a deterministic policy. Three seeds therefore establish observed feasibility, not an iteration bound, controller superiority, or a general convergence theorem.

\subsection{Agent-Team Mission and Real-LLM Loop}
\label{sec:agentloop}
Archive $E$ places an agent, rather than the fixed controller, in the operator seat. The \emph{team mission} files an optimization goal on a channel task board: bring the Modbus-TCP setpoint node to $204.704\pm0.75\,^\circ$C within its recipe window, using at most three governed writes, reading recent data first. The lead dispatches the task; a bound worker reads the five-minute acquisition window through an explicit time-range query (\texttt{daq\_query} with \texttt{from/to/bucket}---time-series semantics), computes a setpoint at 78\% of the window, and issues one governed write. The register readback matches, the optimization record opens and is judged \texttt{keep}, the parameter journal attributes the change to the agent, and the board state machine reaches \texttt{COMPLETED}: six mission checks, all passing (Table~\ref{tab:agentloop}). The mission's policy is deterministic, so this unit measures the team path, not model capability.

The \emph{real-LLM loop} assigns the closed-loop task to an \texttt{omp}-harness agent (glm-5.3-flash) whose tool calls the model itself chooses under the platform's operating rules. Figure~\ref{fig:agenttrace} reconstructs the execution from the channel transcript. The first read identifies a startup ramp (44.7 to 199.3\,°C in 90\,s) rather than a fault; the model issues the governed write $197.48\,^\circ$C with an explicit hypothesis, the register returns 197.5\,°C, and an optimization record opens. A one-second-bucket re-query returns a stale window; the model switches to 15\,s buckets and a narrower interval, obtains the post-write series 199.41--200.10\,°C inside the recipe band, and judges the record \texttt{keep} citing those samples. The task terminates \texttt{COMPLETED} with the required sentinel in 132\,s, and every write crosses the same governed path and journal as the deterministic suites.

Both units demonstrate execution capability through the team and tool surfaces; neither demonstrates autonomous optimization quality or cross-model generality. The loop's steps are structured and graded by an oracle sentinel, in a single run with one model version. Unlike the campaigns of Sec.~\ref{sec:case}, it runs on a fresh fixture with live alarm context and verified readback, which is why its judge step rests on usable observations.

\subsection{Mock-Driver Governance Ablation}
\label{sec:e1a}
Figure~\ref{fig:e1a} uses only the complete frozen \texttt{20260914-baseline/}\allowbreak\texttt{run-e1lite-4arm} CSV and run record: seed 42, three fresh-fixture repetitions per arm. Only this complete three-repetition source set is used; two source runs of a historical nine-repetition aggregate are unavailable. Later runs are not substituted for them. The four arms retain both mechanisms, bypass the soft interlock, bypass readback, or bypass both; the ungated arm still retains the hard engineering range.

Full and no-readback reject 6/6 attacks in each repetition. No-interlock and ungated reject 4/6: the two window-only attacks execute while hard-range attacks remain rejected. Every arm has 0/3 designated legal false blocks and 3/3 arm-specific boundary outcomes per repetition. Accepted boundary probes in bypass arms are expected behavior under that specification, not safety successes. Journaling an executed breach supplies attribution but does not prevent it.

Each latency summary uses 23 successful writes (three designated legal plus twenty timed writes), excluding attacks and boundary probes. Across the three repetitions, p50 ranges are 125.4--140.9 ms (Full), 125.5--140.6 (no-interlock), 124.7--125.4 (no-readback), and 110.9--125.0 (ungated); p95 ranges are 158.2--158.9, 150.2--159.3, 156.4--156.7, and 155.5--156.4 ms. These descriptive ranges do not establish statistical equivalence. Mock readback echoes commands, so this ablation cannot quantify readback's value under device disagreement. Repeated seeded cases are not independent random attacks, and no binomial confidence interval is presented as deployment reliability.

\label{sec:case}

Sampling settings and model-version control are insufficient for a controlled comparison with the fixed controller. Three DAQ thickness windows contain 87 samples each, all fixed at 55.33 $\mu$m; the fourth has no thickness window and an MQTT connection refusal. All four pressure-node connection tests fail. Yet the last simulator-truth thickness sample is in-window in every campaign. This observation disagreement prevents the DAQ failure from establishing physical non-convergence or LLM inferiority. Conversely, isolated truth endpoints do not prove two consecutive valid evaluations or justify retrospective replacement of the original scoring window. The fresh-fixture loop of Sec.~\ref{sec:agentloop} repairs the observation path on a live line; it does not rehabilitate these historical scores.

The historical scoring script substitutes zero for missing thickness and fixes melt temperature at 210 rather than measuring it. The fixed-controller evaluator instead uses acquired temperature and leaves scores null when required observations are missing or constraints fail. Thus the old outputs in the table are audit artifacts, not valid comparable objective estimates; the missing-thickness main score is N/A, not a 0.35 ratio. We neither aggregate these ratios nor contrast their median with the deterministic result. All four inspector checks fail. The regular expression also captures the prompt's literal \texttt{CONVERGED} template, so a matched string is not independent convergence evidence.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Limitations}
\label{sec:repro}
Archive provenance is necessary but insufficient for reproducibility. The putative replication partner \texttt{20260914153400-ce0} is unavailable, so bit-identical $R/R'$ replication cannot be established. Moreover, \texttt{compare.mjs} reads \texttt{results}, whereas integrated records store \texttt{checks}. Two such records can produce empty comparison maps and a \texttt{REPRODUCIBLE} verdict without checking their outcomes. This does not invalidate the 55 recorded checks in $R$, but it invalidates that comparator verdict as proof of integrated repeatability.

Other historical outcomes remain visible rather than being silently excluded. The API-only \texttt{co} record skips four PLC checks; \texttt{pds} records fetch failures in the closed-loop and interruption checks; integrated \texttt{18no} warns on the MQTT tool loop. Later \texttt{xac}, \texttt{15ns}, \texttt{51c}, and \texttt{vzo} contain passing checks within their respective scopes, not erasure of earlier failures. Static, mock, PLC, integrated, ablation, and LLM evidence therefore retain separate denominators.

The archived command \texttt{node bench/pipeline.mjs --profile integrated --seed 42 --cl-seeds 3} identifies the integrated profile, not a one-command reproduction of this entire chapter. The companion evidence ledger records source locations, hashes, qualifications, and withdrawals; the figure-data JSON preserves the selected raw-source provenance. Fresh cold-boot executions of the released card (2026-09-17) reproduce the deterministic lanes: static 20/20 anchors, integrated profile 61/61 checks, PLC 13/13, the four-arm ablation pattern, and a 60-probe API survey; the PLC and ablation lanes compare \texttt{REPRODUCIBLE} against the frozen baseline with non-empty per-check tables, and archive $E$ reruns the same command with \texttt{--agent omp}. The integrated-record comparator gap noted above persists, so cross-run integrated replication still has no valid machine verdict. Simulation fidelity, shared-environment timing, small fixture sets, and the historical LLM observation failures limit interpretation. The evidence supports exercised governance and integration behavior, not physical safety certification, general fault tolerance, or comparative agent intelligence.
