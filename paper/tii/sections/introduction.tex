\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{L}{arge} language models (LLMs) are being explored for industrial diagnosis, engineering assistance, and control-program generation \cite{raza2025industrial,sabetta2025assistant,liu2026agents4plc,fakih2024llm4plc}. Recent systems also connect agents to simulated controllers or real machines \cite{vyas2026ftc,hofmann2025nlc}. For a production-line deployment, the engineering problem is not whether an LLM can issue a command but how to preserve its meaning, authority, and history across heterogeneous equipment, multiple agents, and changing operating recipes.

Consider a team correcting film thickness within pressure and temperature limits: one member inspects telemetry, another proposes a setpoint, and a lead coordinates the task. A protocol connection alone does not identify which member may change which node, which recipe applies to the current batch, whether a human must approve, or whether the subsequent observation measures the commanded register or the process response. Implementing these associations separately for each application duplicates configuration and makes failures hard to reconstruct; conversely, one shared interface does not by itself establish atomic execution, physical safety, or valid optimization. Fig.~\ref{fig:intro} contrasts the two patterns: advisory, application-specific integration on one side, and explicit binding over shared production context on the other, with readback and attribution attached to every governed write.

We present \system{}, a configurable framework for this supervisory integration problem: an industrial node combines its acquisition or setpoint interface with engineering semantics, and an explicit binding grants a deployed channel member access to that node; a line/product/recipe/run model supplies the production context shared by observations and actions. The platform core hosts agent orchestration and industrial supervision together, while engine subprocesses, optional external stores, and field endpoints retain their own boundaries. This organization reduces application-specific routing within the supported driver set without equating shared state with an end-to-end transaction.

The agent loop operates at supervisory, seconds-to-minutes timescales. Manual bindings require an approval decision; automatic bindings omit that wait but retain the applicable execution checks; write attribution and separately triggered compensation make a failed proposal, a failed write, and an unsuccessful adjustment inspectable as different events.

The contributions are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A \textbf{node-native integration model} connecting heterogeneous acquisition and control nodes, deployed agent teams, and versioned production runs through explicit capabilities and shared identifiers (Sec.~\ref{sec:arch}).
\item An \textbf{implemented supervisory workflow} that separates team coordination, source-dependent write checks, agent-submitted judgments, physical compensation, and human decisions; its seven mechanisms and the cross-cutting human-in-the-loop (HITL) boundary are described with their actual scopes and exceptions (Sec.~\ref{sec:mech}).
\item A \textbf{traceable, layered evaluation} anchored on one reference archive detailed in Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol} and an eleven-archive rerun family sharing its seed (Sec.~\ref{sec:agentloop}). All 75 suite checks pass, and the writable stacks reject every predefined
out-of-constraint probe while blocking none of the designated legal writes. A
fixed non-LLM controller reaches $J/J^*=0.963$--$0.972$ of an offline reference over three seeds, and a scripted team mission brings a simulated film line from 28.10 to 25.68\,$\mu$m with three governed writes on two
protocols; seven of the ten reportable runs attain the objective band (six
of nine excluding the reference). All evidence is
simulation-tier and characterizes the platform paths, not agent quality.
\end{enumerate}
These contributions correspond to the evaluation's evidence layers
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval}, Table~\ref{tab:layers}), each layer
reported separately rather than pooled into one score. The paper specifies an
integration model, implements it end
to end, and reports what that implementation does and does not guarantee; it
proposes no new control law, learning method, or safety certification argument, and
claims configuration reuse within the existing driver and scenario vocabulary, not certified
safety, general industrial autonomy, or LLM-controller superiority.
