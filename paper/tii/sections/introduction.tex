\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{S}{upervisory} process adjustment seeks to keep product quality
within its target band while production continues. On a film line, screw speed,
line speed, and zone temperatures influence thickness; on a biaxially oriented
line, draw ratio and stretch temperature couple several sections. Each
adjustment must respect equipment limits and the active recipe window. The
engineering problem is therefore not only choosing a setpoint, but linking its
authority, operating context, and observed effect throughout an iterative
search.

Language-model agents are being explored for industrial diagnosis, engineering
assistance, and PLC program generation
\cite{raza2025industrial,sabetta2025assistant,liu2026agents4plc,fakih2024llm4plc},
including interaction with simulated controllers and machine demonstrators
\cite{vyas2026ftc,hofmann2025nlc}. Such agents can propose and coordinate
interventions, but protocol connectivity alone specifies neither which team
member may write a parameter nor which limits and approval rules apply. A
supervisory loop must also distinguish an authorized proposal, a completed
write, and a beneficial process response.

\system{} addresses this integration problem through explicit member--node
bindings and shared line/product/recipe/run identifiers. Nodes expose
engineering quantities rather than register addresses; bindings grant deployed
members scoped tool authority; and hypothesis records link changes to
subsequent observations and judgments. Fig.~\ref{fig:intro} contrasts this
organization with application-specific integration. Shared context supports
attribution but does not establish atomic execution, physical safety, or
optimization success.

The loop operates at seconds-to-minutes supervisory timescales; fast
regulation and plant protection remain outside it. Manual bindings require
approval, while automatic bindings retain the applicable execution checks
without that wait. Judgment and compensation are separate operations,
preserving the distinction between a rejected proposal, a driver-accepted
write (readback-validated where supported), an ineffective adjustment, and an
attempted restoration.

The contributions are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A \textbf{node-native supervisory model} connecting heterogeneous
acquisition and control nodes, deployed team members, and production runs
through explicit capabilities and shared identifiers. Recipe windows constrain
the search; they are not inferred safe operating regions
(Secs.~\ref{sec:arch} and \ref{sec:objective}).
\item An \textbf{implemented governed loop} linking source-dependent admission,
intervention records with optional hypotheses, agent-submitted judgments,
separately executed compensation, and human approval, with explicit execution
boundaries (Sec.~\ref{sec:mech}).
\item A \textbf{layered, traceable evaluation} separating protocol checks,
fixed-controller performance, and scripted team missions
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval}). The reported decision policies are deterministic and
the plants simulated; these tests characterize platform paths, not LLM
reasoning or controller superiority.
\end{enumerate}
The contribution is an execution and evidence framework for supervisory search,
not a new control law, learning method, or safety certification. Configuration
reuse is evaluated only within the implemented drivers and scenarios.
