\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{L}{arge} language models (LLMs) are being explored for industrial diagnosis, engineering assistance, and control-program generation \cite{raza2025industrial,sabetta2025assistant,liu2026agents4plc,fakih2024llm4plc}. Recent systems also connect agents to simulated controllers or real machines \cite{vyas2026ftc,hofmann2025nlc}. For a production-line deployment, the engineering problem is therefore not whether an LLM can issue a command but how to preserve its meaning, authority, and history across heterogeneous equipment, multiple agents, and changing operating recipes.

Consider a team asked to correct film thickness within pressure and temperature limits: one member may inspect telemetry, another propose a setpoint, and a lead coordinate the task. A protocol connection alone does not identify which member may change which node, which recipe applies to the current batch, whether a human must approve, or whether the subsequent observation measures the commanded register or the process response. Implementing these associations separately for each application introduces duplicated configuration and makes failures difficult to reconstruct; conversely, one shared interface does not by itself establish atomic execution, physical safety, or valid optimization. Fig.~\ref{fig:intro} contrasts this application-specific pattern with the binding and production context used here.

We present \system{}, a configurable framework for this supervisory integration problem: an industrial node combines its acquisition or setpoint interface with engineering semantics, and an explicit binding grants a deployed channel member access to that node; a line/product/recipe/run model supplies the production context shared by observations and actions. The platform core hosts agent orchestration and industrial supervision together, while engine subprocesses, optional external stores, and field endpoints retain their own boundaries. This organization reduces application-specific routing within the supported driver set without equating shared state with an end-to-end transaction.

The agent loop operates at supervisory, seconds-to-minutes timescales; regulatory control and plant-side protective functions remain in PLCs. Within the platform, proposal, admission, device execution, and outcome assessment are distinct operations. Manual bindings require an approval decision; automatic bindings omit that wait but retain the applicable execution checks; write attribution and separately triggered compensation make a failed proposal, a failed write, and an unsuccessful adjustment inspectable as different events.

The contributions are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A \textbf{node-native integration model} connecting heterogeneous acquisition and control nodes, deployed agent teams, and versioned production runs through explicit capabilities and shared identifiers (Sec.~\ref{sec:arch}).
\item An \textbf{implemented supervisory workflow} that separates team coordination, source-dependent write checks, agent-submitted judgments, physical compensation, and human decisions; its six mechanisms and the cross-cutting HITL boundary are described with their actual scopes and exceptions (Sec.~\ref{sec:mech}).
\item A \textbf{traceable, layered evaluation} organized around the latest
machine-readable integrated archive. The run passes 75/75 checks with no
warnings or failures; its four writable protocol fixtures reject 24/24
predefined out-of-constraint probes with 0/4 false blocks, and an independent
film-line scenario rejects 9/9 hard-range probes with 0/3 false blocks. Three
fixed-controller seeds reach $J/J^*=0.968$--0.972, while a deployed AgentTeam
mission reduces biaxial-film thickness from 28.10 to 25.68\,$\mu$m with three
governed writes on three actuator families (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval}).
\end{enumerate}
These contributions correspond to the evaluation's evidence units
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval}, Table~\ref{tab:layers}): the integration model is
exercised by the multi-stack and lifecycle suites, the mechanisms by the
governance and lifecycle probes, and the assembled workflow by the
fixed-controller and AgentTeam missions. Each unit is reported separately
rather than pooled into a single score.
The central claim is reusable supervisory integration within these tested settings, not certified safety, general industrial autonomy, or LLM-controller superiority.
