\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Industrial Agents and Closed-Loop Applications}
Industrial LLM applications span industrial knowledge-graph construction \cite{zhang2026comaikg} and diagnosis \cite{liu2024kgdiag}, PLC program generation and verification \cite{liu2026agents4plc,fakih2024llm4plc,ren2025metaindux}, and digital-twin configuration \cite{xia2024llmsim,gill2025llmdt}. These uses differ from supervisory setpoint execution, but it would be inaccurate to characterize all industrial agents as advisory: natural-language interfaces have controlled machines over OPC UA \cite{hofmann2025nlc}, and LLM agents have been studied in simulated fault-tolerant control \cite{vyas2026ftc}. InstructMPC instead uses language-derived context for disturbance prediction in model-predictive control \cite{wu2025instructmpc}. Our focus is the reusable association of team members, heterogeneous nodes, production context, and execution records, rather than a new control law or a claim that agents have not previously actuated equipment.

\subsection{Orchestration, Industrial Models, and Execution Boundaries}
General-purpose frameworks such as AutoGen, LangGraph, and CrewAI organize agent tasks and tool use \cite{wu2023autogen,langgraph2024,crewai2024}; industrial integration additionally requires node semantics, driver behavior, and operating context. We use a line/product/recipe/run organization informed by ISA-95 and ISA-88 \cite{isa95,isa88}, without implementing their complete procedural models. A node semantic card supplies the engineering quantities needed at the tool boundary, adjacent in purpose to standardized asset descriptions \cite{iec63278}. Hard action shielding and control-barrier methods enforce formally specified admissible actions \cite{alshiekh2018shielding,ames2017cbf}. The mechanisms here are narrower implementation-level checks and approval policies; they are not substituted for those methods or for certified plant protection. Human supervision also requires a clear division of responsibility, not merely an approval dialog \cite{parasuraman2000model,bainbridge1983ironies}.

\subsection{Evaluation of Tools and Industrial Control}
AgentBench and consequential tool-use benchmarks evaluate agent behavior and policy compliance \cite{liu2024agentbench,yao2024taubench,xu2024company}. Industrial datasets such as TEP and SWaT support process-monitoring research \cite{downs1993tep,goh2017swat}, but replay is not evidence of successful physical actuation. We therefore separate source checks, mock-driver probes, software-protocol integration, fixed-controller optimization, and exploratory LLM runs. No external-framework ranking, dataset-replay result, or physical-device validation is claimed. This separation permits a framework mechanism to be evaluated without attributing a scripted controller's performance to an LLM.
