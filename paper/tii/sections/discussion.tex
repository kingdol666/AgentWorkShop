\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\subsection{What the Integration Model Establishes}
The useful unit of reuse is the deployed member--node
capability--production-run association: it lets one task use engineering
quantities across drivers while execution and compensation stay attributable.
The latest run provisions four writable protocol lines, one satellite, and a
separate film-line scenario, and exposes a 49-signal biaxial-film line. These
results support configuration reuse within the existing driver and scenario
vocabulary; they do not measure the effort to author a scenario, validate its
windows, or integrate a new protocol.

Separating proposal, admission, and disposition prevents several misleading success criteria: a recorded \texttt{keep} is the agent's judgment, a matching readback is a driver-level observation, and sustained process convergence is a separate experimental outcome. The LLM case studies further show that observability and scoring must be checked before decision quality is compared.

The framework's mechanism density is also unevenly placed. The governed-write
path concentrates range and recipe-window checks, ownership, cooldown, and
readback. The latest deterministic run exercises these mechanisms cleanly. The
historical real-LLM campaigns instead exposed observation and scoring failures:
writes executed as designed, but some judgments used stale or constant windows.
Observation-quality gating, including freshness service levels and rejection of
stale or constant windows before judging, remains a framework-level extension.

\subsection{Implementation and Evidence Boundaries}
Asynchronous device I/O is not atomic with concurrent recipe changes, and multi-node recovery can partly fail. Cross-agent in-window oscillations, shared-node contention over longer horizons, and availability during storage failure require further evaluation; batch-gated acquisition leaves a monitoring gap between runs.

Approval reduces permitted automation but does not establish endpoint security, protect against a privileged compromised harness, or validate approved engineering intent; least-privilege deployment and network separation remain separate responsibilities \cite{iec62443,nist80082}. Governed writes are defense-in-depth at the basic process control system layer, not a certified safety instrumented function: their safety argument presupposes independent regulatory limits and an independent protection layer in the IEC 61511 sense \cite{iec61511}, and the platform implements no detection or degraded-mode response when such layers are absent or bypassed---an integrator and operator responsibility. Manual-binding approval workload grows with the frequency of agent proposals, and approval fatigue is a documented risk \cite{parasuraman2000model,bainbridge1983ironies,parasuramanmanzey2010complacency}; policy granularity over roles, batch phases, and magnitudes is a design extension point, not a present capability. The present evidence includes no physical hardware campaign, operator workload study, cross-engine comparison, or statistical equivalence test; Sec.~\ref{sec:repro}'s archive and comparator boundaries bound replication claims. Repairing these evidence paths, aligning observations with simulator truth, and testing concurrent configuration changes are prerequisites to stronger claims.

Deployment in a real plant carries prerequisites this paper does not discharge: authoring and validating semantic cards and recipe windows is operator-side engineering under management of change, and its effort is unmeasured. Placement is an integration decision: where the core and its stores sit across the IT/OT hierarchy, and how IEC 62443 zones and conduits are assigned, is the integrator's \cite{iec62443}. A deployment design must also answer the platform's failure modes---approval channel, alarm paths, open records after a core crash---and pin engine model versions where regulated industries require reproducible behavior. The single-host Windows capacity boundary is likewise untested.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\system{} integrates multi-agent coordination with heterogeneous industrial
supervision through explicit node capabilities and shared production context.
The architecture explains how connection, team deployment, acquisition,
source-dependent execution checks, recipe operations, and human decisions
cooperate without becoming one atomic control function. The latest run
provides three complementary results: four protocol fixtures reject all
predefined out-of-constraint writes with no legal-write blocks; a fixed
controller converges in all three seeds within 0.968--0.972 of the archived
grid reference; and an AgentTeam mission uses three governed writes on three
actuator families to bring biaxial-film thickness from 28.10 to
25.68\,$\mu$m. These results demonstrate the tested integration and governance
paths, not physical safety, model superiority, or general plant performance.
Physical validation, stronger execution consistency, controlled agent
comparisons, and observation-quality gating remain open. Platform and benchmark
sources use the repository's license; the simulator is maintained separately.
