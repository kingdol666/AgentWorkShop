\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\subsection{What the Evidence Establishes}
The main result is a traceable supervisory path from member authority to
multi-node intervention and observed response. Primary archive $B$ links
protocol-level constraint checks to a scripted team mission that reaches its
thickness band. The interlock ablation identifies the mechanism responsible
for rejecting the tested window violations, while the frozen-process drill
shows why command acceptance and process response need separate checks.
Together, these results support an inspectable execution layer across the
implemented drivers, rather than relying on task completion as evidence of
successful control.

This separation guides diagnosis. A binding or range rejection calls for
revising authority or the proposal; a transport or readback failure calls for
inspecting execution; an accepted write with an adverse response calls for a
new strategy or compensation. A \texttt{keep} records the caller's verdict,
not independent confirmation of process success. The scripted mission thus
demonstrates coordinated execution toward an objective, while the
fixed-controller trials isolate loop closure under a known model. Neither
measures LLM decision quality or comparative optimization performance.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:repro}
The archives retain commands, seeds, source fingerprints, and per-check
evidence, enabling reruns and comparisons. Reconstructing a run also requires
the separately versioned simulator and its reset state: initial conditions,
asynchronous execution, and sampling windows can change outcomes despite
deterministic policies. Harness fingerprints do not pin that simulator, and
working-tree hashes can depend on line endings.

The tests and performance reference are internal to the study. Checks were
developed against this implementation, and $J^{*}$ derives from the same
simulator used by the controller. Historical warning-only mission outcomes
show the limits of aggregate regression gates. The current archive also has a
journal check with an empty evidence array
(Sec.~\ref{sec:repeatability}); retained observation aggregates omit raw
buckets and timestamps. Observation-quality gating is not enforced, so stale
or unrepresentative telemetry can affect judgments. Optimization records lack
a run identifier, and their time-based queries can span run boundaries.
Historical real-LLM campaigns used different scoring inputs and observation
windows and are not comparable optimization evidence.

External validity remains limited to the simulated scenarios: no
physical-device validation, TEP or SWaT replay, human-operator study, or
external-framework comparison is reported. Configuration reuse within the
implemented drivers does not establish transfer to other equipment or
production regimes.

\subsection{Deployment Boundaries}
Execution and recovery are not atomic. Bookkeeping may fail after actuation;
readback agreement does not establish process recovery, and multi-node
compensation can partially succeed. Cross-agent oscillation within permitted
ranges, long-horizon contention, storage-failure availability, and worst-case
recovery time remain unevaluated. The backstop's sampling and recheck cadence
is not a recovery deadline. At its retained-history threshold $K=2$,
escalation replaces automatic restoration and can remain unresolved without
an operator.

Bindings govern sanctioned tools, not arbitrary I/O by a privileged or
compromised engine. Deployment requires least privilege, endpoint security,
and IT/OT separation \cite{iec62443-3-3,nist80082}. Controller interlocks and
required IEC~61511 protection must remain independent: governed writes are
neither a certified safety function nor a substitute for plant protection
\cite{iec61511}.

Manual approval introduces risks of fatigue and over-reliance
\cite{parasuraman2000model,bainbridge1983ironies,leesee2004trust,parasuramanmanzey2010complacency}.
The present decision endpoint lacks line- or owner-scoped approver
authorization and treats an omitted decision flag as approval. Fail-closed
decision validation, scoped approver roles, protection of recipe-window edits,
and enforced segregation of duties are therefore deployment prerequisites,
not demonstrated guarantees. Operational validation must also address
approval-channel and alarm-path failures \cite{eemua191}, crash-orphaned
records, synchronized clocks, and off-host evidence.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\system{} connects agent teams to industrial supervision through member--node
capabilities, production context, and intervention records. Its governed loop
keeps authorization, device acceptance, submitted judgment, and compensation
distinct. Protocol checks, the interlock ablation, and scripted simulated
missions establish how these mechanisms support traceable supervisory search.
Physical validation, stronger execution consistency, observation-quality
gating, and controlled agent comparisons are the next steps.
