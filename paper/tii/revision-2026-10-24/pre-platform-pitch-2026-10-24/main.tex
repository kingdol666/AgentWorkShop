\title{A Governed Supervisory Execution Layer for LLM-Agent Plant Writes}

\author{Anonymous Authors}
\markboth{IEEE Transactions on Industrial Informatics, Submitted Manuscript}{Governed Execution for LLM-Agent Plant Writes}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Industrial large language model agents require a traceable connection
between task-level proposals, commanded controls, and process observations.
This article presents a governed supervisory execution framework that
connects these stages beneath a replaceable strategy layer. Semantic
acquisition and control nodes connect agent identity to
line--product--recipe--run context. Server-side channel profiles separate
observation, model training, bounded exploration, and operational recipe
work; capabilities and source-specific admission resolve execution
authority. Required approvals govern actuation, and graded receipts
distinguish transport acceptance from endpoint confirmation. Optimization
records, batch receipts, and recipe versions retain the evidence appropriate
to each lifecycle. Evaluation combines protocol and process missions,
admission ablation, controlled confirmation challenges, nine regression
executions totaling 782 checks, and a recipe-based injection-molding
trajectory. Removing soft-window admission allowed six otherwise refused
probes to execute, whereas removing readback left admission unchanged.
A publish-only receipt retained its unverified grade despite successful
transport acceptance. In one sequential recipe experiment, four approved
pressure steps from 45 to 65 bar accompanied a weight change from
31.28 to 32.30 g with a fixed 6-s hold time, followed by retention of the
accepted recipe. All process experiments used repository simulators;
approvals were scripted. The results demonstrate inspectable execution
evidence on the exercised routes, linking task intent, authorization,
control history, observed quality, and retained production parameters.
\end{abstract}
