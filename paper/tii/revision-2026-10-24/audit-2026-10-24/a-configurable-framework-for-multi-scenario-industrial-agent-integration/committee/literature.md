## Literature Dialogue Review

### Gap Derivation Audit
- Claimed gap (quote + location):
  - `sections/related.tex:38–47`: “The selected systems in Table~\ref{tab:nearest} report complementary integration settings: reviewed control sequences, module-scoped skills, and scheduling. Their reported evaluations do not address the combined configuration of agent harnesses, production context, semantic process I/O, and distinct node/recipe workflows considered here.”
- Why the gap is (not) logically established:
  - The claim is explicitly bounded to the selected studies, and the table gives a concrete, source-scoped description of three different integration settings. The next sentence—“This gap statement is limited to the selected studies, not the wider literature” (`sections/related.tex:42–43`)—avoids an unsupported field-wide novelty claim. On that bounded reading, the gap follows from the comparison and is not a straw man.
  - The remaining weakness is the selection boundary: the subsection cites a wider set of industrial-agent work, including agent-based supervision, manufacturing resource-capability exploration, shop-floor multi-agent systems, and digital-twin fault handling (`sections/related.tex:4–6`; cited keys include `biskupovic2025supervision`, `lim2025llmenabled`, `zhao2026shopfloor`, and `gill2025llmdt`), but the table neither explains why MADE, Köhle et al., and Waseem are the closest comparison set nor positions those other cited works against the stated combination. The table therefore establishes a gap against its three rows, not against the wider body of relevant work already cited in this paper. This is a scope/evidence limitation, not evidence that the authors mischaracterize those works.

### Pseudo-Innovation / Straw-Man Signals
- No direct straw-man or exaggerated “first/no prior work” claim is present in the reviewed text. The qualification “limited to the selected studies” (`sections/related.tex:42–43`) is appropriately cautious, and the paragraph also says the framework “does not replace process protection or establish operator effectiveness” (`sections/related.tex:46–47`).
- The opening sentence is more citation inventory than synthesis: “Industrial-agent research spans natural-language control, PLC programming, factory skills, shop-floor coordination, digital-twin support, and production scheduling” (`sections/related.tex:4–6`) lists six areas and cites eight works without indicating which works substantiate which themes or what integration distinction the reader should take from them. The following paragraph narrows to three examples, but does not bridge the broader map to that selection. This weakens the literature dialogue, though it does not itself make a false claim.

### Fix Plan (3 concrete edits)
1. Add one sentence to the first subsection that groups the cited studies by the integration problem they address (e.g., control/PLC, resource or shop-floor coordination, and digital-twin support), rather than leaving the six-topic list and eight-citation cluster unmapped.
2. State the criterion for selecting MADE, Köhle et al., and Waseem in Table~\ref{tab:nearest}; then add a concise positioning sentence for the already-cited supervision, resource-exploration, shop-floor, and digital-twin studies, or explain why they do not test the same framework-level combination. Keep the gap explicitly scoped to the evidence actually compared.
3. Preserve the table’s source-specific evidence caveat, and label the final column as source-specific evaluation evidence (or otherwise make clear that task counts, state cross-verification, throughput, and the proposed framework’s configuration evidence are different evidence types rather than directly comparable performance measures).
