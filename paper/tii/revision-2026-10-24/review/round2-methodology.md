## Overall verdict

**Major revision / hold pending artifact reconciliation.** The manuscript-source ZIP presents a careful, simulator-backed implementation study whose main claims are generally proportionate to its evidence. The separately delivered top-level PDF is a different manuscript, however, so the object to be reviewed is ambiguous. Reconcile the PDF and source package before treating this revision as a coherent submission.

For the manuscript in the source ZIP, the evidence supports tested integration and route behavior in the archived simulator and software-test settings. It does not support physical-plant transfer, process-performance improvement attributable to the framework, statistical reliability, human-approval effectiveness, or comparative optimization benefit. The manuscript states these boundaries in most of the right places.

## Strengths

- The experimental section defines a run or sequential trajectory as the evidence unit and explicitly says that writes within one trajectory are not independent trials (`sections/evaluation.tex:43–52`). Q1–Q6 archives are identified as complementary rather than pooled into a single current-version demonstration (`sections/evaluation.tex:3–12, 17–30`).
- Sample sizes and denominators are unusually clear for a systems paper: Q4 is one seed-42 trajectory for each of four simulators (`sections/evaluation.tex:64–69`); Q5 is 782 deterministic assertions over nine sequential suite executions, not plant samples (`sections/evaluation.tex:202–228`); Q6 is one sequential trajectory with four pressure steps, scripted approvals, and a pacing override (`sections/evaluation.tex:269–305, 315–324`).
- The protocol results distinguish five acquisition families from four writable routes, identify RTU-over-TCP as acquisition-only, and disclose six latency writes per writable line plus the upper-median/observed-maximum interpretation (`sections/evaluation.tex:54–62, 115–123`; `REPRODUCIBILITY.md:70–78`). The mock-driver ablation gives its denominator and separates the 18 attack probes per arm from other probes (`sections/evaluation.tex:125–158`).
- Q4 reports near-boundary outcomes, WWTP reversions and pH movement, and the annealing step without an optimization-record ID rather than presenting only successful endpoints (`sections/evaluation.tex:71–97, 102–111`). Q3 defines the objective and expressly limits its reference to a finite grid, not a global optimum (`sections/evaluation.tex:179–200`; `REPRODUCIBILITY.md:80–109`).
- The source-ZIP manuscript discloses that Q1–Q4 use a historical direct-node interface, that Q6 bypassed the default cadence, and that the archives are not a joint current-version trial (`sections/evaluation.tex:17–30, 288–295, 339–345`; `sections/discussion.tex:26–42`). It also separates endpoint confirmation from process attainment and reports missing melt temperature and the freshness-check limitation (`sections/evaluation.tex:328–331`).
- The current TII requirements reference calls for an original technical contribution tied to industrial application; it does not specify a minimum replication count or a required inferential test (`requirements/IEEE-TII-REQUIREMENTS.md:9–15, 34`). The source-ZIP manuscript is framed as an industrial integration framework and limits its results to exercised simulator configurations. Its low replication is therefore a limitation on inference, not by itself evidence of a rules violation.

The source ZIP’s `main.tex:63` instead titles the manuscript **“A Configurable Framework for Multi-Scenario Industrial Agent Integration”** and gives a materially different abstract at `main.tex:70–99`. The ZIP contains its own `main.pdf`, whose SHA-256 is `1589858f4766f8dd656b13471c8d2f15be889bb54434d07878b6a9d80b4b0b81`; the top-level PDF SHA-256 is `51118C0E71C486665117CD2CEB2E6622C57995A443B3C8A59CAB5D60DC659028`. This is a review-identity problem, not a conclusion about either version’s data. The top-level PDF’s p. 1 abstract and framing do not match the final platform-centered source and evidence map, making it unclear which claim set the reviewer should assess. **Action:** choose the intended manuscript, regenerate the top-level PDF from that exact source, and update the delivery manifest to include the top-level PDF hash and page count. Recheck that the selected PDF and source package carry the same title, abstract, evidence-unit statements, and limitations.

### Moderate — Put the failed R12 restoration in the Q6 boundary summary

The evaluation’s October 23 boundary paragraph reports the refused trials, missed target, and absent recipe update (`sections/evaluation.tex:339–345`), but does not mention that the restoration assertion failed. The discussion does report the failure (`sections/discussion.tex:32–36`), and the reproducibility notes give the decisive result: pressure remained at 50 bar instead of the requested 45-bar baseline; four of eight harness assertions passed (`REPRODUCIBILITY.md:177–186`).

Because this is the current-route result most relevant to recovery after a partially executed recipe workflow, keep it beside the R12 result, not only in the later scope paragraph. **Action:** add the retained pressure and failed-restoration outcome to the Q6 boundary summary in the evaluation, while preserving the distinction between this cadence-boundary check and an independent repeat of R6. ## Reproducibility and claim boundaries

The evidence package supports offline inspection and integrity verification, and includes derived figure data; it does not provide a complete historical runtime for exact end-to-end reruns. The source notes state that R1’s recorded commit covers nine harness files while the run fingerprints eleven, that the simulator dirty-tree state was not archived, and that R6 has no exact commit, seed, or per-sensor timestamp series (`REPRODUCIBILITY.md:29–40`; `DEPENDENCIES-AND-REPLAY.md:19–31`). Rerunning the historical routes requires a compatible full checkout, simulator, configuration, and in some cases engine-provider setup (`DEPENDENCIES-AND-REPLAY.md:33–68`). These limits are candidly disclosed and do not invalidate the archived observations, but the results should be described as archive-backed implementation evidence, not as independently reproducible repetitions.

The present evidence supports tested configuration coverage, route-specific implementation behavior, and the recorded one-run process outcomes. It cannot support general plant coverage, physical effectiveness or safety, human oversight benefit, fault frequencies, hard real-time behavior, a current integrated end-to-end trial, or comparative control/optimization benefit. This claim boundary is consistent with the manuscript’s Engineering Interpretation (`sections/discussion.tex:26–42`) and the evidence map (`PLATFORM-PITCH-EVIDENCE-MAP.md:29–44`).
