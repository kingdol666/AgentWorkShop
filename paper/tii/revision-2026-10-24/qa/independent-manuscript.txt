A Governed Supervisory Execution Layer for
LLM-Agent Plant Writes
Anonymous Authors
Abstract—Industrial large language model agents require a
traceable connection between task-level proposals, commanded
controls, and process observations. This article presents a gov-
erned supervisory execution framework that connects these stages
beneath a replaceable strategy layer. Semantic acquisition and
control nodes connect agent identity to line–product–recipe–run
context. Server-side channel profiles separate observation, model
training, bounded exploration, and operational recipe work;
capabilities and source-specific admission resolve execution au-
thority. Required approvals govern actuation, and graded receipts
distinguish transport acceptance from endpoint confirmation.
Optimization records, batch receipts, and recipe versions retain
the evidence appropriate to each lifecycle. Evaluation combines
protocol and process missions, admission ablation, controlled
confirmation challenges, nine regression executions totaling 782
checks, and a recipe-based injection-molding trajectory. Re-
moving soft-window admission allowed six otherwise refused
probes to execute, whereas removing readback left admission
unchanged. A publish-only receipt retained its unverified grade
despite successful transport acceptance. In one sequential recipe
experiment, four approved pressure steps from 45 to 65 bar
accompanied a weight change from 31.28 to 32.30 g with a fixed
6-s hold time, followed by retention of the accepted recipe. All
process experiments used repository simulators; approvals were
scripted. The results demonstrate inspectable execution evidence
on the exercised routes, linking task intent, authorization, control
history, observed quality, and retained production parameters.
Index Terms—Agent teams, human-in-the-loop, LLM agents,
supervisory control, write governance.
I. INTRODUCTION
L
ARGE language model (LLM) agents increasingly par-
ticipate in industrial operation: they generate operator-
reviewed OPC UA control sequences [1], invoke factory
skills over tool protocols [2], coordinate shop-floor tasks
[3], [4], and schedule a simulated robot-operated line [5].
These applications require a connection between reasoning
and production context: who may change a parameter, under
which product and recipe, what was commanded, and how the
process responded.
To bring injection-molded part weight into specification,
an agent may use recent measurements to diagnose insuffi-
cient packing and propose higher holding pressure. Execution
also requires a writable signal mapping, parameter limits,
authorized production scope, and approval. Its record must
distinguish the pressure request, endpoint confirmation, and
measured weight.
Three distinctions must survive this handoff: permission
to invoke a tool versus authority over a production object,
transport acceptance versus endpoint confirmation, and a
trial command versus a retained recipe. Reconstructing these
distinctions inside every agent application couples execution
policy to the selected reasoning engine and obscures how an
intervention relates to a running batch. We address the follow-
ing systems question: how can an industrial agent platform
retain a common execution and evidence contract when the
proposal strategy, equipment protocol, and production context
change?
We present a governed supervisory execution framework
connecting agent teams to semantic data-acquisition (DAQ)
and write-control (DCW) nodes. A deterministic execution
boundary sits beneath the replaceable strategy layer. The
strategy acquires evidence, proposes a change, and interprets
observations. The platform resolves authority, applies source-
specific limits and required approval, dispatches commands,
and grades confirmation. Line–product–recipe–run objects
supply production context. Separate task, intervention, and
batch records then connect the original intent to commanded
controls and observed outputs.
The contribution is a common execution-and-evidence con-
tract integrating production-scoped authority, graded confirma-
tion, and route-specific records:
1) a capability-based architecture that binds channel iden-
tity and semantic industrial nodes to line–product–
recipe–run context, separating information exchange
from execution authority;
2) route-specific execution mechanisms that join admission,
approval, graded confirmation, observation, and com-
pensation while preserving distinct node-intervention
and recipe-batch records; and
3) an implementation and evaluation of this contract across
replaceable strategies and protocol mappings, using ad-
mission ablation, confirmation drills, process missions,
repeated regression runs, and a recipe trajectory.
Sections III–IV explain the architecture and execution
mechanisms; Sections V–VI evaluate and interpret their
simulator-backed evidence.

B. Execution Contracts and Automation Practice
MCP standardizes tool discovery and calls with request-
scoped authorization [12]; ISA-88 and ISA-95 supply recipe
and operations concepts [13], [14]. The platform instantiates
a reduced production model and adapts run-to-run control dis-
cipline [15] to agent-originated interventions. Software-agent
constraints such as AgentSpec [16] and CaMeL’s capability-
based information-flow control [17] address tool behavior
and data flow. Here those concerns meet semantic industrial
quantities, active production context, and protocol-specific
confirmation.
Electronic-record governance provides a complementary ac-
countability perspective: FDA guidance discusses trustworthy
records and audit trails [18], while IEC 62443-3-3 specifies
industrial system-security requirements [19]. Command con-
firmation also has historical telecontrol precedents, including
DNP3 select-before-operate [20]. The framework brings actor
identity, production context, source-dependent admission, and
retained confirmation evidence into one supervisory workflow.
Control-oriented assurance addresses state evolution through
shielding, control-barrier functions, and Simplex architectures
[21]–[23]. Those approaches complement execution contracts
that resolve which industrial operation is authorized and what
evidence its execution produces. The remaining integration
problem is to preserve those distinctions across replaceable
strategies, heterogeneous protocols, and versioned production
context.
III. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
The platform connects task-level reasoning to production-
scoped execution through three component groups (Fig. 1).
Team and strategy services turn tasks and observations into
candidate operations using channels, members, shared mem-
ory, and replaceable engine adapters. Governance services
resolve the actor’s production-scoped authority, admission,
and required approval, then retain dispatch and confirma-
tion records. Industrial integration maps semantic DAQ/DCW
quantities to protocol I/O and returns timestamped obser-
vations. Shared line–product–recipe–run identifiers connect
these responsibilities. Registration services, a typed SDK,
and REST, MCP, agent-to-agent, CLI, and console interfaces
provide sanctioned extension points.
Three state objects have complementary roles. A task states
the intended outcome and responsible member. Production
objects identify the configured definition and active execution
context. Execution records state what was admitted, sent,
confirmed, and observed. An engine can therefore be replaced
without redefining a recipe or a driver’s evidence contract.
The project is distributed under Apache-2.0; repository links
are withheld for anonymous review.
A. Semantic Nodes and Production Context
DAQ nodes expose engineering quantities with units, pre-
cision, timestamps, and quality metadata. Their contracts
cover scalars, vector profiles, image frames, and mirrored
manufacturing-execution-system (MES) records. MES integra-
tion supports mirrored acquisition and direct endpoint map-
pings. DCW nodes describe writable engineering quantities
and their driver configuration. Drivers own serialization, trans-
port, and available readback; platform services own authority
and admission. A proposal can name holding pressure in bar
while its driver supplies the protocol representation.
Implemented protocol families include Modbus TCP, Mod-
bus RTU-over-TCP, OPC UA, MQTT, and HTTP/REST, with
MES REST mappings. Q1 exercises simulator writes over
Modbus TCP, OPC UA, MQTT, and HTTP; RTU-over-TCP
is acquisition-only. The admission ablation uses a separate
mock driver. DAQ acquisition uses per-node cadences and
a rotating sweep. Timestamped history supplies decisions,
exports, and response queries independently of the agent’s
reasoning duration.

B. Capabilities and Channel Profiles
A channel organizes a job and its collaborating members;
persisted capabilities authorize industrial operations. Node
bindings define readable or controlled node scope and exe-
cution mode, while recipe capabilities define an authorized
production scope. Channel profiles restrict which tools a job
may invoke; capabilities determine whether its member may
act on the selected production objects.
In the inspected implementation, observation channels per-
form acquisition, analysis, and knowledge work, while Auto
Modeling Lab (AML) training channels prepare datasets and
models. Both have read-only plant access. AML-optimization
channels invoke optimization_explore with control
and target-scope validation. Operational channels use autho-
rized recipe operations. Direct public dcw_control and
dcw_rollback are closed; sanctioned platform workflows
reach the internal governed node executor. Consequently, a
written task instruction or retrieved document cannot expand
the industrial tool surface.
Collaboration transfers diagnostic reports and evaluated
model references to members proposing bounded interven-
tions. Each recipient retains its own profile and capability
checks: information transfer does not transfer execution au-
thority.
C. Analysis, Models, and Candidate Proposals
Analysis and modeling supply evidence to the proposer. A
merged DAQ export aligns timestamped signals and produc-
tion context for industrial data diagnostics (IDD); diagnostic
reports describe the observed operating regime. Retrieval-
augmented generation (RAG) supplies relevant process knowl-
edge and prior reports for interpretation. These outputs inform
the strategy’s choice of controls, targets, and stopping criteria.
Write authority is resolved independently when the candidate
reaches execution.
AML provides a separate data-to-model path. Its dataset
builder selects line–product–recipe-scoped histories, cleans
samples by state and range, aligns them to a sampling grid,
and constructs windows within runs. Run-based partitions and
training-partition normalization produce a dataset manifest,
tensors, and a cleaning report. They identify the training
controls, targets, features, runs, and transformations. The asyn-
chronous production training route invokes Python, exports an
Open Neural Network Exchange (ONNX) artifact, evaluates it,
and applies gates before candidate registration. Registry stages
distinguish candidate, shadow, production, and retired models;
promotion carries its own approval workflow.
Model binding and eligibility checks govern model-assisted
tools, including declared uncertainty, out-of-distribution, and
physics information. Current lineage enforcement is primarily
line-scoped. Each production training job retains its exported
model, evaluation report, and gate decision as identifiable
artifacts. Bounded exploration also operates without a model.

The execution boundary resolves the actor and production
context, applies the route’s authorization, admission, pacing,
and approval checks, then retains driver receipts. Observation
and interpretation complete the supervisory loop; compensa-
tion retains its own outcome.

B. Human Approval and Recipe Control
Manual execution bindings create an approval request car-
rying actor, node, candidate value, applicable interval, expiry,
reasoning, and parameter bases. This structured object gives
the approver the proposed operation alongside the governing
context. The default timed wait expires after 180 s and refuses
execution; a hold mode waits for an explicit decision. One
pending request per member–node pair suppresses duplicates.
The decision note returns to the proposing member, complet-
ing the interaction between strategy and approver.
Approval is bound to an action and followed by execution
checks. The executor rechecks authorization and active limits,
thereby accounting for capability or product changes during
the wait. For recipe_apply, a changed definition requires
a new proposal; an unchanged approval dispatches a copied
parameter snapshot. The approved definition thus determines
the values passed to batch dispatch.

C. Graded Command Confirmation
Command evidence has two levels: the driver’s accep-
tance ACK and the batch verifier’s terminal verdict. The
driver assigns readback-verified, transport-ack,
or unverified. A matching post-command read supports
the first; a transport or MES application receipt supports the
second. The batch verifier assigns verified, unverified,
or failed. It accepts an existing matching readback or
performs up to three rereads, 2 s apart. A matching reread up-
grades the final ACK to readback-verified; exhausted
confirmation attempts produce an unverified verdict, and
command failure produces a failed verdict. MES acceptance
without a read mapping follows the unverified outcome. Write
history retains the initial ACK, while the batch result stores
the final ACK and terminal verdict.
Batch dispatch records each parameter’s result and summa-
rizes verified, unverified, and failed counts. This granularity
preserves a verified parameter even when another parameter’s
command fails. A readback-verified value describes the end-
point’s returned setting; target attainment is evaluated from
process observations. Section V-E tests the distinction with
a publish-only endpoint whose ok=true broker receipt is
transport acceptance and whose terminal confirmation remains
unverified.
D. Observation, Verdict, and Compensation
Bounded exploration couples an intervention with a target
summary. It captures the starting control before asynchronous
execution and averages 1-s buckets over a trailing 45-s target
window. A successful changed-value write opens the node
record. The exploration workflow waits 10–300 s (default 45 s)
and queries another trailing 45-s window. These are query
windows relative to their query times: approval can separate
the baseline from actuation, and a 10-s wait can leave about
35 s of pre-write observations in the later window. The receipt
and target statistics are returned separately for interpretation
of this single-factor probe. The exploration summary is saved
after this observation; refused or expired approval instead
produces a rejected summary without actuation or post-write
sampling.
The governed node lifecycle accepts attributed keep,
rollback, or uncertain verdicts with the caller’s inter-
pretation and queried window. Compensation requests a new
command under its applicable admission policy and retains its
own outcome, including partial completion. In the archived
experiments, a harness also compares recipe value, endpoint
readback, and an acquisition-store sample. This three-way
check measures agreement between representations sharing an
endpoint.
A record backstop supplies monitoring while an optimiza-
tion record remains open. After 120 s it polls every 30 s; three
cumulative samples outside the recipe monitoring window trig-
ger a system-attributed rollback toward the preceding value. Its
window has a monitoring role separate from the admission in-
terval and mission target. Available samples determine breach
counts; missing samples contribute no breach. A caller’s keep
verdict closes the record and its monitoring. When retained
rollback records reach the configured limit, further actuation
follows the approval policy.
Attribution joins actor, production context, receipt, verdict,
and recipe/run records. The write audit deduplicates the same

The process missions follow six stages. First, provision
the semantic nodes, their signal mappings and limits, and
a baseline recipe in the active production context. Second,
acquire the initial controls and target observations to establish
the starting state. Third, submit a bounded candidate under
the declared capability, binding, and approval policy. Fourth,

each parameter’s verification result. Fifth, wait the scenario’s

continue, compensate, or stop. A retained parameterization can
subsequently become a recipe version.

B. Q1: Protocol Closure and Admission Ablation
The suite acquired 55 samples over five protocol fami-
lies and issued governed writes over four; the RTU-over-
TCP line was acquisition-only. Archived upper-median write
latencies ranged from 19.3–47.8 ms, with observed maxima
20.1–248.9 ms. The four writable lines accepted legal writes
and rejected 24/24 out-of-window probes. A configuration-
only second preset added 9/9 rejected probes on three further
writable lines. These results exercise the same resolver against
different signal maps.
Table II isolates admission from confirmation in a mock-
driver API fixture. Each arm replayed six attack classes in
three deterministic repetitions (18 attacks per arm); separate
boundary, legal, and timed probes are excluded from that
denominator. Removing the soft-window interlock allowed six
soft-window probes to execute, while the hard-range probes
remained refused. Archived outcomes and journal records
link the refusals and executed commands to their configured
arms. Removing readback did not alter admission. Confirma-
tion can therefore check an execution outcome, but cannot
replace a pre-write gate. A frozen-measurement drill further
separated command and process evidence: the setpoint and
readback agreed, yet the monitoring quantity remained outside
its window and triggered backstop rollback after 130.2 s. A
manual-binding drill suspended execution until harness-issued
approval (461 ms mediation span).

VI. ENGINEERING INTERPRETATION
The evidence addresses the three distinctions motivating the
design. Profile tests exercise production-scoped authority; ad-
mission ablation isolates the pre-write interval from readback.
The publish-only challenge separates transport acceptance
from endpoint confirmation, and paired trajectories link inter-
ventions to observations and retained recipes. These results
support an inspectable execution contract on the exercised
routes.
An LLM, scripted policy, or deterministic controller sup-
plies a candidate through its authorized interface; the platform
applies the execution contract. Process reuse changes the
signal map, objective, and guards while retaining production
context and execution records. In Q6, observed acceptance of
approved trials precedes retention of the recipe version.
Human participation is represented through explicit au-
thority and attributed decisions. This supports inspection of
what an approver authorized and what evidence the proposer
later used. Automation research distinguishes such workflow
structure from operator comprehension, complacency, and al-
location of responsibility [24]–[26]. The server-side boundary
resolves execution authority independently of prompts and
retrieved documents [27], [28]. Deployment combines this
boundary with credential management, endpoint security, and
IT/OT separation [19]. The supervisory layer coexists with
independently engineered PLC interlocks and safety instru-
mented systems; IEC 61511 governs the latter’s process-safety
lifecycle [29].
Experimental scope. The process evidence uses accelerated
simulators, scripted approvals, and sequential trajectories. Q1–
Q4 cover the historical direct-node interface; current profile
and composition tests cover software behavior; Q6 covers one
recipe trajectory. No single current-version end-to-end study
jointly varies strategy, protocol, and production context, and
a complete current AML-exploration control–response trial is
not archived. Q6’s observed guards, snapshots, and cadence
settings are specified in Section V-F. These evidence units
establish the exercised mechanisms without estimating com-
parative optimization benefit, fault frequency, human perfor-
mance, hard real-time behavior, or physical transfer. Matched
initial states, repeated independent runs, and a fixed-policy
or SCADA comparator would support those further estimates.
Established process benchmarks such as Tennessee Eastman
[30] provide an additional setting for that evaluation.
VII. CONCLUSION
The proposed framework connects replaceable industrial
agent strategies to production-scoped authority, graded com-
mand evidence, and distinct node/recipe records. Admission
and confirmation experiments demonstrate the separation of
pre-write permission from post-write evidence. Controller and
process missions exercise strategy and configuration reuse,
while the injection-molding trajectory links approved trials to
an accepted recipe. Together, these simulator-backed results
support a common execution and evidence contract for in-
spectable industrial agent supervision.
