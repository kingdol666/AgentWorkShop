\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}
The evaluation follows the execution chain from interface reuse to process
evidence. Q1 tests protocol closure and the distinct roles of admission and
confirmation. Q2--Q3 replace the proposal strategy with agent and controller
workflows. Q4 pairs controls and outputs across four processes. Q5 tests
repeated deployment execution, timing, and confirmation semantics. Q6
follows a complete operational recipe trajectory from baseline observation
to a saved version. All process experiments use repository simulators.

\subsection{Evidence Units and Experimental Procedure}
\label{sec:testbed}
\label{sec:procedure}
Q1--Q4 use the seed-\resSeed{} integrated archive
\texttt{20260921184204-10lc}, its raw scenario records, and companion ablation
run \resAblationRun. The cast-film reference preset has an author-defined
objective $J$ combining thickness conformance, defects, energy, and
throughput. These historical missions use the direct-node interface.
Scripted policies occupy the proposal slot except for two live engine
tasks in Q2. Q3 additionally uses seeds 43--44. Q5 uses nine
deployment-suite executions from October 2026; Q6 uses the October 18 recipe
archive. The archives identify each tested interface and its own evidence
unit. Separately, 65 current channel/profile and composition tests pass in
temporary storage with mock I/O, covering approval outcomes, starting-control
capture, and approved-recipe snapshot consistency. Q6 exercises the recipe
route; these evidence sets are not pooled as one interface experiment.

The process missions follow six stages. First, provision the semantic nodes,
their signal mappings and limits, and a baseline recipe in the active
production context. Second, acquire the initial controls and target
observations to establish the starting state. Third, submit a bounded
candidate under the declared capability, binding, and approval policy.
Fourth, retain the dispatch receipt and available readback, preserving each
parameter's verification result. Fifth, wait the scenario's declared
response interval and query the relevant DAQ quantities. Sixth, evaluate
the observed target and guards, then continue, compensate, or stop. A
retained parameterization can subsequently become a recipe version.

This ordering produces three complementary result objects. The requested
control sequence $u$ records the intervention, the endpoint confirmation
records command evidence, and the target sequence $y$ records process
observations. The task verdict joins them under the declared acceptance
criteria. Figures~\ref{fig:missions} and~\ref{fig:injection} display $u$ and
$y$ together so that a target change can be inspected alongside the
corresponding control history; these plots validate evidence linkage and
route behavior. An evidence unit is a run or a sequential
trajectory; several writes within it are sequential observations rather
than independent trials.

The integrated suite has 20 phases spanning commissioning, protocol I/O,
governance, agent missions, controller reuse, and concurrent processes. The deployment
pipeline uses S0 prechecks, S1 APIs, S2 acquisition/MES, S3 negative paths,
S4 closed-loop execution, S5 fine-tuning, S6 diagnostics, and S7 report
generation. Checks are author-defined regression assertions. Reported
quantities are rejected probes, per-parameter confirmation grades,
recipe/readback/mirror deviations, process-target margins, and timestamp
spans. At approximately six writes per protocol line, latency is reported
as a median and observed maximum rather than a tail percentile.

\subsection{Q1: Protocol Closure and Admission Ablation}
\label{sec:protocol}
The suite acquired \resDaqSamples{} samples over five protocol families and
issued governed writes over four; the RTU-over-TCP line was acquisition-only.
Median write latencies ranged from \resPfiftyRange{}, with observed maxima
\resPninetyfiveRange{}. The four writable lines accepted legal writes and
rejected 24/24 out-of-window probes. A configuration-only second preset
added 9/9 rejected probes on three further writable lines. These results
exercise the same resolver against different signal maps.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} isolates admission from confirmation in a
mock-driver API fixture. Each arm replayed six attack classes in three
deterministic repetitions (18 attacks per arm); separate boundary, legal,
and timed probes are excluded from that denominator. Removing the
soft-window interlock allowed six soft-window probes to execute, while
the hard-range probes remained refused. Archived outcomes and journal
records link the refusals and executed commands to their configured arms.
Removing readback did not alter admission. Confirmation can therefore
check an execution outcome, but cannot replace a pre-write gate. A frozen-measurement drill further
separated command and process evidence: the setpoint and readback agreed,
yet the monitoring quantity remained outside its window and triggered
backstop rollback after \resBackstop{}. A manual-binding drill suspended
execution until harness-issued approval (\resHitlLatency{} mediation span).

\subsection{Q2--Q3: Strategy Replacement and Controller Handling}
\label{sec:agentteam}
The archived team workflow linked a parent task, a bound worker, a command,
a verdict, and its journal. On the single-node cast-film mission, the policy
changed the heater from \resMissionFrom{} to
\resMissionTo\,$^{\circ}$C; the observation settled at
\resMissionPv\,$^{\circ}$C within the declared target. The task reached
\texttt{COMPLETED}. The BOPET mission expanded this workflow to nine devices
and 49 signals, with three manipulated nodes
(Section~\ref{sec:scenarios}).

Two live engine tasks (omp adapter, glm-5.3-flash) occupied the same proposal
slot on a scratch line. The archive records a bounded write, a goal-driven
descent to \resGoalFinal\,$\mu$m for a $52.0\pm0.8$\,$\mu$m target, and a
recipe save. The original contemporaneous omp logs and checked run outcomes
trace proposal, governed action, observations, and retained recipe.
The reported \resGoalFinal\,$\mu$m is the run-summary terminal value;
the log's 52.02\,$\mu$m window mean is a different aggregation.

\label{sec:controller}
A deterministic controller used the dimensionless cast-film objective
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J={}&55J_{\mathrm{thick}}+25J_{\mathrm{quality}}\\
&+8J_{\mathrm{energy}}+7J_{\mathrm{throughput}},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
whose components score thickness conformance, product quality, and
control-based energy and throughput proxies. The latter two terms use
screw speed and line speed, rather than independently metered energy and
production output. The functions in~\eqref{eq:objective}, constraints, grid,
and replay settings are specified in the accompanying \path{REPRODUCIBILITY.md}.
It made two governed writes
per seed (42--44), without rejection. It raised $J$ from approximately
\resJzero{} to $J/J^*=\resControllerMin$--\resControllerMax{}, with
$J^*=\resJstar$ from an admissible setpoint grid. This finite restricted grid
supplies a reference normalization, not a global continuous optimum.
The result links the controller's native objective to admissible commands
and endpoint evidence, exercising a conventional strategy through the
same execution services.

\subsection{Q4: Paired Controls and Outputs Across Processes}
\label{sec:scenarios}
The concurrent campaign provides one seed-42 trajectory per process.
Figure~\ref{fig:missions} pairs archived manipulated controls $u$ with the
corresponding response $y$ rather than showing response alone. All four
terminal attainment checks passed; two lay near a target boundary.

Injection molding used two changes: hold pressure
$45\rightarrow58.2$\,bar and hold time $6\rightarrow10.64$\,s. Part weight
rose from \resInjStart{} to \resInjFinal{}\,g for a
$32.5\pm0.35$\,g window, only $0.02$\,g above its lower limit.
BOPET changed casting speed $32\rightarrow33.8$\,m/min, fast-roll speed
$118\rightarrow120.3$\,m/min, and rail width $3000\rightarrow3035$\,mm.
Thickness fell from \resBiaxStart{} to \resBiaxFinal{}\,$\mu$m for a
$25.0\pm0.7$\,$\mu$m target, $0.04$\,$\mu$m inside the upper limit.
Its three changes each have a recorded keep verdict and journal identifier.

Wastewater treatment required 15 writes across several nodes. Dissolved
oxygen rose from \resWwtpStart{} to \resWwtpFinal{}\,mg/L, within
$3.4\pm0.6$\,mg/L. Some admissible blower proposals reduced compliance
margin and were reverted. Terminal guards remained within their declared
limits, although pH moved toward its lower bound (6.59 to 6.38; limit 6.0).
Dissolved oxygen, total phosphorus, and pH are different quantities and are
not concatenated into one output trajectory.

Annealing combined furnace-temperature changes with line-speed increases
from 140 to 164\,m/min. The recorded hardness trajectory moved from
\resAnnealStart{} to 99.84\,HV; the separate terminal measurement was
\resAnnealFinal{}\,HV, within $95\pm6$\,HV. A 176\,m/min attempt produced
101.3\,HV and was reverted. The first temperature step has no optimization
record identifier in the archive, which bounds record-level traceability.
Together, the four missions exercise configuration-based process reuse and
retain the distinction between a candidate command, a reverted candidate,
and a terminal observation.

\resChannelsLive{} channels, \resDaqOnline{} online DAQ nodes,

The seven-leg fault suite tested the interpretation of receipts. A mock
acceptance was promoted by reread; a broker outage produced one failed
parameter while its healthy sibling verified; and a publish-only endpoint
returned a broker receipt yet remained unverified after three attempts.
Two parameters were promoted from transport acknowledgment to readback
verification. Manual-to-automatic mode changes required confirmation, and a
manual-mode recipe release queued approval before six parameters verified.
API and documentation checks completed the suite. MES application-receipt
semantics are covered by route tests, whereas the campaign used MES only in
read mode. The suite therefore checks that receipt grades follow the available
endpoint evidence under each deliberately injected condition.

\subsection{Q6: Recent Recipe-Based Injection Trajectory}
\label{sec:recipeexperiment}
The October 18 recipe archive (R6 in the accompanying reproducibility notes)
pairs recipe-path controls, receipts, and process observations. The replay
entry is \path{scripts/e2e-injection-recipe-closedloop.mjs}.
The setup used OPC UA setpoints and HTTP quality acquisition against the
injection simulator. A live omp/deepseek-flash worker separately diagnosed
insufficient packing. The archived intervention sequence and approvals
were scripted, allowing the candidate, receipt, wait, observation, and
recipe-update sequence to be inspected explicitly.

Starting from 45\,bar and a fixed 6-s hold time, the script proposed
5-bar pressure increments. Each step called \texttt{recipe\_trial}, answered
the pending approval card, and retained the batch receipt. After 65\,s,
it queried platform control-node values and DAQ outputs. It stopped when
weight was in $[32.15,32.85]$\,g, flash was at most 0.4\%, and sink mark was
at most 1.5\%, or after at most six pressure proposals. The replay also
defines overshoot compensation and a hold-time fallback. The archived
successful run used four pressure proposals and no hold-time change.
The harness set \texttt{AW\_TRIAL\_INTERVAL\_MS=0}, disabling the per-line
trial cadence, and issued \texttt{emergency=true} trials to bypass the
separate recipe-operation interval. Approval, admission, and command
confirmation remained active. This configuration tests the execution chain
with experimental pacing.
An earlier archived attempt exercised the default 300-s cadence: it
refused a premature trial and the target was not attained in that attempt.

Figure~\ref{fig:injection} shows the complete baseline-to-terminal sequence.
Pressure advanced $45\rightarrow50\rightarrow55\rightarrow60\rightarrow65$
\,bar; weight increased
$31.28\rightarrow31.49\rightarrow31.74\rightarrow32.05\rightarrow32.30$\,g,
and sink mark decreased from 1.977\% to 1.277\%. Final flash was 0.019\%.
Each trial receipt reported one verified parameter out of one.
The displayed controls are platform-node snapshots, while the receipt
supplies the archived verification grade. After the final check,
\texttt{recipe\_update} saved version 2 with 65\,bar/6\,s. The recipe log
contains four trials and the update, while the optimization journal is empty,
as expected for this lifecycle.

The paired plot explains why the script continued and where it stopped.
At 60\,bar, the 32.05-g observation remained below the 32.15-g lower
acceptance limit, so the next 5-bar candidate was required by the declared
rule. At 65\,bar, the 32.30-g observation entered the weight band, and the
1.277\% sink-mark and 0.019\% flash observations satisfied their respective
limits. Holding time remained 6\,s throughout, making pressure the sole
changed control in this trace. The final recipe update records the accepted
65\,bar/6\,s parameterization; it is distinct from both the four trial
receipts and the observed quality values. The later update rationale contains
different quality figures without a linked raw sample; the plot uses the
archived final snapshot, not those rationale figures.

The quality assertion allowed a missing melt-temperature value, and the
freshness check used the newest timestamp among available sensors. The
reported pass therefore covers the three observed quality quantities;
temperature and individual-sensor freshness remain outside that assertion.
The duplicate archive adds no repeat.
A separate later check started at 65\,bar,
32.29\,g, and 1.385\% sink mark; one 70-bar trial ended at 32.55\,g and
0.976\%. It is a new initial state and is not appended to the plotted trace.
Q6 connects baseline evidence, approved control changes, observed quality,
and the retained recipe through one inspectable operational loop.
